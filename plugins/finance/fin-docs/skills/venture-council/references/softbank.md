# SoftBank — Masayoshi Son / Vision Fund

**Identity:** Not a VC firm — a conviction machine with a 300-year corporate plan.
Mission unchanged since founding: "Information Revolution — Happiness for everyone."
Declared goal (2025): "Becoming the World's No. 1 ASI Platform Provider" — "I am going
all in—staking everything on the world of ASI." Arm + OpenAI are "the two essential
engines"; Stargate (~$500B US AI infrastructure JV), Ampere, ABB robotics as "Physical
AI." Vision Fund checks historically $100M–$1B for 20–40% ownership; capital itself is
the weapon — fund the category king, price rivals out.

## Thesis

- Everything must sit on the road to ASI: AI infrastructure, agents, Physical
  AI/robotics, platforms with increasing-returns economics. Son's math: ASI leaders
  capture ≥5% of global GDP at ~50% margins. Off-thesis pitches fight the entire
  corporate narrative.
- **Cluster of No. 1s:** the portfolio is a loosely coupled cluster of category-#1
  companies. There is no seat for a strong #2.
- **Patient capital, planetary TAM:** 7–15 year horizon ("I waited 10 years on Jack Ma
  to get profitable"); the fund buys out impatient shareholders. A market that isn't
  global-powerhouse scale can't absorb the check.
- Serial-pivot identity: software → Yahoo → mobile → Alibaba → Vision Funds → Arm →
  ASI. Criticism of each pivot is answered with NAV growth.

## Founder psychology

- **He reads eyes, not decks.** On Jack Ma ($20M, no plan, zero revenue): "His eyes
  were very strong. Shiny eyes. I could tell from the way he talked that he had
  charisma and leadership." Met every founder in person before any Vision Fund deal.
- **Speed as doctrine.** Yahoo: six minutes → $2M. His best returns came from his
  fastest decisions; he expects speed reciprocity — slow-walking a term sheet reads as
  weak conviction.
- **Crazy > smart.** To Neumann: "Who wins in a fight—the smart guy or the crazy guy?…
  You are correct. But you are not crazy enough." He tests whether ambition expands
  when capital does.
- **The post-WeWork correction is real and documented.** "My investment judgment was
  poor in many ways, and I am reflecting deeply on that" (2019); he was "foolish" on
  WeWork (2020). The modern Son pairs craziness-in-vision with a governance sniff:
  discipline in cash, control, and founder conduct. Vision-only theater no longer
  clears the bar.
- **Existential stakes:** "I think I was born to realize ASI. I am super serious about
  it." Founders whose company is a *mission organ*, not a financial instrument, mirror
  him.

## Pitch evaluation lens

- **Founder first, company second** — the screen is a charisma/conviction read; the
  deck is supporting evidence.
- **Altitude first:** does this ride the information-revolution → ASI wave? Opening
  with features or unit economics loses him; opening with a 10-year world-state and a
  claim to global #1 holds him.
- **The capital-absorption test:** "Sometimes you need hundreds of millions upon
  billions" (VF partner). The founder needs a credible, aggressive answer to *"what
  would you do with 10x the money you're asking for?"* If extra capital doesn't buy
  dominance faster, the model doesn't fit.
- **Winner-take-all with a stick:** SoftBank historically threatened to fund the top
  competitor of startups that refused investment. Geography rule: won't fund your
  direct competitor in your market; will in adjacent ones — and can "take you global
  overnight."
- **Post-2022 discipline overlay:** record Vision Fund losses → "defense mode" → 2023
  "offense" pivot for AI. The checkbook is open again, but the bar is AI-thesis fit
  plus unit-economics honesty, not growth alone.

## Red flags

- **"Not crazy enough":** incremental ambition, lifestyle-business framing, niche
  market, plans to be profitably #3.
- Refusing capital / capping the round — signals small ambition; historically invited
  the fund-your-competitor countermove.
- No credible use for 10x money.
- Post-WeWork governance triggers: founder self-dealing, super-voting excess, blurred
  personal/company lines, cult-of-founder without operating discipline — exactly what
  Son says he "turned a blind eye" to and won't again.
- Cash-torching unit economics with no path (the WeWork lesson).
- Anything off the ASI road; dismissing AI reads as "goldfish" behavior (his word).

## First 60 seconds — how the room behaves

1. Reads the founder, not the deck — decision forms in minutes; expect slides to be
   interrupted to probe the person: why you, why is this your life's work.
2. Altitude check: 10-year world-state, global #1 claim, information-revolution fit.
3. The escalation move: offers MORE money than asked and watches whether ambition
   expands to absorb it. Hesitation = small thinking.
4. The crazy test — paired with the post-WeWork governance sniff: crazy vision, sober
   cap table.
5. Expects the founder to decide as fast as he does.

**THE question this firm asks first:** "How big is this really — and what would you do
if I gave you ten times what you're asking for?"
