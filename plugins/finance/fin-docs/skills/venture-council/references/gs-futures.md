# GS Futures — Future of Construction Fund

**Identity note:** Users may say "GS Construction Futures Fund." The actual entity is the
**Future of Construction Fund** — a dedicated ~$70M early-stage vehicle (Seed–Series B)
inside **GS Futures**, the San Mateo CVC arm of Korea's **GS Group**, with **GS E&C**
(GS Engineering & Construction, a top global EPC and Korea's #1 high-rise multifamily
builder) as anchor LP. Firm founder/MP: **Tae Huh**. Construction Fund MP: **Aaron
Toppston** (ex-Navitas Capital VC; ex-Walsh Group P3 infrastructure MD; started in
construction insurance at Aon). This is NOT a Goldman Sachs fund.

## Thesis

- Seed–Series B in construction digitization, automation, robotics, and **Physical AI
  applied to jobsite execution and real-asset operations**. Firm-wide pillars: Physical
  AI & Robotics · Built Environment & Construction · Energy & Industrial Transition ·
  Digital Platforms & Infrastructure.
- Core mechanic — "built for deployment": the parent conglomerate is the product. GS
  E&C's global project footprint = pilot sites, reference customer, and Korea/SE Asia
  market entry. A pitch that doesn't say what it would DO with an EPC parent is leaving
  their main value-add on the table.
- Named investment theme: "the shift from software as a **system of record** to software
  as a **system of execution**" (Toppston, 2026). Another Procore-adjacent record-keeper
  loses; a tool that changes how work physically happens wins.
- Portfolio pattern (confirmed): jobsite-execution software (Clearstory change orders,
  SmartPM schedule analytics, Primepoint drawing intelligence, CivCheck permitting),
  site robotics for repetitive/precise work (August Robotics layout, Viabot, Skydio
  drones), building intelligence (Butlr sensing), industrial/energy ops AI (Phaidra,
  Pila). Commercial-construction skew, strong data-center tailwind.

## What this strategic CVC needs that a financial VC doesn't

- **Deployability is the thesis.** "Most excited by companies that can move from pilot
  to production." Expect: *could this run on a GS E&C project next quarter?* A pitch
  with no field story is a research project.
- **Exact buyer mapping.** They wrote it themselves: the buying decision aligns to "a
  return on investment or risk reduction to the project owner, general contractor, or
  another party." Founders must name who pays and whose risk shrinks — owner vs GC vs
  sub are different companies with different incentives.
- **Contract-mechanics fluency.** Their own explainers cover GMP contracts, risk
  transfer down the sub chain, general conditions ≈10% of project value, insurance
  costs, the litigious design→construction gap. Ignoring where risk and cost actually
  sit reads as naive tourism.
- **Strategic fit articulated by the founder**, not assumed by the fund: field
  validation plan, Asia expansion angle, integration with EPC workflows.

## Founder psychology

- Prize the "rare combination of product knowledge and industry experience" — their
  favorite teams pair a serious technologist with a construction operator.
- Respect founders who "intentionally chose a problem that is difficult" and hold a
  technical moat "not easily replicated with off-the-shelf models."
- Field validation is the credibility currency: "In construction, where skepticism
  toward new tools is high, that level of early validation matters."
- They publish to de-mystify the industry — they will test whether the founder actually
  understands stakeholders, contracting, and value-chain incentives. (Nuance for this
  skill: domain fluency must show up in *answers to their questions*; the opening pitch
  still has to be legible to the non-construction partners in the room.)

## Red flags (from their own writing)

- Treating construction as monolithic — "Construction is not a monolithic sector."
  Not specifying commercial vs civil vs residential value chain fails the first filter.
- Can't name the buyer or whose risk is reduced.
- Shallow AI wrappers: "OCR-based tools extract text but miss meaning... many existing
  solutions stop at search."
- No field proof: undifferentiated "solutions that sound the same" with zero pilot
  evidence = noise.
- Margin-polish-only tools: they want teams that "rethink how work should happen
  entirely," not incremental workflow improvement on a system of record.
- Pilots that never convert — pilot→production economics unexplained.

## First 60 seconds — how the room behaves

1. Classify: which construction vertical, which workflow, who's the buyer. A founder
   who can't place themselves in the value chain loses the room immediately.
2. Strategic-fit scan: would GS E&C deploy this? Which jobsite, which geography?
3. Field-proof probe: what did pilots find, and did any convert to paid production?
4. Moat test: defensible beyond an off-the-shelf model wrapper?
5. Pattern-match: system of execution (fund it) or another system of record (pass)?

**THE question this firm asks first:** "Walk me from pilot to production — on whose
jobsite, who signs the PO, and what risk did you take off the project?"
