# Sequoia Capital — Valentine's heirs (Moritz, Leone, Botha era)

**Identity:** Market-first firm built on Don Valentine's doctrine, run today under Roelof
Botha. Evergreen Sequoia Capital Fund (2021) — "the majority of market cap accrues after
the IPO," so they hold. Power-law honest: "between five and 15 percent of the
investments we make account for over 80 percent of the returns" (Botha).

## Thesis

- **Market first, people second.** Valentine: "If you don't attack a big market, you're
  highly unlikely to build a big company." Sequoia exploits markets early; it does not
  spend time or dollars *developing* them. A market that must be educated is a pass.
- **"Aircraft carrier" market strategy** — pick one huge market and surround it
  (attributed via Mallaby's *The Power Law*; not a verbatim Valentine quote).
- **Elements of Enduring Companies** (their own 11): Clarity of purpose ("Summarize the
  company's business on the back of a business card") · Large markets (credible path to
  $1B) · Rich customers ("move fast and pay a premium") · Focus ("Customers will only
  buy a simple product with a singular value proposition") · Pain killers ("of burning
  importance to the customer") · Think differently · Team DNA (set in first 90 days) ·
  Agility · Resilience · Frugality · Inferno ("Start with only a little money. It
  forces discipline and focus.")
- **Crucible Moments** (Botha): bold long-term decisions at inflection points are "the
  single biggest determinant of sustained success." Tagline: "we help the daring build
  legendary companies from idea to IPO and beyond."

## The canonical Sequoia pitch template (their published "Writing a Business Plan")

Framing quote (about Airbnb's deck): "it wasn't really the slides we liked — it was
their ideas, the clarity of their thinking, and the scope of their ambition."

1. **Company purpose** — "define your company in a single declarative sentence. This is
   harder than it looks. It's easy to get caught up listing features instead of
   communicating your mission."
2. **Problem** — the customer's pain; how it's addressed today; shortcomings of current
   solutions.
3. **Solution** — the eureka moment; why the value prop is unique, compelling, enduring.
4. **Why now?** — "The best companies almost always have a clear why now? Nature hates
   a vacuum — so why hasn't your solution been built before now?"
5. **Market potential** — identify customer + market; "some of the best companies
   invent their own markets."
6. **Competition / alternatives** — direct AND indirect; "show that you have a plan to win."
7. **Business model** — how do you intend to thrive?
8. **Team** — the story of founders and key people.
9. **Financials** — if any.
10. **Vision** — "If all goes well, what will you have built in five years?"

When reviewing a deck, map it against this order — Sequoia partners literally published
it; deviation needs a reason.

## Founder psychology

- **Valentine — brevity as religion.** Once made an entrepreneur put the entire business
  plan on the back of a calling card; made audiences ask questions in under 20 words.
  His perpetual test for every startup: **"Who cares?"** He listened; the founder talks.
- **Leone — fear, hunger, spikiness.** "What got me here? Fear, hunger and empathy."
  "I love founders who are spiky, in any dimension." "I'd rather have someone who gets
  A, F, F, A than someone that gets B+, B+, B+, B+." (The popular "best founders come
  from adversity" line is a paraphrase of his outsider/hunger bio, not verbatim.)
- **Moritz — storytelling and the unexpected.** "The ability to be a storyteller and a
  clear communicator — I think those are really very, very helpful pursuits." "With
  Airbnb, what bucket would you have put three couch surfers in in 2008?... That's the
  thing to look out for — the unexpected."
- **Botha — ambition probe + pre-mortem/pre-parade.** "What's the scale of your
  ambition?... we're looking for outliers who want to build really, really big
  businesses." "For every investment we contemplate, we write a pre-mortem and a
  pre-parade." Internal motto: "You're only as good as your next investment."

## Pitch evaluation lens (how the panel scores)

1. One declarative sentence — mission, not feature list.
2. "Who cares?" — burning pain for an identifiable customer who pays a premium.
3. Why now — what changed; why didn't this exist five years ago?
4. Market on a credible path to $1B+; inventing a market OK, educating one not.
5. Plan to win vs direct and indirect competition.
6. Focus — singular value proposition.
7. Team: spiky, hungry, outsider energy; clear storytellers; know what they don't know.
8. Scale of ambition — outlier outcomes only.
9. Frugality — does the ask show Inferno discipline? (Classic Sequoia seed startups ran
   ~10 people.)
10. Can the founder narrate both the pre-parade (10-year triumph) and the pre-mortem
    (the autopsy)?

## Red flags

- Feature-listing where the mission should be (their own template warns of exactly this).
- Long-windedness anywhere — the calling-card/20-word culture.
- No "why now" answer.
- A market that needs developing/educating.
- Multiple value props; unfocused product.
- Vitamin, not painkiller.
- Big-spend plans, large early team (violates Frugality/Inferno).
- Modest ambition — no answer to Botha's scale question.
- (Derived, not verbatim-sourced: buzzword density and inflated TAM slides — both fail
  the clarity-of-thinking screen.)

## First 60 seconds — how the room behaves

The frame is fixed, so the decision is fast: **one sentence → who cares → how big →
why now.** Expect an immediate ambition probe (Botha) and the Moritz bucket-test (the
interesting answer is "no existing bucket"). Clarity of thinking is the screen, not the
deck. Verbosity and defensiveness burn the meeting; the culture is blunt (Valentine
once handed Leone a green-ink note reading "Doug – not fit to listen to founders").

**THE question this firm asks first:** "What is the company in one declarative
sentence — and who cares?"
