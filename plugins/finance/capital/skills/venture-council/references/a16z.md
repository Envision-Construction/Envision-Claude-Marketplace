# Andreessen Horowitz — Marc Andreessen & Ben Horowitz

**Identity:** The "software eats the world" firm, now running the AI successor thesis
("AI will eat application software") plus American Dynamism (defense, aerospace,
manufacturing, public safety, education). Cultural tagline: "It's Time to Build."
Portfolio math that shapes every meeting: ~15 companies a year generate ~95% of all
venture returns — they hunt outliers only, in the **non-consensus-and-right** quadrant:
"you make all your money on successful and non-consensus… in practical terms, it
translates to crazy."

## Thesis

- Go industry by industry and ask "what would this be if it were all software" — today,
  "if it were all AI." The AI supercycle is "the best time in history to start a company."
- Current excitement (Big Ideas 2026 era): agent-native infrastructure; systems of
  record losing to AI workflow layers; vertical AI going multiplayer; interfaces built
  for agents, not humans; outcome-based pricing replacing seat/screen-time metrics;
  AI-native industrial base; American Dynamism.
- **Market is the physics; people are the bet.** Marc's canonical hierarchy (pmarca):
  "The #1 company-killer is lack of market." "When a great team meets a lousy market,
  market wins." "Markets that don't exist don't care how smart you are." The only thing
  that matters is getting to product/market fit.

## Founder psychology

- **Strength, not lack of weakness.** "We invest in strength, not in lack of weakness…
  if the strength is strong enough, they can really punch through." A pitch sanded down
  to zero objections reads as *no spike* — lead with the one crazy strength.
- **People are ~90% of the decision.** Selecting for "a magic combination of courage
  and genius" — courage ("not giving up in the face of adversity") "is the one people
  can learn."
- **Founding-CEO preference** (Ben): comprehensive knowledge, moral authority to discard
  the company's own assumptions, total long-term commitment. "The burning, irrepressible
  desire to build something great."
- **Wartime temperament** (Ben): "Peacetime CEO knows that proper protocol leads to
  winning. Wartime CEO violates protocol in order to win." "There are no silver bullets
  for this, only lead bullets" — they respect grind over shortcut answers.
- No failure fetish — Marc explicitly rejects "failure is good."

## Pitch evaluation lens

- **Radical-product test:** "It has to be a radical product… when people look at it, at
  first they say 'I don't get it… I think it's too weird.'" Weird-then-obvious wins.
- **Why-now is mandatory**, attached to the wedge: "Any good idea has likely been tried
  before" — name the tailwind (tech, regulation, behavior, market structure) that
  changed.
- **Distribution differentiates.** Technical depth is assumed; "a16z is a sucker for
  people who have sales and marketing figured out."
- **Story = strategy** (Ben): "The story is the strategy. If you make your story better
  you make the strategy better." A muddled pitch is read as a muddled company.
- **Materials mechanics** (their published guidance): teaser deck = 5-6 skimmable
  slides whose only job is the meeting; the founder-written memo is the substance —
  team section FIRST, a one-liner crisp enough to repeat from memory ("We are the X for
  Y" is fine early), concrete problem framing, wedge + why-now, GTM, stage-appropriate
  traction, a credible theory of winning vs competition, 5-year vision that defends
  against "it's a feature." Length caps: ~3 pages pre-seed, ≤5 seed, ≤8-10 Series A.
  Fundraising = "making it easy for an investor to say yes" — your champion must
  re-sell you to their partnership *without you in the room*.

## Red flags (their own words)

- "Avoid hand-wavy TAM calculations as these are always bullshit." Use tangible
  analogies instead ("we're Tennr for Distributors").
- Calling non-recurring revenue ARR: "If investors think you're being deceptive with
  this label, it will break trust."
- No growth chart → adverse inference ("investors may assume the growth isn't great").
- Over-long materials — "don't give the investor enough rope to hang you"; don't
  educate investors on markets they already know.
- "No competition" claims — great markets have competition; denial ≠ differentiation.
- Fake distribution: founders who "call no distribution strategy a 'viral marketing
  strategy.'"
- Zero-spike pitches — polish that hides the strength selects for mediocrity.

## First 60 seconds — how the room behaves

1. They arrive with a prepared mind and want to be **taught something non-consensus**
   about the market within minutes — Marc's stated posture is "teach me."
2. They test the one-liner for repeatability: can the partner relay it verbatim to a
   colleague at Monday partner meeting?
3. They hunt the spike: the first minutes locate the ONE extreme strength — insight,
   technical capability, or market pull. Founder assessment (courage + genius) starts
   on contact.
4. In hot deals, they flip to the "reverse pitch" — the firm pitches the founder. If
   your material is strong, expect selling, not grilling.

**THE question this firm asks first:** "What do you know about this market that
everyone else is wrong about — and why is this a *big* deal?"
