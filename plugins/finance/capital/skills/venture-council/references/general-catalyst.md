# General Catalyst — Hemant Taneja & partners

**Identity:** ~$43B AUM "global investment and transformation company" that explicitly
set out to "transcend venture capital." Three core strategies: Seed, Creation
(build-and-transform: AI-native rollups, company hatches like Hippocratic AI), and the
Customer Value Fund — plus HATCo (bought Summa Health to build "the first AI-native
community hospital"), Percepta (in-house AI engineering), and GC Institute (policy: DC,
Brussels, Delhi). CEO: Hemant Taneja (author of *Unscaled*, *Intended Consequences*).

## Thesis

- **Responsible Innovation is the core mission, not a screen.** "Intentionally invest
  in growth and good, to enable positive, powerful change that endures." Venture-native
  *inputs* (founder mindset, mechanisms, business-model choices) — not ESG checkbox
  *outputs*. Every deal gets a formal responsible-innovation review.
- **Health Assurance:** proactive, tech-forward, value-based care — "help people stay
  well, bend the cost curve." Lineage: Livongo (~25% at the $18.5B Teladoc sale) →
  20+ health-system partner network → HATCo as a permanent-capital, decades-horizon
  vehicle. They bought a hospital system so founders have a proving ground. Point
  solutions are the named failure mode.
- **Applied AI:** AI captures **labor budgets, not software budgets** ("going after the
  workforce spend... the opportunity is so large"). Default outcome is that AI value
  accrues to incumbents — founders win by picking one industry and becoming its
  transformative force. Abundance framing (a tutor/clinician for everyone) over
  extraction.
- **Defense/resilience:** Anduril seed, Helsing, Applied Intuition — but "not investing
  in 'day one' offensive systems," and "not hundreds of defense companies but the dozen
  that can deliver impact at scale."
- **Long duration:** from "exit as the sole endgame" to enduring, compounding
  enterprises that also create liquidity.

## Founder psychology

- **Missionary over mercenary**: "That missionary approach of building companies is
  incredibly important in these times." Values as "guiding light" at peak ambiguity.
- **Enduring long-game ambition** "to play the long game around these transformations."
- **Contrarian conviction backed by insight** — the Livongo archetype: everybody
  passed; GC championed it for years pre-consensus. "The best seed conversations feel
  like the unveiling of a hidden truth."
- **Bet the person**: "we first and foremost invest in founders... the best founders
  might not always succeed, we believe they will not fail."
- **Purpose over ego**: "from a 'cult of personality' to a 'cult of purpose.'"

## Pitch evaluation lens

- **Systems-level transformation over point solutions.** The pitch should read as an
  industry changing, not a feature shipping.
- **Radical collaboration with incumbents**: who inside the legacy industry are you
  building *with*? Pure-disruption rhetoric ("we kill the hospitals/banks/GCs") misfits.
- **Business model as destiny**: they test whether the *revenue model itself* aligns
  with the societal outcome ("Healthcare became unaffordable, advertising led to
  polarization" — wrong models, not wrong tech).
- **Regulatory/policy fluency**: "self-regulation is better than regulation" — founders
  who pre-empt regulators score points.
- **The stakeholder question is coming**: "always asking: 'who wins and who loses?'"
  One sentence pre-answering second-order effects reads as intentionality.
- **AI-native economics**: labor-budget capture with proprietary-data advantage, not a
  thin wrapper competing for software budgets.

## Red flags

- "Growth at any cost... fueled by hubris" — named sin, alongside SPAC mania, paying
  ahead, poor governance.
- Ignoring unintended consequences / second- and third-order effects (social media's
  15-year arc is their canonical cautionary tale).
- Adversarial posture toward incumbents; disruption for its own sake.
- Profit-only framing: "'profit-only' capitalism has run its course."
- Founder cult of personality; checkbox virtue (reverse-engineered ESG benchmarks).
- Thin-wrapper SaaS / fragmented point solutions (structurally disfavored — only ~$4B
  of $43B AUM is classic SaaS).

## First 60 seconds — how the room behaves

(No literal "why this/why now/why you" formula in GC sources; the GC-native pattern is
*hidden truth → industry transformation → intentional mechanism*.)

1. Listens for the **non-consensus insight** about how an industry must change —
   thesis first, product second.
2. Sizes the market as **industry transformation** (labor-budget TAM if AI-native),
   not a software category.
3. Checks that the **business model produces the mission** — alignment IS the moat.
4. Scans for **incumbent partners** and regulatory posture in the first minute.
5. Waits to hear "who wins and who loses" answered unprompted.
6. Reads for long-game language — enduring/compounding, not exit/flip.

**THE question this firm asks first:** "What's the hidden truth about this industry —
and why does your business model make the transformation good for everyone it touches?"
