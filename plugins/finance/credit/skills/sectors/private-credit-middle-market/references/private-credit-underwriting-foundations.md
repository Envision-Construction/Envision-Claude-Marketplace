# Private Credit Underwriting Foundations

## What Makes Private Credit Distinct

Private credit is defined less by a single instrument and more by an underwriting model:

- Small lender group with concentrated hold sizes
- Limited or no secondary liquidity
- Direct access to management, owners, and advisers
- Customized documentation with meaningful maintenance tests
- Ongoing lender involvement after closing

These features shift the analytical burden away from trading optionality and toward cash-flow durability, governance quality, and early-warning control rights.

## Middle Market Context

In the middle market, lenders typically face borrowers that are large enough to support institutional underwriting but small enough that reporting quality, management depth, and lender access vary widely from deal to deal.

The useful framing questions are:

1. Is the business simple enough to underwrite through a full cycle?
2. Is cash generation durable enough to support a held-to-maturity lender?
3. Does the ownership model improve or weaken downside control?
4. Will the lender receive information soon enough to intervene before value erodes?

Avoid relying on market-size statistics or fixed borrower-size cutoffs as the primary definition. The underwriting problem is whether the borrower behaves like a relationship-driven, information-asymmetric middle market credit.

## Core Underwriting Questions

### 1. Business Durability

- What actually drives recurring cash generation?
- How cyclical are revenue, margin, and working capital?
- How concentrated are customers, suppliers, contracts, or end markets?
- What operating disruptions would impair debt service fastest?

### 2. Earnings Quality

- Which EBITDA adjustments are genuinely non-recurring?
- Which costs are necessary to keep the business functioning?
- How much capital expenditure and working capital reinvestment is required to preserve earnings?
- Does reported EBITDA overstate lender-relevant cash generation?

### 3. Ownership and Governance

- Is there a sponsor with both incentive and capacity to support the business?
- If non-sponsored, who provides governance discipline and reporting rigor?
- Who controls key relationships, strategic decisions, and capital allocation?
- What behavior has ownership shown in past amendments, recapitalizations, or downturns?

### 4. Structural Control

- Where does the lender sit in the capital structure?
- What collateral, guarantees, and information rights actually exist?
- How quickly can the lender detect underperformance?
- What amendment, cure, or value-leakage pathways can weaken the original underwriting?

## Private Credit vs. Broadly Syndicated Loans

| Dimension | Private Credit Lens | Broadly Syndicated Lens |
|---|---|---|
| Lender objective | Hold and monitor | Price and trade |
| Documentation | Bespoke, control-oriented | Standardized, market-clearing |
| Information flow | Direct and frequent | Agent-mediated and periodic |
| Covenant style | Maintenance-based | Often incurrence-based |
| Amendment dynamics | Relationship negotiation | Consent solicitation across a wider lender base |
| Exit assumption | Often maturity or refinancing | Secondary sale is more feasible |

This does not automatically make private credit safer. Stronger documents can be offset by valuation opacity, smaller-company volatility, and lender reluctance to force a relationship break.

## Ownership Model Lens

The first branch in the analysis is usually sponsor-backed versus non-sponsored:

- **Sponsor-backed**: More institutional governance, faster access to capital, and more financial engineering risk
- **Non-sponsored**: Tighter alignment to the operating business, but greater key-person, reporting, and governance-substitution risk

Read `references/sponsor-vs-non-sponsor-underwriting.md` once the ownership model is clear.

## Structure Lens

After establishing borrower quality, identify whether the structure introduces special underwriting questions:

- **Unitranche**: Does FOLO economics change control, recovery, or amendment incentives?
- **DDTL / accordion**: Is contingent exposure being underwritten as real leverage or treated as optional?
- **PIK component**: Does the structure delay cash pain while increasing terminal claim size?
- **Fund or vehicle overlay**: Are BDC or other vehicle constraints changing how the lender behaves?

Read the corresponding specialized reference only when that structure is present.

## Durable Underwriting Principles

The following principles should hold across cycles:

- Underwrite cash earnings, not presentation EBITDA
- Give full weight to ownership behavior, not just stated support
- Treat documentation quality as a control tool, not a substitute for business quality
- Underwrite unfunded commitments and contingent features as real downside obligations
- Translate every approval into a monitoring plan with measurable triggers
