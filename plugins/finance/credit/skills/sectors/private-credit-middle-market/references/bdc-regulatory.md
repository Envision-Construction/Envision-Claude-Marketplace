# BDC Regulatory Framework and Constraints

## How to Use This Reference

This is the private-credit skill's authoritative BDC reference. Use it when the investment is held in, compared with, or analyzed through a BDC structure and you need both the qualitative framework and the current regulatory or market-standard numeric constraints that shape underwriting, sizing, and surveillance.

## Why BDC Structure Matters

BDCs are not just another wrapper around direct lending. Their regulatory design shapes:

- how much leverage they can use
- what they can own
- how much income they must distribute
- how often they must mark and disclose portfolio value

Those constraints change lender behavior during both benign and stressed periods.

## Current BDC Constraints

### Asset Coverage

Asset coverage is the practical leverage governor. When marks deteriorate, leverage capacity contracts, which can reduce flexibility precisely when the portfolio needs it most.

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum asset coverage ratio | 150% for BDCs that elected SBCAA relief | 1940 Act Section 18 |
| Prior minimum if relief not elected | 200% | 1940 Act Section 18 |
| Maximum debt-to-equity at 150% | 2.0x | Derived from 150% asset coverage |
| Typical target leverage | 1.0x-1.25x debt-to-equity | Conservative operating buffer |

Analytical implications:

- NAV declines can restrict new borrowing
- the vehicle may be forced to delever sooner than an unregulated private fund
- management may become more defensive around marks, amendments, and exits

### Qualifying Asset Rules

Qualifying asset requirements constrain investable universe and reduce the ability to rotate into whatever public or large-cap credits look most attractive at a given time.

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum qualifying assets | 70% of total assets | 1940 Act Section 55 |
| Typical operating headroom | 75-85% | Industry practice |
| Public company threshold | Market cap below $250M | Eligible portfolio company test |
| Maximum non-qualifying assets | 30% of total assets | Inverse of 70% test |

Analytical implications:

- portfolio construction can remain concentrated in middle market private assets
- headroom matters; a BDC with limited qualifying-asset flexibility has less room to reposition

### RIC Status and Distribution Requirements

Distribution requirements make retained earnings a weak source of capital accumulation.

| Test | Requirement | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Annual income distribution | Minimum 90% of taxable income | Subchapter M |
| Excise tax avoidance | Distribute 98% of ordinary income plus 98.2% of capital gains by year-end | IRC Section 4982 |
| 50% diversification test | At least 50% of assets in positions where no issuer exceeds 5% of assets and no issuer owns more than 10% of voting securities | Subchapter M |
| 25% concentration limit | No more than 25% of assets in a single issuer or controlled group | Subchapter M |
| Income source test | At least 90% of gross income from qualifying investment income | Subchapter M |

Analytical implications:

- strong current income does not automatically create balance-sheet resilience
- external capital market access remains important
- non-cash income items, including PIK, deserve extra scrutiny

### Leverage and Funding Mix

| Source | Typical Leverage | Typical Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secured credit facility | 0.5x-1.0x equity | SOFR + 175-250 bps | First lien on portfolio; advance rates commonly 50-70% |
| Unsecured notes / bonds | 0.25x-0.75x equity | 5.5-7.5% fixed coupon | Usually available to stronger public BDC issuers |
| CLO / securitization | Variable | SOFR + 150-225 bps for senior tranches | Efficient for seasoned, financeable portfolios |
| Total target leverage | 1.0x-1.25x equity | Blended 4.0-5.5% | Conservative versus 2.0x regulatory maximum |

### Additional Structural Constraints

| Constraint | Practical Requirement | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Affiliated transactions | Generally prohibited without exemptive relief or approved co-investment procedures | 1940 Act Section 57 |
| Preferred stock | Treated as senior securities and requires 200% coverage if outstanding | 1940 Act Section 18 |
| SBA debentures | Excluded from asset coverage for eligible SBIC subsidiaries up to statutory program limits | SBIC program rules |
| PIK income | No hard regulatory cap, but should be tracked for cash earnings and dividend sustainability | Board and investor scrutiny |

### Valuation and Disclosure

Quarterly fair value marks and public reporting create both transparency and pressure.

| Area | Typical Application |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | Rare for private credit-heavy BDCs |
| Level 2 | Used for more observable syndicated positions |
| Level 3 | Majority of most BDC portfolios; commonly 70-90% of assets |
| Core public filings | 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, N-2, proxy statement |

Analytical implications:

- portfolio marks affect leverage capacity
- schedule-of-investments disclosure gives analysts better look-through than many private vehicles
- management assumptions around marks should be compared with non-accruals, exits, and peer marks where possible

### Practical Health Thresholds

| Metric | Healthy | Watch | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset coverage ratio | >175% | 160-175% | <160% |
| Non-accruals at fair value | <2% | 2-5% | >5% |
| Debt-to-equity | <1.25x | 1.25x-1.50x | >1.50x |
| Net investment income / dividend | >100% | 90-100% | <90% |
| Level 3 assets as % of portfolio | <80% | 80-90% | >90% |
| Weighted average portfolio yield | >10% | 9-10% | <9% |

## How BDC Rules Affect Underwriting Behavior

- BDCs may favor structures that preserve mark stability and current income
- portfolio managers may be slower to tolerate deeply impaired positions because marks and non-accruals affect both optics and capacity
- facility choices can reflect the interaction between regulatory leverage, ratings, and funding-market access
- PIK income requires special care because it supports taxable income without necessarily providing cash

## Practical Output

When discussing a BDC-backed or BDC-held private credit position, summarize:

1. Which BDC constraint matters most
2. Whether the structure consumes scarce leverage or qualifying-asset capacity
3. How marks, distributions, or reporting could change behavior in stress
4. Which thresholds from this reference were used and whether the company or instrument screens as qualifying
