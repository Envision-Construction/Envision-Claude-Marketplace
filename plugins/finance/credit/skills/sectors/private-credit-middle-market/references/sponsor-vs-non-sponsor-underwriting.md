# Sponsor vs. Non-Sponsor Underwriting

## Why the Distinction Matters

The presence or absence of a financial sponsor changes the credit in three ways:

1. Who governs the borrower
2. How information reaches the lender
3. Who can contribute new capital when performance weakens

Treat ownership model as a first-order underwriting variable, not a footnote.

## Comparison Framework

| Dimension | Sponsor-Backed | Non-Sponsor |
|---|---|---|
| Governance | Formal board oversight and institutional decision-making | Governance may be concentrated in founder, family, or management team |
| Information quality | Usually stronger reporting packages and adviser support | Often more uneven reporting, controls, and forecasting discipline |
| Capital support | Sponsor may inject equity or support amendments | Support depends on owner liquidity and willingness |
| Documentation posture | More pressure for flexibility, baskets, and cure rights | More scope for lender protection and tighter maintenance tests |
| Amendment dynamic | Negotiation often shaped by sponsor relationship value | Negotiation shaped by borrower dependence on lender and limited alternatives |
| Key downside risk | Financial engineering and documentation leakage | Key-person, governance, and reporting fragility |

Neither model is inherently superior. Sponsor-backed deals can fail through aggressive structure; non-sponsored deals can fail through weak governance and concentrated management dependency.

## Sponsor-Backed Underwriting

### Key Questions

- Is the sponsor known for operational execution or for financial engineering?
- Does the sponsor have remaining capacity and willingness to support the asset?
- Is the value-creation plan operationally credible, or does it rely on leverage, multiple expansion, or add-on timing?
- Does the sponsor's proposed documentation preserve optionality at the lender's expense?

### Focus Areas

#### Sponsor Behavior

- Prior amendment behavior
- History of dividend recaps or value extraction
- Track record in stressed situations
- Reputation for transparency with lenders

#### Alignment

- Equity contribution quality, not just headline amount
- Management rollover and incentive design
- Fund life and dry powder position
- Whether ownership is concentrated in the main fund or diluted across co-investors

#### Governance Quality

- Quality of board oversight
- Strength of installed CFO and finance function
- Frequency and quality of management reporting
- Realism of acquisition and integration plans

### Common Sponsor-Backed Risks

- EBITDA addback creep
- Loose restricted payment and incremental debt capacity
- Equity cure used as a substitute for real operational improvement
- Aggressive add-on strategy that changes the original credit
- Sponsor prioritizing franchise relationship or equity optionality over lender protection

## Non-Sponsor Underwriting

### Key Questions

- Can the business function without the owner or founder?
- Are financial statements lender-grade, or do they need remediation?
- What replaces the governance discipline a sponsor would normally provide?
- Are there related-party arrangements or personal objectives that distort the business?

### Focus Areas

#### Key-Person Risk

- Depth below the founder or owner
- Transferability of customer and supplier relationships
- Succession planning
- Documentation of critical operating processes
- Insurance, non-compete, or guarantee support where relevant

#### Governance Substitution

- Independent board or adviser involvement
- Required reporting package and delivery speed
- Lender consent around management change, acquisitions, and related-party actions
- Quality of annual budget and forecast discipline

#### Financial Hygiene

- Audit quality and accounting basis
- Related-party transactions
- Personal expenses or owner compensation distorting EBITDA
- Working capital and tax practices that differ from institutional norms

### Common Non-Sponsor Risks

- The owner is the business
- Reporting quality is too weak to monitor covenant drift early
- Personal motives override creditor discipline
- Governance becomes reactive only after stress emerges
- Documentation does not compensate for the lack of institutional control

## Documentation Differences by Ownership Model

| Topic | Sponsor-Backed Lens | Non-Sponsor Lens |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance covenants | Fewer tests, more cushion pressure from sponsor | More tests can be appropriate to compensate for governance risk |
| EBITDA definition | Higher risk of expansive addbacks and pro forma adjustments | Higher risk of weak accounting quality and owner adjustments |
| Restricted payments | Focus on leakage and recap optionality | Focus on owner distributions and related-party extraction |
| Equity cure | Often requested and sometimes useful | Less relevant unless owner liquidity is clear and enforceable |
| Information rights | Usually easier to negotiate, but discipline still matters | Must be explicit and detailed; do not assume lender-quality reporting appears organically |

## Decision Rules

Prefer a sponsor-backed structure when:

- Governance quality and lender access are meaningfully stronger
- The sponsor has a credible support record
- Documentation still preserves lender control

Prefer a non-sponsored structure when:

- The business is simpler, lower levered, and cash generative
- Ownership is aligned and transparent
- Documentation compensates for governance and reporting risk

Escalate caution when either model shows:

- Repeated amendment history
- Poor reporting credibility
- Value extraction behavior
- Thin management bench
- Documentation that prevents timely intervention

## Practical Output Template

When summarizing ownership risk, state:

1. Ownership model and why it matters
2. Governance strengths and weaknesses
3. Evidence of support or value-leakage behavior
4. Documentation implications
5. Monitoring items that should be tracked post-close
