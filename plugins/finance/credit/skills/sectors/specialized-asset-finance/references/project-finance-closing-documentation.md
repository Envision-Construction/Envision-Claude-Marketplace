# Project Finance Closing Documentation

Project finance closing documentation should answer one question for the lender: if the project underperforms, who controls the asset, the contracts, and the cash? The package below focuses on those timeless control points rather than current pricing or market convention.

## 1. Core Credit Documents

### Common Terms or Credit Agreement

The main credit agreement or common terms agreement should establish:

- Borrower and obligor structure
- Facility purpose, permitted debt, and use of proceeds
- Draw conditions and completion requirements
- Reporting, covenant, and reserve obligations
- Cash waterfall, distribution restrictions, and enforcement triggers
- Default mechanics, voting thresholds, and agent authority

### Facility-Specific Documents

Separate facility agreements or note documents may be needed when the structure includes:

- Multiple debt tranches
- Construction and term phases
- Hedging support
- Standby, contingency, or working-capital facilities

The document set should make subordination, sharing, and amendment mechanics unambiguous across tranches.

## 2. Borrower and Security Perimeter

### SPV and Organizational Documents

The formation package should prove that the project sits in a bankruptcy-remote borrower with:

- Narrow permitted activities
- Separateness covenants
- Clear governance and approval rights
- Transfer mechanics that permit enforcement or replacement ownership

### Security Package

The collateral package should cover the assets that actually support recovery:

- Equity pledges over the SPV
- Security over project accounts
- Assignments of material contracts, permits, and insurance proceeds
- Mortgages, vessel or aircraft liens, or other asset-specific perfection steps where relevant
- Local-law filings and opinions needed to preserve priority

The lender should be able to trace how control moves from the sponsor to the secured parties after default.

## 3. Direct Agreements and Counterparty Control

Direct agreements are central to non-recourse enforcement because they preserve value in the project contracts after borrower distress.

For key counterparties such as the offtaker, EPC contractor, O&M provider, concession grantor, and major suppliers, direct agreements should typically provide:

- Notice before termination
- Cure rights for lenders
- Consent to assignment or novation
- Step-in or replacement rights where legally available
- Recognition of the collateral assignment and account waterfall

If a core contract can terminate without lender notice and cure rights, the lender may lose practical control of the project before enforcing on the collateral.

## 4. Construction and Completion Protection

Construction-period documentation should show who absorbs time, cost, and performance risk.

### EPC and Completion Support

Review whether the documentation clearly addresses:

- Scope certainty and change-order control
- Completion timetable and testing regime
- Delay and performance remedies
- Bonding, parent support, or other completion credit support
- Interface risk if multiple contractors share responsibility

### Drawdown Discipline

Construction draw conditions should ordinarily require evidence that:

- No default is continuing
- Equity is funded as required
- Construction remains within approved budget and schedule
- Insurance and permits remain in force
- The independent engineer supports the requested draw

The purpose of draw conditions is not paperwork; it is to prevent debt from funding an unrecoverable project drift.

## 5. Intercreditor and Enforcement Mechanics

Any multi-tranche structure should document:

- Priority of payment in normal operations and enforcement
- Amendment and consent thresholds
- Standstill arrangements between senior and junior creditors
- Who can direct the collateral agent
- How proceeds are shared after realization

These provisions should be internally consistent with the operating waterfall, reserve structure, and direct agreements. A technically strong security package can still fail if intercreditor rights are ambiguous at the point of stress.

## 6. Concession, Offtake, and Revenue Documents

The revenue-side contracts should align the debt tenor with the enforceable cash-flow tail.

Review whether the offtake or concession package clearly addresses:

- Contract term relative to debt maturity
- Payment basis: take-or-pay, availability, tolling, volume exposure, or merchant tail
- Price-adjustment mechanisms and indexation
- Termination compensation
- Change-in-law and force-majeure treatment
- Assignment and lender cure rights

Where repayment depends on contracted cash flow, the lender is effectively underwriting the contract as much as the physical asset.

## 7. Conditions Precedent to Close

The closing checklist should confirm that corporate, legal, technical, and financial diligence has converted into enforceable deliverables.

Typical conditions precedent include:

- Executed finance documents and all security perfection steps
- Delivered legal opinions on enforceability, capacity, perfection, and local-law matters
- Executed project contracts and direct agreements
- Evidence of required insurance placement
- Independent engineer reports and any agreed technical sign-offs
- Approved financial model and equity commitment evidence
- Opened controlled accounts and completed account-control documentation
- Required permits, licenses, and regulatory approvals

## 8. Review Questions

Use this document to answer the following before calling a project "closed":

1. Can the lender control the cash, the equity, and the core contracts after default?
2. Are completion and performance risks contractually pushed to parties with both capability and credit support?
3. Do the direct agreements preserve value in the project during distress rather than merely documenting intent?
4. Does the documentation bridge the full debt life, or does recovery still depend on assumptions outside the legal package?
