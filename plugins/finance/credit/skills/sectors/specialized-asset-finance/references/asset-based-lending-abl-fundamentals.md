## Asset-Based Lending Fundamentals

Asset-based lending is collateral-first lending. Maximum debt is set by the value and eligibility of pledged assets rather than by EBITDA multiples alone. That makes ABL useful when the borrower has meaningful liquid assets but uneven or insufficient cash flow for a conventional cash-flow facility.

## Borrowing Base Logic

At its core:

`Maximum availability = sum of eligible collateral by category x applicable advance rate`

The borrowing base depends on two questions:

1. **What collateral is eligible?**
2. **How much of that eligible value should the lender advance against?**

Eligibility rules should exclude assets that are difficult to realize or easy to manipulate, such as:

- aged or disputed receivables
- concentrated or related-party receivables
- obsolete, damaged, or consigned inventory
- assets without clear title or controllable proceeds

Advance rates should reflect:

- liquidation value rather than headline fair value
- volatility of the collateral
- quality of reporting and lender control
- time and cost required to realize value

Use `references/typical-deal-parameters.md` for current advance-rate and pricing conventions.

## Core Collateral Types

### Accounts Receivable

Receivables are strongest when they are short-dated, diversified, and owed by solvent counterparties with clean documentation. Key risks include disputes, dilution, concentration, and slow collections.

### Inventory

Inventory is strongest when it is fungible, saleable, and supported by reliable counts and turnover data. Key risks include obsolescence, seasonality, shrinkage, and weak liquidation channels.

### Equipment and Fixed Assets

Equipment support is strongest when the assets are essential, well maintained, and have a deep resale market. The lender should focus on orderly and forced-sale value, depreciation pace, and whether amortization keeps pace with collateral decline.

## Facility Design

Common ABL structures include:

- **Revolving facilities** tied to periodic borrowing-base certificates
- **FILO or junior availability tranches** that sit below the core advance base
- **Term components** supported by slower-turning assets such as equipment

The structure should match collateral behavior. Short-duration assets support flexible revolvers; slower-turning assets usually require more amortization discipline.

## Monitoring and Control

ABL protection depends on active administration, not just initial underwriting.

### Borrowing-Base Reporting

Borrowers should provide regular borrowing-base certificates with enough detail to test:

- collateral eligibility
- concentrations
- dilution or credits
- availability versus current borrowings

### Field Exams and Appraisals

Use field exams and appraisals to validate data quality, collateral condition, and liquidation assumptions. Frequency should reflect collateral volatility and borrower credibility.

### Cash Dominion and Trigger Framework

Cash dominion, blocked accounts, or similar controls should activate when the lender needs direct control of collections. Treat dominion triggers as warning signs that the borrower is moving from ordinary-course financing toward workout behavior.

## When ABL Fits

ABL is most appropriate when:

- the borrower is asset-rich but cash-flow-volatile
- working-capital needs fluctuate with seasonality or cycle
- collateral can be monitored and realized more reliably than EBITDA can be forecast
- the lender wants self-correcting availability rather than a fixed debt quantum

ABL is less reliable when collateral data is weak, liquidation channels are thin, or the business depends on assets whose value disappears once operations stop.
