## Risk Identification and Allocation

Project finance works when major risks are allocated to the party best positioned to control them and when the lender can see that allocation clearly in the documents. This reference is a map of the main risk categories and the questions a lender should ask.

## Construction Risk

Construction risk covers delay, cost overrun, design failure, site surprises, and completion shortfalls.

Ask:

- Is there a credible party standing behind completion?
- Are time, cost, and performance obligations clearly assigned?
- Can the lender monitor progress before funding more debt?

## Operating Risk

Operating risk covers asset reliability, maintenance execution, cost creep, and performance degradation over time.

Ask:

- Who is responsible for maintaining availability and condition?
- Does the structure fund predictable maintenance before stress appears?
- Can poor performance be detected early through reporting and technical review?

## Market and Revenue Risk

Market risk covers volume, price, utilization, curtailment, and tariff or demand shortfall.

Ask:

- Is revenue contracted, regulated, or exposed to market prices?
- Does the downside case sit with the project, the offtaker, or a public counterparty?
- Can price or volume deterioration happen at the same time as refinancing stress?

## Counterparty Risk

Counterparty risk arises when the project depends on the continued performance of an offtaker, contractor, operator, supplier, or guarantor.

Ask:

- Is the counterparty creditworthy enough for the role it plays?
- Are support obligations enforceable and durable through stress?
- Can the lender replace the counterparty without collapsing project value?

## Political and Regulatory Risk

Political and regulatory risk includes expropriation, permit loss, tariff intervention, adverse legal change, transfer restrictions, and contract repudiation.

Ask:

- Does the project depend on a stable legal regime or public behavior that may not hold in stress?
- Are compensation, arbitration, or insurance protections meaningful in practice?
- Can cash be converted, distributed, or enforced across relevant jurisdictions?

## Financial Risk

Financial risk includes rate exposure, currency mismatch, reserve weakness, and refinancing dependence.

Ask:

- Does debt service move with variables the project cannot control?
- Is the structure robust without assuming easy refinancing?
- Are sponsors providing real support, or only a narrative backstop?

## Summary Table

| Risk Type | Primary Question | Typical Lender Protection |
|---|---|---|
| Construction | Who gets the project built on time and on spec? | EPC support, completion tests, independent engineer |
| Operations | Who preserves performance and condition after close? | O&M standards, reserves, reporting, replacement rights |
| Revenue | Who absorbs weak volume, price, or utilization? | Contract structure, tariff regime, sweep and lock-up mechanics |
| Counterparty | What if a key party fails? | Guarantees, direct agreements, step-in and replacement rights |
| Political / Regulatory | What if the legal or public framework shifts? | Concession protections, arbitration, insurance, structural buffers |
| Financial | What if rates, currency, or refinancing turn against the project? | Hedging, amortization discipline, reserve design, equity support |
