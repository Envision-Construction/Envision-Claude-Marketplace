# Worked Example: Multifamily Acquisition Underwriting

## Meridian Gardens — 250-Unit Class B Multifamily, Austin TX Metro

This worked example walks through a complete lender underwriting of a stabilized multifamily acquisition. The case is illustrative and should not be used as a source for current cap rates, spreads, reserves, or market assumptions; use root references for live benchmarks. Use this as a template for structuring multifamily credit analysis from property-level NOI through loan sizing and stress testing.

## 2. NOI Build-Up

The underwriter NOI is constructed from trailing twelve-month (TTM) actuals, adjusted for known items and normalized to reflect stabilized operations. All figures annualized.

### Revenue

| Less: Vacancy & Credit Loss | (6.0%) of GPR | ($243,000) |
| **Effective Gross Rent** | | **$3,807,000** |
| Parking Income | 85 reserved spaces x $75/mo x 12 | $76,500 |
| Laundry Income | TTM actual | $42,000 |

| Application/Admin Fees | TTM actual | $28,500 |
| Late Fees & Other | TTM actual | $14,200 |

**Vacancy assumption:** The underwriter applies 6.0% vacancy and credit loss, above the property's trailing 5.2% physical vacancy, to reflect (a) Austin submarket new supply pipeline (2,400 units delivering within 3-mile radius over 24 months), and (b) normalized credit loss of ~0.8% consistent with Class B tenant profile.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Effective Gross Income** | $4,000,000 |

**Note on reserves:** Replacement reserves of $250/unit/year ($62,500 total) reflect the 2019 renovation recency. **Operating expense ratio:** Total expenses at 46.0% of EGI are within the 42-48% range typical for Class B garden-style multifamily in the Austin metro. Real estate taxes are the largest single line item; Travis County reassessment risk at acquisition is addressed in Section 7.

The selected cap rate is conservative relative to the sponsor's 4.90% assumption.

## 5. Loan Sizing

The underwriter sizes the loan against three simultaneous constraints. The maximum loan amount is set by the most restrictive constraint.

### Constraint Analysis

| Constraint | Parameter | Calculation | Maximum Loan |
|---|---|---|---|
| LTV | 75.0% max | 75.0% x $41,100,000 | $30,825,000 |
| DSCR | 1.25x min (IO) | NOI net of reserves / 1.25 = max debt service; solve for principal at 6.50% IO | $25,569,231 |
| Debt Yield | 8.0% min | $2,097,500 / 8.0% | $26,218,750 |

### DSCR Constraint Detail

```
Required minimum DSCR = 1.25x
NOI net of reserves = $2,097,500
Maximum annual debt service = $2,097,500 / 1.25 = $1,678,000
At 6.50% interest-only rate:
Maximum loan = $1,678,000 / 0.065 = $25,815,385
```

### Sizing Summary

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Appraised Value | $41,100,000 |
| LTV Constraint (75%) | $30,825,000 |
| DSCR Constraint (1.25x @ 6.50% IO) | $25,815,385 |
| Debt Yield Constraint (8.0%) | $26,218,750 |
| **Binding Constraint** | **DSCR** |
| **Recommended Loan Amount** | **$25,800,000** |
| Actual LTV | 62.8% |
| Actual DSCR | 1.25x |
| Actual Debt Yield | 8.13% |

**Sizing commentary:** DSCR is the binding constraint, which is typical in the current rate environment where elevated interest rates compress debt service capacity before LTV limits are reached. The resulting 62.8% LTV provides meaningful equity cushion. The lender recommends a $25,800,000 first mortgage (rounded down from the DSCR-implied maximum for administrative convenience and modest additional cushion).

## 6. Stress Scenarios

Stress testing evaluates the loan's resilience under adverse conditions. Each scenario is applied independently and then in combination to identify break-even thresholds.

### Individual Stress Scenarios

| Scenario | Assumption | Stressed NOI | DSCR (IO) | LTV (at 5.25% cap) | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base Case** | As underwritten | $2,097,500 | 1.25x | 62.8% | Meets all thresholds |
| **Rate Shock (+200 bps)** | Rate to 8.50% | $2,097,500 | 0.96x | 62.8% | **Fails DSCR** |
| **Rate Shock (+100 bps)** | Rate to 7.50% | $2,097,500 | 1.08x | 62.8% | Below 1.20x minimum |
| **Occupancy Decline (to 85%)** | Vacancy 15% (vs. 6%) | $1,733,500 | 1.03x | 76.0% | **Fails DSCR**; LTV stressed |
| **Occupancy Decline (to 88%)** | Vacancy 12% | $1,855,500 | 1.11x | 70.7% | Below 1.20x minimum |
| **Expense Surge (+15%)** | All OpEx up 15% | $1,821,500 | 1.09x | 67.4% | Below 1.20x minimum |
| **Rent Decline (-10%)** | All rents down 10% | $1,716,800 | 1.02x | 77.2% | **Fails DSCR**; LTV stressed |

### Combination Stress (Severe Downside)

| Combined Scenario | Stressed NOI | DSCR (IO) | LTV | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Occupancy 88% + Expense +10% | $1,671,500 | 1.00x | 74.6% | Breakeven; no margin |
| Occupancy 85% + Rate +100 bps | $1,733,500 | 0.90x | 76.0% | **Fails DSCR and LTV** |
| Rent -5% + Expense +10% + Rate +100 bps | $1,645,000 | 0.85x | 72.9% | **Severe stress; cash trap triggered** |

### Break-Even Analysis

| Metric | Break-Even Point | Current Cushion |
|---|---|---|
| Occupancy (DSCR = 1.00x) | 82.3% | 11.7 percentage points |
| Rent Decline (DSCR = 1.00x) | -12.8% | $173/unit headroom |
| Rate Increase (DSCR = 1.00x) | +165 bps (to 8.15%) | 165 bps of rate cushion |
| Expense Increase (DSCR = 1.00x) | +21.2% | Substantial but monitor insurance/tax |

**Stress conclusions:** The loan withstands moderate single-factor stresses but is vulnerable to combination scenarios, particularly those involving simultaneous occupancy decline and rate increases. The 5-year fixed rate mitigates refinance risk during the initial term. The primary risk is a repricing event at maturity if rates remain elevated and NOI growth underperforms. The 62.8% in-place LTV provides meaningful value cushion; the property would need to decline approximately 37% in value before the loan balance exceeds asset value.

| Coverage | Annual Premium | $/Unit | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|

**Insurance risk factors:** Texas property insurance has experienced sustained hardening driven by hail, wind, and convective storm losses. The underwriter models 5.0% annual insurance escalation (above the 2.5% general expense growth) through the hold period. This is reflected in the DCF (Section 4). Should insurance costs escalate at 10%+ annually, the NOI impact is approximately $18,500 per year of additional escalation, or roughly 0.9% of NOI, which is manageable but cumulative over a 10-year hold.

### Property Tax Assessment

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Current Assessed Value | $34,200,000 (Travis County, 2025) |
| Current Tax Rate | 1.81% (combined county/city/school) |
| Current Tax Expense | $619,020 |
| Post-Acquisition Assessed Value (est.) | $41,100,000 (reassessment to sale price) |
| Post-Acquisition Tax Expense (est.) | $743,910 |
| Year 1 Tax Increase | $124,890 (+20.2%) |
| Appeal Potential | Moderate; 10-15% reduction achievable based on comp adjustments |

**Tax underwriting approach:** The underwriter models real estate taxes at $620,000 in Year 1, which assumes a successful appeal reducing the reassessed value by approximately 15% from the full acquisition price. This is consistent with typical Travis County appeal outcomes for multifamily properties. If the appeal is unsuccessful, the incremental tax burden of approximately $124,000 would reduce Year 1 NOI by 5.8% and compress DSCR to 1.17x on an IO basis, which would breach the 1.20x soft covenant. The lender should condition the loan on the sponsor engaging a qualified Texas property tax consultant within 60 days of closing.
