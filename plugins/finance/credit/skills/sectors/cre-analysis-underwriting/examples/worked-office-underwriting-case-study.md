## 3. Weighted Average Lease Term (WALT) Calculation

WALT is calculated by weighting each tenant's remaining lease term by their share of occupied SF.

| Tenant | SF | Remaining Term (yrs) | SF x Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| TechFlow Inc. | 42,000 | 2.75 | 115,500 |
| Southern Insurance Group | 28,000 | 1.00 | 28,000 |
| DataCore Analytics | 22,000 | 0.25 | 5,500 |
| Meridian Medical Associates | 18,000 | 3.50 | 63,000 |
| Regional Accounting LLP | 14,000 | 0.75 | 10,500 |
| CreativeWorks Agency | 10,000 | 1.25 | 12,500 |
| SmallBiz Consulting | 8,600 | 2.00 | 17,200 |
| Legal Associates PLLC | 5,000 | 1.75 | 8,750 |
| **Total** | **147,600** | | **260,950** |

**WALT = 260,950 / 147,600 = 1.77 years**

> **Assessment:** A WALT of ~1.8 years is short for an office property (typical institutional threshold is 3-5 years). This indicates significant near-term lease rollover risk and will be a key underwriting concern. Combined with 18% existing vacancy, the property faces substantial re-leasing execution risk over the loan term.

## 6. Three-Constraint Loan Sizing

Using Underwriter Adjusted NOI of $2,037,656 for all calculations.

### Loan Parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Underwriter NOI (adjusted) | $2,037,656 |
| Cap Rate | 7.25% |
| Appraised Value | $28,105,600 |
| Interest Rate | 5.75% (SOFR 3.00% + 275bps) |
| Amortization | 30-year |
| Annual Debt Constant | 7.20% |
| Loan Term | 5 years (balloon) |

### Constraint Analysis

| Constraint | Formula | Calculation | Max Loan | Binding? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LTV (65%)** | Appraised Value x Max LTV | $28,105,600 x 65% | **$18,268,640** | **Yes** |
| DSCR (1.25x) | NOI / (Min DSCR x Debt Constant) | $2,037,656 / (1.25 x 7.20%) | $22,634,178 | No |
| Debt Yield (10.0%) | NOI / Min Debt Yield | $2,037,656 / 10.0% | $20,376,560 | No |

**Sized Loan: $18,268,640 (constrained by LTV)**

### Why LTV is Binding

The LTV constraint is binding because the relatively high cap rate (7.25%) reflects the post-COVID suburban office risk discount, which suppresses the appraised value relative to the property's cash flow. In a lower cap rate environment (e.g., 6.25% for Class A urban office), the same NOI would support a higher appraised value, and the DSCR or debt yield constraint would more likely be binding.

| 7.75% (+50bps) | $26,292,335 | $17,090,018 | ($1,178,622) |

**Key takeaway:** Each 25bps move in cap rate shifts proceeds by approximately $630K-$660K. If cap rates compress 50bps (market recovery scenario), the borrower gains over $1.35M in additional loan capacity. Conversely, a 50bps widening reduces proceeds by nearly $1.2M. This asymmetry reflects the convex relationship between cap rate and value at higher cap rate levels.

## 8. Rate Stress (DSCR Impact)

Using the sized loan amount of $18,268,640 and Underwriter NOI of $2,037,656:

| Rate Scenario | All-In Rate | Annual Debt Service | DSCR | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base Case (SOFR 3.00% + 275bps) | 5.75% | $1,315,342 | 1.55x | Comfortable |
| +100bps | 6.75% | $1,443,821 | 1.41x | Adequate |
| +200bps | 7.75% | $1,578,136 | 1.29x | Marginal — near 1.25x floor |
| +300bps | 8.75% | $1,718,126 | 1.19x | **BREACH** — below 1.25x minimum |

**Analysis:**

- The loan can absorb approximately 200bps of rate increase before breaching the 1.25x DSCR floor
- At +200bps, the DSCR of 1.29x provides only 4bps of cushion above the covenant — functionally at the boundary
- A +300bps scenario (all-in rate of 8.75%) would result in a clear covenant breach at 1.19x
- **Rate cap recommendation:** Require a SOFR cap at 5.00% (200bps above current) for the loan term to protect against rate stress. Cost estimate: 150-200bps upfront on notional

**Critical finding:** 41.1% of NRA (74,000 SF) rolls within 24 months. Combined with 18% existing vacancy, total exposure is effectively 59.1% of NRA at risk within 2 years.

### Downside Scenario: DataCore Vacates + One Additional Tenant Loss

| Metric | Current | DataCore Vacates | DataCore + 1 Additional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occupied SF | 147,600 | 125,600 | 111,600 |
| Vacancy Rate | 18.0% | 30.2% | 38.0% |
| Estimated NOI Impact | — | -27% | -35% |
| Resulting DSCR (est.) | 1.55x | 1.13x | 1.01x |

At 38% vacancy, the DSCR drops to approximately 1.01x — effectively breakeven on debt service and a clear covenant default.

### Mitigation Requirements

1. **Pre-funding condition:** Require that 50% of DataCore's space (11,000 SF) is either re-leased with executed LOIs or that the borrower escrows 18 months of TI/LC costs ($25-30/SF = $275,000-$330,000) before loan closing
2. **Rollover reserve:** Establish a $500,000 funded reserve at closing for TI/LC on near-term expirations, replenished from excess cash flow
3. **Leasing trigger:** If occupancy falls below 75%, sweep 100% of excess cash flow to a leasing reserve (cash trap)
4. **Southern Insurance renewal:** Require evidence of renewal discussions or executed LOI for Southern Insurance (28,000 SF, Mar 2027 expiry) within 6 months of closing
5. **Reporting:** Quarterly leasing reports including pipeline, LOIs, and executed leases with rent and term detail

| Net Absorption (trailing 12M) | Positive (150,000 SF) | Negative (-200,000 SF) | N/A |
| Under Construction | 150,000 SF (90% pre-leased) | 1.2M SF | Limited competitive risk |

### Demand Drivers

- **Corporate relocations:** Continued inflow from Northeast and West Coast companies seeking lower-cost suburban office markets; Alpharetta benefits from proximity to GA-400 corridor and tech talent pool
- **Technology sector:** Growing presence of mid-size tech firms; Alpharetta positioned as Atlanta's secondary tech hub
- **Healthcare services:** Medical office demand stable; Meridian Medical Associates is representative of growing medical tenant base in suburban office parks

### Risk Factors

- **Work-from-home (WFH) structural shift:** Office demand broadly challenged by hybrid work adoption. Suburban office partially insulated vs. CBD (shorter commutes, lower density preference), but not immune to reduced space-per-employee ratios
- **Flight to quality:** Tenants increasingly favor newer, amenity-rich buildings; Class B+ vintage 2005 product faces competitive pressure from newer Class A deliveries, potentially requiring capital investment to maintain competitiveness
- **Metro-wide vacancy:** Atlanta metro office vacancy at 18% is elevated; while Alpharetta outperforms, a macro deterioration could pressure the submarket
- **Interest rate environment:** Higher rates compress CRE valuations and increase refinancing risk at loan maturity

### Submarket Assessment

Alpharetta is a relative bright spot within a challenging Atlanta office market. The submarket's 12% vacancy rate, positive net absorption, and limited new supply provide a constructive backdrop. However, the subject property's 18% vacancy matches the weaker metro-wide average rather than the submarket, suggesting property-specific leasing challenges that need to be addressed through capital investment and active management.

### Recommendation

Proceed with caution. The property's cash flow metrics support the loan at current rates, but the combination of short WALT, above-market vacancy, and 41% near-term rollover creates meaningful execution risk. Key conditions for approval:

1. Pre-funding resolution of DataCore space (re-lease or escrow)
2. Funded TI/LC reserve of $500,000
3. SOFR rate cap at 5.00% for loan term
4. Cash flow sweep trigger at 75% occupancy
5. Quarterly leasing and occupancy reporting
6. Evidence of Southern Insurance renewal discussions within 6 months
