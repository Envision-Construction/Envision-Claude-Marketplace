# Property Risk Assessment

Property risk assessment asks whether the real estate can continue to produce income, preserve value, and remain financeable through ordinary stress.

## 1. Risk Categories

| Risk Category | Core Question |
|---|---|
| **Physical condition** | What capital needs or system failures can impair occupancy or cash flow? |
| **Functional utility** | Does the building still meet what the market needs? |
| **Location and market position** | Is the asset competitively placed within its submarket? |
| **Environmental and hazard exposure** | Can contamination or natural hazards impair value, occupancy, or lender remedies? |
| **Legal and regulatory risk** | Could zoning, use restrictions, rent regulation, or title issues change economics? |
| **Execution risk** | Does the business plan depend on capex, leasing, or repositioning that may fail? |

## 2. Physical Condition and Capital Needs

Property condition matters when it changes actual cash flow or future financeability.

### Review:

- roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, elevators, paving, and life-safety systems
- deferred maintenance backlog
- remaining useful life of major systems
- recurring capital requirements versus one-time catch-up capex
- whether current reported NOI already assumes unrealistic maintenance intensity

If large capital needs are near-term, the lender should treat them as a real call on cash flow rather than an abstract future concern.

## 3. Functional Obsolescence

A building can be physically serviceable but economically obsolete.

### Examples of functional weakness

- low clear height in logistics assets
- inefficient floor plates in office
- poor parking, loading, access, or circulation
- outdated building systems or weak power / data infrastructure
- layouts that no longer match tenant demand

The key underwriting question is: **can the next tenant use the building without major additional capital?**

## 4. Location and Market Position

Location risk is not just a map issue. It is a competitiveness issue.

Review:

- access, visibility, and convenience for the likely user base
- neighborhood trajectory
- quality of surrounding inventory
- liquidity of the submarket in sale or refinance
- whether the subject is above, at, or below local competitive standard

If the subject chronically underperforms stronger nearby assets, that is often a property problem, not just a market problem.

## 5. Environmental and Hazard Exposure

Environmental or hazard risk matters when it can:

- reduce value
- delay leasing or sale
- create cleanup or insurance costs
- impair lender foreclosure or recovery

Coordinate this review with `skills/cre-analysis-underwriting/references/environmental-liability-assessment.md`.

## 6. Legal and Regulatory Risk

Review:

- zoning and legal conforming use
- certificates, permits, and required approvals
- title exceptions, easements, and access constraints
- rent regulation, affordability restrictions, or preservation controls
- litigation or compliance issues tied to operation of the property

An otherwise good asset can become hard collateral if legal constraints narrow use or cap economics.

## 7. Business-Plan and Execution Risk

Risk increases when value depends on future work rather than present income.

### Key questions

- Is repayment tied to current performance or future stabilization?
- How much capex, leasing, or repositioning remains?
- What happens if the timeline slips or rents underperform?
- Are reserves and sponsor liquidity sufficient for downside execution?

## 8. Translating Risk into Underwriting

Property risk should change underwriting through:

- lower or more conservative NOI
- wider cap rate or broader value range
- lower leverage
- larger reserves
- stronger closing conditions
- tighter monitoring or escalation triggers

If a risk is material but does not change structure, proceeds, conditions, or monitoring, it has probably not been fully underwritten.

## 9. Output Template

Show:

1. **Key risk**
2. **Why it matters to cash flow, value, or refinanceability**
3. **Evidence supporting the concern**
4. **Mitigant or structural response**
5. **Residual risk if the mitigant fails**

## Common Mistakes

- Treating deferred maintenance as cosmetic when it affects tenant retention or reserve need
- Assuming a building is competitive because it is occupied today
- Ignoring legal or title constraints until closing
- Mentioning risk qualitatively without changing underwriting assumptions
- Underestimating the interaction between physical weakness and weaker market liquidity

## Portfolio Context

This section covers situations where multiple CRE exposures are being compared, aggregated, or scaled. It is not a substitute for portfolio-wide risk parameters in root references.

### What Scaling Risk Means

A property can look acceptable on its own and still be a poor portfolio addition if it increases concentration, maturity clustering, strategy risk, or exposure to the same macro driver.

### Main Portfolio Lenses

| Lens | Key Question |
|---|---|
| **Property type** | Does the exposure increase concentration in one operating model or demand cycle? |
| **Geography** | Are multiple assets exposed to the same local market shock, weather hazard, or regulation? |
| **Strategy** | Is the portfolio becoming too dependent on lease-up, capex, or development execution? |
| **Tenant / income profile** | Are cash flows diversified by tenant type, lease duration, and occupancy model? |
| **Maturity ladder** | Do multiple loans depend on the same refinance window? |

### CRE-Specific Scaling Risks

- too much exposure to one market narrative
- too much rollover in the same period
- too much business-plan risk disguised as "light value-add"
- repeating the same tenant, sponsor, or submarket exposure through multiple deals
- stacking correlated insurance, climate, or regulatory risk

### What a Good Portfolio View Should Show

1. **Asset mix**
2. **Market mix**
3. **Strategy mix**
4. **Refinance schedule**
5. **Largest correlated risk drivers**

For actual portfolio limits and mandate thresholds, use `references/portfolio-risk-parameters.md` rather than this local skill file.

### Portfolio Context Common Mistakes

- Scaling one good deal into a bad concentration
- Treating diversification by property count as diversification by risk driver
- Ignoring maturity clustering
- Calling multiple assets "diversified" even though they depend on the same macro thesis
