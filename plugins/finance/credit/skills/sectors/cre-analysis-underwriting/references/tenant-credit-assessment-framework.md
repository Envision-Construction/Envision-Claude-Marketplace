# Tenant Credit Assessment Framework

Tenant credit matters most when lease cash flow is concentrated, specialized, or expensive to replace. The goal is not to produce a perfect corporate credit memo for every tenant, but to determine how dependable the lease stream really is.

## 1. Start with Concentration

Tenant credit analysis should scale with exposure.

| Exposure Profile | Credit Work Needed |
|---|---|
| **Diffuse tenancy** | Focus on rent roll behavior, collections, and market depth more than individual tenant credit |
| **Moderate concentration** | Review major tenants individually and map rollover consequences |
| **Single-tenant or highly concentrated** | Tenant credit is effectively the whole credit and should be underwritten accordingly |

## 2. What to Review

For material tenants, gather what is reasonably available:

- financial statements or public filings
- payment history and credit reports where relevant
- lease economics relative to the tenant's business
- business reliance on the location
- remaining term, options, and termination rights

The question is whether the tenant can and wants to stay.

## 3. Core Questions

- Is the tenant financially able to perform under the lease?
- Is the location strategically important to the tenant?
- How replaceable is the space to this tenant and how replaceable is the tenant to the landlord?
- If the tenant vacates, how hard and expensive is it to re-lease the space?

## 4. Financial Indicators

Exact thresholds will vary by tenant type, but the most useful indicators are:

- revenue direction and business stability
- profitability or cash generation
- leverage and liquidity
- fixed-charge burden, including rent
- signs of stress such as losses, shrinking scale, or payment issues

For smaller private tenants, the absence of good data is itself a risk input.

## 5. Lease Matters Too

Tenant credit cannot be separated from lease structure.

### Review:

- remaining lease term
- renewal options and pricing
- reimbursement structure
- early termination or contraction rights
- assignment and sublease flexibility
- guarantees or credit support

A strong tenant with an easy termination right can still create weak lease cash flow.

## 6. Rent Burden and Location Dependence

Two practical lenses matter:

- **Rent burden**: Is the lease payment comfortably absorbable within the tenant's economics?
- **Location dependence**: Would moving materially disrupt operations, sales, logistics, or customer relationships?

High location dependence can offset some financial weakness. Low location dependence can make even a healthy tenant more mobile.

## 7. Portfolio and Rollover Effects

Tenant credit should be linked to the expiration schedule.

Key outputs:

- top-tenant concentration
- years with major rent at risk
- weighted average lease term
- downside case if a major tenant does not renew

The loss of a weak near-term tenant is more important than the long-term presence of a stronger small tenant.

## 8. Property-Type Differences

| Property Type | Tenant Credit Emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Office** | Credit quality, WALT, and release cost are central |
| **Retail** | Tenant sales and occupancy cost matter as much as financial statements |
| **Industrial** | Tenant use, facility dependence, and replacement demand are key |
| **Multifamily** | Individual tenant credit matters less than aggregate collections and affordability |
| **Special purpose / single tenant** | Tenant credit often dominates the whole underwriting outcome |

## 9. Output Template

Show:

1. **Concentration summary**
2. **Material tenant review**
3. **Lease-specific risks**
4. **Rollover implications**
5. **Downside scenario if a major tenant weakens or vacates**

## Common Mistakes

- Doing deep tenant credit work on immaterial tenants while ignoring concentration
- Treating lease term as a substitute for tenant health
- Ignoring termination rights, assignment flexibility, or weak guarantees
- Assuming private or unrated tenants are safe because current rent is being paid
- Failing to connect tenant weakness to re-leasing cost and downtime
