## Part 5: Investment Strategy Spectrum & Lender Positioning

Strategy labels are underwriting shorthand, not valuation answers. They tell the lender how much of the asset's value comes from present cash flow versus future execution.

Use this file to classify strategy risk and frame lender questions. Use `references/typical-deal-parameters.md` and `references/market-benchmarks.md` for current leverage, pricing, and market-term calibration.

### 1. Strategy Spectrum

| Strategy | Asset State | Value Source | Main Risk | Lender Stance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Stabilized, high-quality, low near-term business-plan dependence | Durable in-place cash flow | Market repricing and tenant concentration, not execution | Most comfortable with term debt and lighter structural complexity |
| **Core-Plus** | Stabilized but with modest operational or leasing upside | Present income plus limited execution | Lease rollover, light capex, modest mark-to-market risk | Underwrite primarily to in-place performance with selective credit for upside |
| **Value-Add** | Transitional, partially leased, underinvested, or operationally fixable | Business-plan execution | Lease-up, rent reset, capex execution, sponsor capability | Require more cushion, stronger reserves, and sharper downside proof |
| **Opportunistic** | Ground-up, major redevelopment, distressed rescue, or deep repositioning | Future completion, leasing, or recapitalization event | Construction, entitlement, market timing, and take-out risk | Treat as high-control capital, not stable-income lending |

### 2. What Changes Across the Spectrum

| Dimension | Core | Core-Plus | Value-Add | Opportunistic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reliance on current NOI** | High | High to medium | Medium to low | Low |
| **Reliance on sponsor execution** | Low | Moderate | High | Very high |
| **Tolerance for lease rollover** | Higher when staggered and creditworthy | Moderate | Low unless underwritten explicitly | N/A or secondary to construction / launch |
| **Importance of reserves and controls** | Moderate | Moderate | High | Very high |
| **Refinance certainty** | Higher | Good if plan is modest | Depends on stabilization success | Often the core underwriting question |

### 3. Core and Core-Plus

For core and core-plus assets, the lender is mainly asking whether current income is durable and whether any "safe upside" is actually observable.

#### Core underwriting questions

- Is the income stream durable without assuming rent growth?
- Are tenant credit, lease term, and asset quality strong enough to support lower volatility?
- Is the property truly stabilized, or is the label disguising hidden capex or rollover risk?

#### Core-plus underwriting questions

- Is the upside incremental rather than thesis-defining?
- Can below-market rents, expense savings, or light renovations be evidenced through current comps and scope?
- Does the asset still work at conservative in-place or underwriter NOI if the upside arrives slowly?

### 4. Value-Add

Value-add means the lender is financing a transition, not just a property.

#### Typical value-add ingredients

- Vacancy or lease rollover that must be absorbed
- Deferred maintenance or amenity refresh
- Weak or below-market in-place rents
- Tenant mix improvement or re-tenanting
- Operating cleanup, collections improvement, or management reset

#### Key lender questions

- What part of the plan is proven today, and what part depends on future market acceptance?
- Can the sponsor execute the physical and leasing plan?
- How much cash burn occurs before stabilization?
- What happens if rents, occupancy, or timing miss the plan materially?

For assets with material capex, lease-up, or funded interest needs, pair this file with `references/construction-development-underwriting.md`.

### 5. Opportunistic

Opportunistic situations are driven by future events rather than current income. Underwriting should focus on project controls, downside funding, and exit optionality.

Typical opportunistic situations include:

- Ground-up development
- Major redevelopment or change of use
- Deeply distressed acquisition requiring rescue capital
- Assets without a reliable operating history

#### Key lender questions

- Can the asset be completed, leased, and refinanced without relying on optimistic markets?
- Is the sponsor capitalized well enough to absorb delay, overrun, or slower absorption?
- Are the approvals, controls, and draw mechanics strong enough to protect the lender if execution slips?

### 6. Common Misclassification Errors

- Calling a transitional asset "core-plus" when repayment depends on significant lease-up or capex
- Treating low occupancy with high theoretical mark-to-market as a modest execution story
- Giving credit for future stabilization before proving reserve sufficiency and take-out feasibility
- Labeling recent delivery with no operating history as core solely because the building is new

### 7. Underwriting Rule of Thumb

The further right an asset sits on the strategy spectrum, the more the lender should:

- rely on conservative current evidence rather than projected upside,
- demand stronger reserves and sponsor support,
- test refinance and timing risk, and
- reduce the amount of value attributed to unproven assumptions.
