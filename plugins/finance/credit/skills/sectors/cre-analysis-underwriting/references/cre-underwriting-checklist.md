# CRE Underwriting Checklist

Use this checklist as a workflow control, not a substitute for judgment. Pull current pricing, leverage norms, and stress calibrations from root references rather than hard-coding them here.

## 1. Initial Screen

- [ ] Identify property type, subtype, location, and business plan: stabilized, light value-add, heavy repositioning, development, or rescue.
- [ ] Determine what repays the loan: in-place cash flow, lease-up, sale, refinance, sponsor support, or some combination.
- [ ] Check whether the requested structure is consistent with the asset's current stage and risk transfer.
- [ ] Confirm the proposed basis and valuation story are explainable through current income, replacement context, and market evidence.
- [ ] Flag early whether the asset is likely to become a surveillance, workout, or rescue-capital case rather than a standard underwrite.

## 2. Financial and Operating Diligence

- [ ] Obtain operating statements long enough to distinguish stable run rate from temporary noise.
- [ ] Rebuild NOI from the rent roll and supporting statements rather than relying on sponsor summary figures.
- [ ] Separate in-place, underwriter, stabilized, and sponsor cases.
- [ ] Validate vacancy, credit loss, reimbursements, management fee treatment, and recurring capital needs.
- [ ] Test whether taxes, insurance, payroll, utilities, or repairs are understated relative to the property's actual profile.
- [ ] For hospitality or other operating businesses, review the operating statement with emphasis on margin durability, management dependence, and reserve needs.

## 3. Tenancy and Lease Review

- [ ] Review tenant concentration by rent, area, and rollover timing.
- [ ] Build a lease expiration schedule and identify cliffs, rollover clusters, and near-term exposure.
- [ ] Compare in-place rents with market rents and note where upside or rollover risk is concentrated.
- [ ] Underwrite downtime, concessions, TI/LC, and renewal probability rather than assuming frictionless rollover.
- [ ] Review sample leases for termination rights, co-tenancy, expense recoveries, assignment provisions, rent abatements, and unusual landlord obligations.
- [ ] For single-tenant or highly concentrated assets, determine whether tenant credit is effectively the whole credit.

## 4. Market and Competitive Position

- [ ] Identify the true submarket and competitive set rather than relying on broad metro averages.
- [ ] Evaluate demand drivers, supply pipeline, absorption, concessions, and liquidity.
- [ ] Determine whether current occupancy and rents reflect market position or property-specific underperformance.
- [ ] Distinguish structural advantages from cyclical tailwinds.
- [ ] For specialized assets, test whether there is a deep buyer and tenant universe on downside.

## 5. Physical, Legal, and Environmental Diligence

- [ ] Review property condition, major systems, deferred maintenance, and recurring capital needs.
- [ ] Confirm zoning, entitlements, certificates, and any use or redevelopment constraints.
- [ ] Review title, survey, easements, encroachments, and access issues.
- [ ] Complete environmental review appropriate to the asset and use.
- [ ] Check flood, seismic, wind, insurance, or climate-related exposures that can alter operating resilience or refinanceability.

## 6. Valuation

- [ ] Build a cap-rate value from underwriter NOI, not sponsor upside.
- [ ] Cross-check with sales comparison and cost or replacement context where appropriate.
- [ ] Explain why each valuation method is or is not reliable for the asset.
- [ ] Show how valuation changes under weaker rents, lower occupancy, or wider exit cap rates.
- [ ] For transitional assets, keep as-is and stabilized values separate.

## 7. Loan Sizing and Structure

- [ ] Size the loan under LTV, DSCR, and debt yield simultaneously.
- [ ] Identify the binding constraint and explain why it binds for this asset and lender type.
- [ ] Test reserves for TI/LC, capex, taxes, insurance, debt service, interest carry, and lease-up where relevant.
- [ ] Review recourse, cash management, extension conditions, and refinance assumptions.
- [ ] For transitional or construction loans, tie future funding to verified milestones and reserve discipline.

## 8. Stress Testing

- [ ] Stress occupancy, rents, expenses, capital needs, cap rates, and refinance rates.
- [ ] For concentrated assets, run tenant-loss and rollover-event scenarios.
- [ ] For transitional assets, stress delayed stabilization, weaker absorption, and reserve exhaustion.
- [ ] Show value, DSCR, debt yield, refinance proceeds, and any equity gap under downside cases.
- [ ] Link scenario severity to `references/stress-scenario-framework.md` rather than inventing ad hoc shocks.

## 9. Approval Conditions

- [ ] List all third-party reports, lease support, tenant information, and reserve requirements still needed for closing.
- [ ] State any sponsor liquidity, guarantee, completion support, or tax and insurance conditions.
- [ ] Identify the items that would change proceeds, structure, or recommendation if they come back negatively.
- [ ] Convert key underwriting uncertainties into explicit closing conditions or post-close monitoring triggers.

## 10. Post-Close Monitoring Setup

- [ ] Define the metrics that matter most for this asset: occupancy, collections, lease-up, rollover, reserve burn, property condition, or operating margin.
- [ ] Set escalation triggers for deterioration in NOI, occupancy, major tenant health, reserve usage, or refinance outlook.
- [ ] For conditionally approved deals, map each condition to monitoring cadence, evidence required, and escalation tier.

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating sponsor pro forma as if it were today's cash flow
- Ignoring rollover friction in office, retail, or other tenant-concentrated assets
- Using metro averages where the subject clearly trades off submarket reality
- Treating a refinance story as certain when it only works under optimistic exit assumptions
- Under-reserving TI/LC, capex, taxes, insurance, or interest carry
- Relying on one valuation method even though the asset is clearly outside that method's comfort zone

## Due Diligence Document Request Checklist

Use this checklist to make sure the lender has the materials needed to underwrite the asset and close the loan.

### Financial and Operating Documents

- [ ] Operating statements for at least 3 periods (for example T-12, T-24, T-36 or equivalent)
- [ ] Current rent roll with rents, escalations, concessions, lease expiry, and vacant-space detail
- [ ] Lease abstracts for major tenants and any tenant that drives concentration risk
- [ ] Accounts receivable aging, collections trends, and bad-debt history
- [ ] Real estate tax bills and assessment history
- [ ] Insurance schedules, claims history, and loss runs
- [ ] Utility invoices and major service cost history
- [ ] Service and maintenance contracts
- [ ] Historical capex spend and current capital plan
- [ ] Any required property operating permits or licenses

### Legal and Title Documents

- [ ] Title commitment and exception schedule
- [ ] Current survey
- [ ] Ground lease documents if applicable
- [ ] Borrower and guarantor organizational documents
- [ ] Authorized signatory evidence
- [ ] Zoning confirmation and certificate of occupancy
- [ ] Litigation and lien searches
- [ ] UCC searches where relevant

### Physical and Environmental Documents

- [ ] Property condition report or capital needs assessment
- [ ] Phase I ESA
- [ ] Phase II or other follow-up testing if triggered by the Phase I
- [ ] Engineering reports on major systems where warranted
- [ ] Fire / life-safety testing and inspection evidence
- [ ] Elevator, boiler, or similar regulated-equipment certificates where applicable
- [ ] ADA review where applicable
- [ ] Specialty reports for seismic, flood, mold, asbestos, or lead when relevant to the asset

### Market and Valuation Documents

- [ ] Independent appraisal
- [ ] Market or submarket study
- [ ] Comparable rent and sale support
- [ ] Tenant credit information for major tenants
- [ ] Lease market survey where rollover or TI/LC burden matters

### Management and Operational Documents

- [ ] Property management agreement
- [ ] Manager license and insurance evidence where applicable
- [ ] Vendor list and major operating contracts
- [ ] Material tenant notices, disputes, or complaint history if available

### Document Request Use Notes

- Ask for the documents first; do not substitute narrative sponsor summaries for primary materials.
- Missing documents should be surfaced as explicit diligence gaps, not filled with assumptions.
- Asset-specific add-ons are expected for hotels, healthcare, industrial specialty use, or major redevelopment situations.
