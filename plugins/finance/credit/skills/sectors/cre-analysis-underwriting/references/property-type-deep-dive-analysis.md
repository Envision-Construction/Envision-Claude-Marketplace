# Property Type Deep Dive Analysis

Property type analysis should answer three questions:

1. **What actually drives cash flow for this asset type?**
2. **What causes cash flow to fail?**
3. **Which underwriting metrics matter most for lender downside?**

Use root references for current market ranges. This file focuses on operating logic and failure modes.

## Multifamily

### Operating model

- Many small tenants create granularity and reduce single-credit exposure.
- Lease terms are short, so current rent rolls reprice to market quickly.
- Turnover, concessions, taxes, insurance, payroll, and maintenance discipline drive margin durability.

### Main underwriting focus

- In-place versus market rent by unit type
- Economic occupancy and collection quality, not just physical occupancy
- Renewal and turnover behavior
- Expense control and recurring capital needs
- Affordability and submarket supply pressure

### Common failure modes

- Rent growth outpaces local affordability
- New deliveries force concessions before headline rents fall
- Deferred maintenance or older-vintage capex consumes cash flow
- Heavy renovation plans assume premium capture without enough absorption support

### Special considerations

- Subsidized or regulated housing can improve revenue visibility but adds regulatory and compliance risk.
- Student housing, senior housing, and other operational variants should be underwritten with their distinct demand cycles and management dependence.

## Office

### Operating model

- Cash flow is driven by a smaller number of larger leases with meaningful rollover events.
- TI/LC burden, downtime, and tenant credit matter as much as face rent.
- Long WALT can stabilize near-term cash flow, but only if tenants are likely to stay.

### Main underwriting focus

- Tenant concentration and rollover schedule
- Tenant credit quality and business relevance of the location
- TI/LC reserve need and release downtime
- Building competitiveness: quality, configuration, parking, amenities, and obsolescence risk
- Whether demand for the product is cyclical or structurally impaired

### Common failure modes

- Large rollover cliffs in weak leasing markets
- Above-market rents that reset downward at expiration
- Functional obsolescence relative to better-positioned inventory
- Under-reserved TI and concessions

## Retail

### Operating model

- Cash flow depends on the property's role in the consumer trip pattern and the health of tenant sales.
- Anchor quality, co-tenancy, and category resilience often matter more than simple occupancy.
- Tenant demand is highly sensitive to traffic, access, visibility, and local trade-area competition.

### Main underwriting focus

- Tenant sales and occupancy cost
- Anchor dependency and traffic generation
- Co-tenancy and percentage-rent provisions
- Category resilience versus displacement risk
- Re-tenanting depth if an anchor or large box goes dark

### Common failure modes

- Anchor departure cascades into tenant outs or rent reductions
- Weak tenant economics masked by temporary sales strength
- Single-tenant retail underwritten like diversified retail
- Overlooking category obsolescence and local competition

## Industrial

### Operating model

- Cash flow often benefits from lower tenanting friction than office, but functional utility is critical.
- Building specification, access, and location can matter more than cosmetic quality.
- Tenant stickiness is high when relocation is operationally disruptive.

### Main underwriting focus

- Clear height, loading, truck court, power, refrigeration, or specialized infrastructure where relevant
- Proximity to labor, highways, ports, rail, or population centers
- Tenant use and replacement demand
- Single-tenant dependence versus multi-tenant fragmentation
- Ability to re-lease the building without major capital spend

### Common failure modes

- Functionally obsolete design even in a strong market
- Overstated demand for highly specialized improvements
- Single-tenant binary exposure treated as generic industrial risk

## Hospitality

### Operating model

- Hospitality is an operating business, not a lease business.
- Revenue reprices constantly and depends on demand, brand, and management execution.
- Margin resilience and reserve discipline matter as much as top-line demand.

### Main underwriting focus

- Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, and GOP or operating margin
- Brand strength, management quality, and termination rights
- FF&E reserve adequacy and renovation cycle
- Demand segmentation: business, leisure, group, contract, or extended stay
- Sensitivity to recession, weather, event, or travel disruption

### Common failure modes

- Assuming recent occupancy is durable without regard to demand mix
- Understating management dependence
- Ignoring renovation cycle and FF&E drain
- Using stabilized-lease assumptions on a daily-rate business

## Special Purpose and Niche Assets

Examples include self-storage, medical office, senior housing, data centers, life science, student housing, and other specialized formats.

### Underwrite in two layers

1. **Real estate layer**: location, replacement cost, physical utility, buyer depth
2. **Operating / user layer**: specialized demand, switching costs, regulation, and alternative-use risk

### Key question

**If the current tenant, operator, or use case disappears, who is the next buyer or user and what capex is required to get there?**

## Property-Type Comparison

| Property Type | Cash Flow Profile | Main Downside Driver | Underwriting Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Multifamily** | Granular, frequently repriced | Occupancy and concession pressure | Market rent, turnover, expense control |
| **Office** | Concentrated lease cash flows | Rollover, TI/LC, and obsolescence | Tenant credit, WALT, release economics |
| **Retail** | Traffic and sales dependent | Anchor loss and category weakness | Tenant sales, co-tenancy, anchor quality |
| **Industrial** | Function-driven occupancy | Functional obsolescence or binary tenant risk | Building utility, location, tenant use |
| **Hospitality** | Operating-business cash flow | Demand shock and management failure | Margin resilience, brand, reserve discipline |
| **Special purpose** | Often niche and relationship-based | Alternative-use weakness | Buyer depth, conversion cost, specialized demand |

## Common Mistakes

- Applying the same vacancy, reserve, and rollover logic to every property type
- Treating all stabilized occupancy as equally durable across asset classes
- Ignoring operating-business risk in hospitality or specialized formats
- Ignoring tenant granularity benefits in multifamily or single-tenant concentration in office, retail, and industrial
- Treating niche assets as safer simply because recent demand has been strong
