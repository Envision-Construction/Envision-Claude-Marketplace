## Part 8: Whole Business Securitization (WBS)

### Definition & Key Distinction

WBS securitizes the **operating cash flows of an entire business** (or material portion), not discrete financial assets. The business continues operating; securitized bonds are secured by the business's assets and cash flows. Differs from traditional ABS (where assets are passive, static pools).

### Common Sectors

- **Restaurant/Hospitality**: Franchise royalty streams, hotel revenue
- **Entertainment**: Theme park revenue, sports venue, concert venue, cinema
- **Healthcare**: Physician group receivables, skilled nursing facility revenue
- **Utility**: Tower lease revenue, wireless tower rents
- **IP/Royalties**: Music royalties, sports royalties, patent royalties
- **Transportation**: Toll road revenue, airline aircraft lease receivables
- **Retail**: Sales revenue of operating stores (rare, newer)

### Securitization Mechanics

**Operating Company (OpCo)** structured as SPV:
- OpCo is the securitization entity (often newly formed or ring-fenced portion of larger business)
- OpCo owns business assets: brand, franchise agreements, leases, IP, physical assets (stores, equipment)
- OpCo continues operating the business (unlike traditional ABS where assets are static)
- Manager/operator runs the day-to-day business (may be original owner under management agreement)

**Securitization bonds secured by**:
- Pledge of OpCo's assets (brand, franchise agreements, equipment, real estate)
- OpCo's operating cash flows (royalties, rents, revenue)
- Margin between revenues and OpCo costs

### Covenants & Restrictions (similar to corporate bonds but more restrictive)

- **CapEx limits**: Restrict capital expenditures to specified amount or % of EBITDA (preserve cash for debt service)
- **Distribution tests**: OpCo can only distribute cash to sponsors if certain leverage/coverage tests met
- **Financial maintenance tests**: DSCR must exceed threshold (e.g., 1.25x), leverage must not exceed ceiling (e.g., 2.0x)
- **Rapid amortization**: If tests fail, rapid amortization triggered (faster principal paydown)
- **Incurrence tests**: Restrictions on new debt, capital leases
- **Asset sales**: Limited asset sales permitted (e.g., up to 10% portfolio)
- **Refinancing**: Limited ability to refinance debt

### Key Risks (Operational)

**Operational risk**: Business must continue operating. Unlike ABS where assets are passive (loans collect payments, equipment depreciates, mortgages amortize), WBS requires ongoing business operations:
- Management execution (can management run the business profitably?)
- Customer demand (will customers continue to patronize restaurant, theme park, etc.?)
- Competition (new entrants, substitute products)
- Regulation (FDA for healthcare, EPA for utilities, labor laws)

**Brand / Customer risk**:
- Theme park appeal dependent on new attractions, guest satisfaction
- Restaurant quality dependent on chef, kitchen staff, service
- Retail brand may lose cachet

**Commodity / Cost inflation risk**:
- Labor cost increases (especially post-COVID in hospitality/restaurant)
- Commodity input costs (food, energy)
- Constraints on pricing (unable to pass costs to consumers without losing sales)

**Lease / Contract renewal risk**:
- Franchise royalty deal expires, renegotiated at lower rate or not renewed
- Key tenant lease expires, not renewed at same rent
- Supplier/vendor contracts may be renegotiated

**Leverage constraint**:
- OpCo heavily leveraged (typically 2.5-4.0x EBITDA leverage) — less flexibility for downturns
- Little room for operational underperformance before covenants breached

### Analysis Framework for WBS

1. **Business quality**:
   - Historical cash flow stability (3-5 year track record)
   - EBITDA growth trajectory
   - Competitive positioning (moat, brand strength)
   - Management team quality and incentive alignment

2. **Franchise/Contract renewal risk**:
   - Remaining contract term (% of OpCo revenue at risk of non-renewal in next 5 years)
   - Historical renewal rates (% of contracts renewed)
   - Renegotiation leverage (OpCo strength vs. counterparty)

3. **Leverage profile**:
   - Total debt / EBITDA (current, pro-forma)
   - DSCR (current, stress-tested)
   - Available flexibility (undrawn reserves, distributor cash)

4. **Stress scenarios**:
   - 10-15% EBITDA decline (recession, COVID shock)
   - 5-10% customer count decline (competition, macro)
   - Covenant headroom (how far down before breach?)

5. **Tax efficiency**:
   - WBS often structured as pass-through entity (partnership, LLC) for tax purposes
   - Sponsors may benefit from operational losses (tax shield), reducing after-tax cost of capital

6. **Exit / refinancing**:
   - Call date (when sponsor can refinance, pay off early?)
   - Refinancing scenarios (need to maintain covenant compliance)
   - Sponsor's motivation to exit vs. hold
