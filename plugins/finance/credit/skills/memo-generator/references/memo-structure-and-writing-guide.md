# IC Memo Structure, Writing Guide & Quality Checklist

## Purpose

The Investment Committee memo is the primary deliverable through which a credit analyst communicates an investment thesis, supporting analysis, and actionable recommendation. A well-constructed IC memo enables decision-makers to evaluate a credit opportunity efficiently, challenge assumptions, and arrive at an informed allocation decision. This document defines the structural standards, section-by-section writing guidance, and quality checklist that govern all IC memos.

---

## Standard Memo Structure

Every IC memo follows a consistent architecture regardless of credit type. The standard structure contains eight to ten sections, ordered to guide the reader from high-level thesis through detailed analysis to actionable recommendation.

### Section Sequence

1. **Executive Summary** — Standalone, decision-ready overview of the entire analysis. One page maximum.
2. **Investment Thesis** — The core argument for or against the credit, stated in two to four bullet points with supporting rationale.
3. **Business Overview** — Description and assessment of the issuer's operations, market position, competitive dynamics, and credit-relevant business characteristics.
4. **Industry Analysis** — Sector backdrop, cyclical positioning, secular trends, regulatory environment, and competitive landscape as they affect credit quality.
5. **Financial Analysis** — Historical performance, projected financials, credit metric trends, liquidity assessment, and scenario analysis. Always includes both management and analyst base cases.
6. **Capital Structure & Covenants** — Complete debt stack with maturities, pricing, security, and intercreditor dynamics. Covenant analysis with headroom calculations for leveraged credits.
7. **Relative Value** — Positioning versus comparable credits on spread, leverage, quality, and structural basis. Must answer the question: why this name over alternatives?
8. **Key Risks & Mitigants** — Paired presentation of material risks with specific, credible mitigants. Ordered by probability-weighted severity.
9. **ESG Considerations** — Environmental, social, and governance factors material to the credit, including sustainability-linked features where applicable.
10. **Recommendation** — Clear action (buy, sell, hold, increase, reduce), conviction level, position sizing guidance, entry parameters, catalysts, and monitoring triggers.

### Appendices (as needed)

- Detailed financial model output
- Organizational charts
- Comparable company and transaction data
- Covenant compliance calculations
- Rating agency summaries

## What Distinguishes a Strong Memo from a Weak One

### Characteristics of a Strong Memo

- **Thesis-driven**: Every section reinforces, challenges, or contextualizes the central thesis. No orphan analysis.
- **Analytically rigorous**: Claims are supported by data, calculations, or sourced observations. Quantitative assertions include the underlying math.
- **Balanced**: Presents both the bull and bear case honestly. Does not suppress inconvenient data points.
- **Forward-looking**: Emphasizes trajectory and catalysts over static snapshots. Historical data serves to establish trends, not as the conclusion.
- **Actionable**: The reader knows exactly what to do, at what price, in what size, and what would change the view.
- **Concise**: Respects the reader's time. Every paragraph earns its place. No boilerplate filler.

### Characteristics of a Weak Memo

- Reads as a company description rather than a credit analysis
- Buries the recommendation or states it without conviction
- Presents financial data without interpreting what it means for credit quality
- Lists generic risks without assessing probability, severity, or mitigants
- Omits relative value context — analyzes the credit in a vacuum
- Contains stale market data or outdated financial information
- Shows confirmation bias — selectively presents data supporting a predetermined conclusion

## Tone and Style

### Voice

IC memos are written in a **factual, balanced, and authoritative** voice. The analyst is an independent assessor, not an advocate. Avoid promotional language, hedged non-commitments, and vague qualifiers.

**Do**: "Leverage of 4.8x is elevated relative to the 3.5x peer median, but the deleveraging trajectory to 3.9x by YE2026 is credible given contracted revenue visibility."

**Avoid**: "The company has somewhat high leverage but management seems confident they can bring it down, which could be positive for the credit."

### Conventions

- Use active voice and direct statements
- Lead paragraphs with the conclusion, then support it
- Quantify whenever possible — replace adjectives with numbers
- Attribute views clearly: distinguish analyst assessment from management guidance, rating agency opinion, and market consensus
- Define abbreviations on first use; use standard credit market terminology thereafter
- Present financial figures consistently (same units, same rounding convention throughout)
- Include source citations inline throughout the memo, not only in footnotes or appendices
- When a stable direct URL exists, include that URL inline with the citation so source links can be harvested into a consolidated appendix later
- Keep locator detail with the citation when available (for example: 10-K page, slide number, filing date, report date, or access date)
- Prefer canonical primary-source URLs; do not cite search-result links when a direct filing, company, or data-provider page is available
- If no stable URL exists for a source, cite it normally without fabricating a link

### Tense

- Historical analysis in past tense
- Current state in present tense
- Projections and recommendations in future tense or conditional

## Length Guidelines by Credit Type

| Credit Type | Target Length | Executive Summary |
|---|---|---|
| Investment Grade Corporate | 5-8 pages | 0.5-1 page |
| High Yield / Leveraged Loan | 8-15 pages | 1 page |
| Private Credit / Direct Lending | 10-20 pages | 1-1.5 pages |
| Commercial Real Estate | 8-15 pages | 1 page |
| Structured Finance (ABS/CLO) | 10-18 pages | 1 page |
| Distressed / Special Situations | 12-20 pages | 1-1.5 pages |

These are guidelines, not hard limits. Complexity dictates length. A straightforward IG rolldown trade merits fewer pages than a complex distressed restructuring. The test is whether every page adds analytical value.

## Framing the Recommendation

### Thesis Clarity

The investment thesis must be expressible in two to four bullet points that a portfolio manager could read in 30 seconds and understand the core argument. Each bullet should be specific, falsifiable, and tied to a measurable outcome.

**Strong thesis bullets**:
- Deleveraging from 5.2x to 3.8x over 18 months driven by $200M mandatory amortization and 150bps EBITDA margin expansion from completed restructuring
- 75bps spread pickup to BB-rated healthcare peers compensates for 0.5x higher leverage and smaller scale, with catalyst from pending IG upgrade (Moody's positive outlook assigned Q1)

**Weak thesis bullets**:
- Good company with solid management team
- Attractive spread relative to peers

### Conviction Level

Every recommendation carries a conviction level that signals the analyst's confidence and the quality of available information:

- **High Conviction**: Thesis is well-supported by proprietary analysis, multiple data points align, catalysts are near-term and identifiable, downside is bounded
- **Medium Conviction**: Thesis is sound but depends on execution or external factors with meaningful uncertainty; some information gaps remain
- **Low Conviction**: Thesis has merit but significant uncertainties persist; position sizing should reflect elevated risk of thesis violation

### Recommendation Components

A complete recommendation includes:
- **Action**: Buy, sell, hold, increase, reduce, or avoid
- **Conviction**: High, medium, or low
- **Instrument**: Specific bond, loan, or CDS reference
- **Size**: Suggested allocation as percentage of portfolio or absolute notional
- **Entry Point**: Target spread, price, or yield for execution
- **Catalysts**: Specific events expected to drive the thesis (with timeline)
- **Stop-Loss / Thesis Violation**: Conditions that would trigger a reassessment or exit
- **Monitoring Triggers**: Metrics and events to watch between IC reviews

## Section-by-Section Writing Guide

### 1. Executive Summary

#### Objective
Distill the entire credit analysis into a single page that enables a decision-maker to form an informed view without reading the full memo. The executive summary is not an introduction — it is a standalone decision document.

#### Structure
Open with a two-line identification block (issuer, instrument, key terms), then move directly to the thesis. Follow with strengths, risks, a metrics snapshot, and close with the recommendation. Use a compact metrics table to deliver quantitative context efficiently.

#### Required Content
- **Identification**: Issuer name, sector, ratings (all agencies), instrument description (CUSIP/facility, size, coupon/spread, maturity, ranking)
- **Transaction Context**: New issue terms or secondary trading level, use of proceeds
- **Thesis Statement**: Two to three sentences capturing the core investment argument
- **Credit Strengths**: Three to four bullets, each quantified (e.g., "Market-leading 35% share in a consolidated industry with high barriers to entry")
- **Credit Risks**: Two to three bullets with probability and severity context
- **Metrics Table**: Leverage, coverage, liquidity, and one credit-type-specific metric — all showing current and projected values
- **Relative Value Snapshot**: One line positioning vs. comps (e.g., "Trading 50bps wide of BB healthcare median despite lower leverage")
- **Recommendation Line**: Bold. Action, conviction, size, entry point, primary catalyst

#### Formatting
- Maximum one page (single-spaced, standard margins)
- Use bullet points for strengths and risks
- Include a small summary table for key metrics
- Bold the recommendation line
- Date-stamp all market data referenced

#### Quality Markers
- A PM can make an allocation decision from this page alone
- Every number references the current or projected period — no stale data
- The thesis is specific enough to be falsifiable
- Strengths and risks are not generic — they reflect this specific credit
- The recommendation is unambiguous

#### Common Mistakes
- Exceeding one page — forces the reader into body sections
- Writing an introduction instead of a summary (saving the recommendation for the end)
- Using qualitative language where numbers exist ("strong margins" instead of "28% EBITDA margin, 400bps above peer median")
- Listing company features as strengths without assessing their credit relevance
- Omitting relative value context entirely
- Omitting projected metrics (showing only current period)
- Missing conviction level or position sizing guidance on the recommendation
- Failing to date-stamp market data

---

### 2. Investment Thesis

#### Required Content
- Two to four bullet points, each falsifiable and quantified
- Measurable outcomes or observable catalysts for each bullet
- Explicit time horizon for thesis realization
- Thesis violation conditions: what would change the view

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Thesis is stated clearly and is directionally correct
- **Strong**: Each bullet references a specific, measurable outcome with a timeline; thesis violation conditions are explicit and actionable

#### Common Mistakes
- Missing time horizon for the thesis
- Quantification absent within thesis bullets (adjectives instead of numbers)
- No conditions under which the thesis fails

---

### 3. Business Overview

#### Objective
Assess the quality, sustainability, and predictability of the issuer's business model from a credit perspective. This is not a company description — it is an evaluation of how business characteristics translate to credit risk.

#### Structure
Lead with a one-paragraph credit assessment of the business (strong, adequate, weak — and why). Then systematically address revenue quality, competitive position, customer dynamics, and operational profile. Close with management and ownership assessment.

#### Analytical Approach

**Revenue Quality Assessment**
- Categorize revenue: recurring/contractual vs. transactional vs. project-based
- Quantify visibility: backlog, contracted revenue, renewal rates
- Assess pricing power: ability to pass through cost inflation, historical pricing trends
- Evaluate cyclicality: revenue beta to GDP, performance in prior downturns

**Competitive Position**
- Do not merely list competitors. Assess the durability of competitive advantages:
  - Scale advantages: cost position, distribution reach, R&D leverage
  - Switching costs: integration depth, data dependencies, contractual lock-in
  - Regulatory moats: licensing, permits, compliance barriers
  - Intellectual property: patents, proprietary technology, brand value
- Quantify market share and trend (gaining, stable, losing)
- Assess barriers to entry and threat of disruption

**Customer and Supplier Dynamics**
- Top 10 customer concentration (revenue percentage and credit quality)
- Customer retention and churn metrics
- Supplier diversification and input cost exposure
- Bargaining power assessment on both sides

**Management and Ownership**
- Track record in capital allocation (M&A discipline, leverage management, shareholder returns)
- Alignment of interests: equity ownership, compensation structure
- Depth of management team beyond the CEO
- Ownership structure: public, private equity, family — and implications for credit behavior

#### Required Checklist Items
- Revenue composition by segment, geography, and customer type
- Revenue quality assessment: recurring vs. transactional, contractual visibility
- Market position with quantified market share
- Competitive advantages assessed for durability (not merely listed)
- Customer concentration analysis with top 10 or top 5 revenue share
- Supplier diversification and input cost exposure
- Management track record in capital allocation
- Ownership structure and alignment of interests

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Business is described accurately with credit-relevant characteristics identified
- **Strong**: Each business characteristic is explicitly linked to its credit impact; competitive advantages are assessed for sustainability with supporting evidence

#### Common Mistakes
- Writing a Wikipedia-style company description
- Listing products and services without assessing credit relevance
- Stating competitive advantages without evidence of durability
- Ignoring customer concentration or dismissing it without analysis
- Describing management as "experienced" without assessing their credit-relevant track record
- Missing revenue quality breakdown

### 4. Industry Analysis

#### Required Checklist Items
- Industry size and growth rate
- Cyclicality profile with historical downturn performance
- Competitive structure: fragmented vs. consolidated, pricing dynamics
- Secular trends: tailwinds and headwinds over the credit horizon
- Regulatory environment and pending material changes
- Technology disruption risk
- Current cycle positioning assessment

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Industry dynamics are described with relevant data points
- **Strong**: Industry analysis directly informs the credit thesis; cycle positioning is supported by data; downturn sensitivity is quantified

#### Common Mistakes
- Missing cycle positioning (where the industry sits currently)
- No quantified historical downturn performance
- Omitting technology disruption assessment
- Ignoring regulatory pipeline beyond current state

### 5. Financial Analysis

#### Objective
Establish the credit trajectory — where the credit has been, where it is going under multiple scenarios, and what drives the difference. Financial analysis in a credit memo is not accounting; it is credit storytelling supported by numbers.

#### Structure
Open with the credit narrative: one paragraph stating the financial story (deleveraging, stable cash generation, margin inflection, deteriorating coverage — whatever defines this credit). Then present historical trends, projections, and scenario analysis in that order.

#### Analytical Approach

**Lead with the Credit Story**
Before presenting any numbers, articulate what the financial trajectory means for the credit:
- "This is a deleveraging story: leverage peaked at 6.2x post-acquisition and is on track to reach 4.5x by YE2026 through mandatory amortization and EBITDA growth."
- "FCF has been negative for three consecutive years due to capex intensity; the inflection to positive FCF in 2026 is the key catalyst."
- "Margins are compressing as input costs outpace pricing, pressuring coverage from 3.2x to 2.5x — the question is whether this stabilizes."

**Historical Trend Analysis**
- Present three to five years of key metrics: Revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA margin, FCF, leverage, coverage
- Focus on trends and inflection points, not individual years
- Explain what drove changes (organic growth, acquisitions, restructuring, market conditions)
- Highlight EBITDA adjustments and quality-of-earnings issues

**Projection Framework**
- Always present at least two cases:
  - **Management/Sponsor Case**: The issuer's own projections (often optimistic). State source and date.
  - **Analyst Base Case**: Your independent assessment. Must be clearly differentiated from management and internally consistent.
- Show the key assumption differences between cases (revenue growth, margins, capex, working capital)
- Project minimum three years forward for key credit metrics
- Present each case with a clear assumptions table before the output metrics

**Scenario Analysis**
- **Downside Case**: What happens in a recession or business-specific stress? Define the scenario explicitly (e.g., "15% revenue decline, 300bps margin compression, no voluntary deleveraging"). Show the resulting metrics and assess covenant compliance, liquidity runway, and debt serviceability.
- **Upside Case** (optional): Useful for identifying early exit or upsize opportunities

**Key Metrics to Always Include**
- Total Debt / EBITDA and Net Debt / EBITDA (current and projected)
- EBITDA / Cash Interest Expense and EBITDA / Total Interest Expense
- FCF / Total Debt (cash flow debt coverage)
- Capex / Revenue (split maintenance vs. growth where available)
- Liquidity: cash + revolver availability - near-term maturities
- Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio (for covenant-relevant analysis)

#### Required Checklist Items
- Three-year historical income statement, cash flow, and balance sheet trends
- Analyst base case projections (minimum three years forward)
- Management case vs. analyst case with key assumption differences highlighted
- Downside/stress scenario with explicitly stated assumptions
- Credit metrics trajectory: leverage, coverage, FCF/debt, capex/revenue
- Margin analysis with trend commentary (gross, EBITDA, FCF)
- Liquidity analysis: cash, revolver availability, near-term maturities
- EBITDA adjustment scrutiny: add-backs identified and assessed for quality
- Working capital dynamics and seasonality impact

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Historical and projected financials presented with key metrics
- **Strong**: Opens with the credit narrative before numbers; management vs. analyst case differences are quantified; downside scenario is detailed and plausible; EBITDA quality is independently assessed

#### Common Mistakes
- Presenting financials without interpreting what they mean for the credit
- Using only the management case without an independent analyst view
- Omitting downside scenario analysis
- Showing metrics at a single point in time without trajectory
- Accepting EBITDA add-backs without questioning quality of earnings
- Not disclosing the source and date of projections
- Missing FCF conversion analysis (EBITDA to actual cash generation)
- Omitting liquidity waterfall including near-term maturities

### 6. Capital Structure & Covenants

#### Objective
Quantify the debt stack, assess tranche-level risk and recovery, and evaluate the adequacy of structural protections. Capital structure analysis must answer: what happens to my tranche if things go wrong?

#### Structure
Present the complete debt stack in a table, then analyze maturity profile, cost of capital, recovery positioning, and covenant protections. Always orient the analysis around the specific tranche under consideration.

#### Analytical Approach

**Debt Stack Table**
Present a complete table with columns for: Instrument, Outstanding Amount, Rate/Spread, Maturity, Ranking, Security, and Recovery Rate (estimated). Include undrawn revolver capacity. Show total debt, net debt, and enterprise value for context.

**Maturity Profile**
- Identify the maturity wall and assess refinancing risk
- Calculate near-term maturities (within two years) as a percentage of total debt
- Assess the issuer's ability to address maturities given current market conditions
- Note any springing maturities or cross-acceleration triggers

**Recovery Analysis**
- For HY and leveraged credits, present a recovery waterfall:
  - Enterprise value under going-concern and liquidation scenarios
  - Distribution to each tranche by priority
  - Estimated recovery rate for the tranche under analysis
- State the valuation assumptions driving recovery (EBITDA multiple, discount to book value)
- Compare to rating agency recovery assumptions

**Covenant Assessment**
- For maintenance covenants: current compliance levels and projected headroom each quarter
- For incurrence covenants: current capacity under each basket
- Evaluate covenant quality: tight, moderate, or loose relative to leverage
- Identify the most restrictive covenant and the scenario in which it binds
- Assess EBITDA add-back provisions and their real-world impact on compliance

#### Required Checklist Items
- Complete debt stack table: instrument, amount, rate, maturity, ranking, security
- Maturity profile with refinancing risk assessment
- Weighted average cost of debt
- Recovery analysis by tranche (HY/leveraged credits)
- Covenant package summary with current compliance levels
- Covenant headroom calculations (current and projected quarterly)
- EBITDA add-back impact on covenant compliance
- Key permitted baskets and carve-outs (material ones)
- Intercreditor dynamics for multi-tranche structures
- Call protection and refinancing optionality

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Debt stack is complete; covenants are described
- **Strong**: Recovery analysis uses explicit valuation assumptions; covenant headroom is projected quarterly under base and stress cases; add-back impact is quantified; the analysis is oriented around the analyst's tranche

#### Common Mistakes
- Describing the capital structure without quantifying amounts and percentages
- Omitting recovery analysis for sub-investment-grade credits
- Stating that covenants exist without calculating headroom
- Ignoring EBITDA add-backs when assessing covenant compliance
- Failing to address the maturity wall or refinancing risk
- Omitting dollar amounts on the debt stack

### 7. Relative Value

#### Objective
Justify why this specific credit is attractive (or unattractive) relative to alternatives. Relative value is the bridge between fundamental analysis and actionable recommendation. Without it, the memo answers "is this a good credit?" but not "is this a good investment?"

#### Structure
Define the peer group, present the comparison framework, quantify the relative value basis, and explain why the mispricing exists and what will correct it.

#### Analytical Approach

**Peer Group Construction**
- Select four to eight comparable credits based on: industry, rating, leverage, size, and structure
- State the selection rationale — explain why each comp is relevant
- Acknowledge limitations of the comp set

**Quantitative Comparison**
- Spread vs. peer median and range
- Leverage vs. peer median (Total and Secured)
- Spread per turn of leverage (efficiency metric)
- Coverage, FCF yield, and other relevant metrics vs. peers
- Present in a single comparison table for easy scanning

**The Relative Value Argument**
- Quantify the specific basis: "Trading at 425bps, 75bps wide of the 350bps BB healthcare median"
- Explain why the differential exists: rating, size, liquidity, business quality, technical factors, recent events
- Assess whether the differential is justified, excessive, or insufficient
- Identify the catalyst for convergence or divergence

**Historical Context**
- Where does the current spread sit vs. its own historical range?
- What events have driven prior tightening/widening episodes?
- Is the current level reflecting a temporary dislocation or a fundamental repricing?

#### Required Checklist Items
- Peer group of four to eight comps with selection rationale
- Comparison table: spread, leverage, coverage, rating for each peer
- Quantified spread basis vs. peer median
- Leverage-adjusted spread analysis
- Explanation of why the valuation differential exists
- Historical spread range with current positioning
- Catalyst for spread convergence or divergence
- Cross-market comparison where relevant (bonds vs. loans vs. CDS)

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Peer comparison table is provided with spread and leverage metrics
- **Strong**: Quantified basis is clearly stated; the reason for mispricing is identified; a specific catalyst for convergence is articulated with expected timeline

#### Common Mistakes
- Analyzing the credit in isolation without any peer comparison
- Selecting comps that are not genuinely comparable
- Stating "attractive relative to peers" without quantifying the basis
- Ignoring structural differences between the subject and comps (secured vs. unsecured, different maturities)
- Using stale spread data
- Missing peer selection rationale
- No catalyst for value realization

### 8. Key Risks & Mitigants

#### Objective
Identify, prioritize, and contextualize the material risks to the investment thesis, paired with specific mitigants that make the risk acceptable. This section demonstrates analytical balance and intellectual honesty.

#### Structure
Present in paired format: each risk immediately followed by its mitigant. Order by probability-weighted severity (highest first). Use three to six pairs for most memos.

#### Analytical Approach

**Risk Identification**
- Business/operational: revenue concentration, competitive disruption, execution risk
- Financial: leverage trajectory, liquidity adequacy, refinancing risk, covenant compliance
- Industry/macro: cyclicality, regulatory change, input cost volatility
- Structural: subordination, intercreditor dynamics, covenant leakage
- Event: M&A, LBO, shareholder activism, litigation, regulatory action

**Risk Assessment**
- For each risk, assess probability (low/medium/high) and severity (impact on credit metrics if realized)
- Quantify where possible: "A 15% revenue decline would push leverage from 4.5x to 6.0x, breaching the 5.5x maintenance covenant"
- Distinguish between risks that are thesis-relevant (would change the recommendation) and risks that are manageable within the position

**Mitigant Quality**
- Mitigants must be specific and credible — not platitudes
- Strong mitigant: "Customer concentration risk is mitigated by five-year take-or-pay contracts with 95% renewal rate and $15M early termination penalties"
- Weak mitigant: "Management is aware of the risk and is working to diversify"

#### Required Checklist Items
- Three to six risk-mitigant pairs presented in order of severity
- Each risk is specific to this credit (not generic boilerplate)
- Each risk is quantified in terms of impact on credit metrics
- Each mitigant is specific and credible
- Business, financial, structural, and event risk categories all addressed
- Probability and severity assessment for each risk

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Material risks are identified with corresponding mitigants
- **Strong**: Each risk is quantified ("15% revenue decline pushes leverage from 4.5x to 6.0x"); mitigants reference structural protections, contractual terms, or observable data; risks are clearly prioritized

#### Common Mistakes
- Generic risk categories without specificity ("macro headwinds," "competitive pressures")
- Listing risks without mitigants
- Mitigants that are vague or merely restate management aspirations
- Failing to quantify the impact of risk materialization
- Omitting event risks (M&A, LBO, activism)
- Presenting risks in random order rather than by severity
- Missing structural risks (subordination, covenant leakage)

### 9. ESG Considerations

#### Required Checklist Items
- Material environmental factors relevant to this credit
- Social factors: labor, product safety, community impact
- Governance: board independence, compensation alignment, shareholder rights
- ESG rating summary from at least one major provider
- Assessment of ESG impact on credit quality (positive, neutral, negative)
- Sustainability-linked features if applicable (KPIs, step-ups/step-downs)

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: ESG factors are acknowledged with basic assessment
- **Strong**: ESG factors are linked to specific credit risks or strengths with quantified impact where possible; governance assessment addresses capital allocation behavior

#### Common Mistakes
- Governance assessment treated as a box-checking exercise
- Missing link between ESG factors and actual credit impact
- Omitting sustainability-linked bond/loan feature analysis (when present)

---

### 10. Recommendation

#### Objective
Translate the preceding analysis into a clear, actionable investment decision with specific parameters for execution, monitoring, and exit.

#### Structure
Lead with the recommendation statement (bold, unambiguous). Follow with the supporting rationale summarized in two to three sentences. Then provide execution parameters, catalysts, and monitoring triggers.

#### Required Content

**Recommendation Statement**
- Action: Buy, Sell, Hold, Increase, Reduce, or Avoid
- Conviction: High, Medium, or Low
- Must be consistent with the analysis — the recommendation should feel inevitable by the time the reader reaches it

**Execution Parameters**
- Specific instrument (CUSIP, facility, tranche)
- Target entry spread, price, or yield
- Recommended position size (as percentage of portfolio or notional amount)
- Expected hold period

**Catalysts and Timeline**
- Specific, identifiable events expected to drive value realization
- Expected timing for each catalyst
- What will confirm or deny the catalyst thesis

**Risk Management**
- Stop-loss trigger: the spread, price, or fundamental threshold at which the position should be exited or reassessed
- Thesis violation indicators: specific outcomes that would invalidate the investment thesis
- Maximum loss tolerance for the position

**Monitoring Framework**
- Key metrics to track (with frequency)
- Rating agency watch items
- Market-based indicators (spread movement, CDS basis)
- Fundamental milestones (earnings, debt reduction, covenant compliance)
- Next scheduled review date

#### Quality Markers
- **Adequate**: Recommendation is clear with key execution parameters
- **Strong**: All parameters are actionable; catalysts are event-specific with dates; stop-loss triggers are measurable; the monitoring framework specifies exactly what to watch and when

#### Common Mistakes
- Burying the recommendation or hedging it with excessive caveats
- Recommending without specifying size, entry point, or conviction level
- Catalysts that are vague ("positive earnings trajectory") rather than specific ("Q3 earnings expected to show 200bps margin expansion from completed plant closure")
- No stop-loss or thesis violation trigger defined
- Recommendation inconsistent with the analysis (bullish analysis, hold recommendation)
- Missing monitoring triggers — the memo is written and then forgotten

## Pre-Submission Final Check

Before submitting to the Investment Committee, verify the following:

### Data Integrity
- [ ] All financial data is sourced and dated (no stale numbers)
- [ ] Numbers are internally consistent: metrics in the executive summary match the financial analysis section
- [ ] Leverage and coverage calculations can be traced to the underlying financial data
- [ ] Market data (spreads, prices) is as of the memo date or clearly dated otherwise
- [ ] Scenario analysis assumptions are explicitly stated and internally consistent
- [ ] No circular references in scenario outputs (check that stress assumptions do not depend on stress outputs)

### Analytical Consistency
- [ ] The recommendation is consistent with the analysis: a bullish analysis leads to a buy, not a hold
- [ ] Risk section addresses the risks implied by the financial projections
- [ ] Relative value positioning is consistent with the thesis
- [ ] Downside scenario metrics are consistent with stated assumptions
- [ ] Covenant headroom projections match the financial model output

### Completeness
- [ ] All required sections are present for the credit type
- [ ] Every risk has a mitigant; every strength is quantified
- [ ] The peer group is appropriate and the comparison is fair
- [ ] Catalysts have specific timelines
- [ ] Monitoring triggers are defined and measurable
- [ ] ESG section is substantive, not a placeholder

### Presentation
- [ ] Executive summary fits on one page
- [ ] Tables are consistently formatted with clear headers and units
- [ ] Financial figures use consistent units and rounding throughout
- [ ] Abbreviations are defined on first use
- [ ] Sources are cited for third-party data and projections
- [ ] Inline citations include direct source URLs whenever a stable URL exists
- [ ] Document is proofread for errors, typos, and formatting inconsistencies
