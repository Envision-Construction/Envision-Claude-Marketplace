# Memo Templates by Credit Type

This reference provides section-level templates for the four primary credit types. Each template specifies the required sections, the analytical focus within each section, and the key data points that must be addressed. Use the appropriate template based on the asset class of the credit under analysis.

## Citation Standard For All Memo Types

- Include source citations inline throughout the memo, not just in a final notes section
- When a stable direct URL exists, include that URL inline with the citation so source links can be collected later into a consolidated appendix
- Keep locator detail with the citation when available, including page numbers, slide numbers, filing dates, report dates, and access dates
- Prefer canonical primary-source URLs over search results or secondary aggregator links
- If no stable URL exists, keep the citation without a URL rather than inventing one

## 1. Corporate Credit (Investment Grade / High Yield / Leveraged Loan)

Corporate credit memos cover publicly rated or broadly syndicated instruments. The analytical emphasis varies by quality tier: IG memos focus on relative value and spread positioning; HY and leveraged loan memos require deeper fundamental analysis with emphasis on downside protection and recovery.

### Section Template

#### 1.1 Executive Summary
- Issuer name, sector, corporate family rating (Moody's/S&P/Fitch), instrument rating
- Instrument: bond CUSIP or loan facility, coupon/spread, maturity, size, ranking
- Use of proceeds (new issue) or trading context (secondary)
- Two-sentence thesis statement
- Key metrics table: Total Leverage, Secured Leverage, Interest Coverage, FCF/Debt, Liquidity
- Three credit strengths and two key risks in bullet form
- **Recommendation**: Action, conviction, target spread/price, size

#### 1.2 Investment Thesis
- State the thesis in two to four falsifiable bullet points
- Each bullet must reference a quantifiable outcome or observable catalyst
- Include the time horizon for thesis realization
- Identify what would invalidate the thesis

#### 1.3 Business Overview
- Revenue composition by segment, geography, and customer type
- Revenue quality assessment: recurring vs. transactional, contractual vs. discretionary
- Market position and competitive advantages (scale, IP, switching costs, regulatory barriers)
- Customer and supplier concentration analysis (top 10 customer revenue share)
- Management quality and track record (especially capital allocation discipline)
- Ownership structure and alignment of interests

#### 1.4 Industry Analysis
- Industry size, growth rate, and cyclicality profile
- Competitive structure (fragmented vs. consolidated, pricing power dynamics)
- Secular trends: tailwinds and headwinds over the credit horizon
- Regulatory environment and pending changes
- Technology disruption risk assessment
- Where in the cycle: early, mid, late, or downturn

#### 1.5 Financial Analysis
- Three-year historical income statement, cash flow, and balance sheet trends
- Analyst base case projection (minimum three years forward)
- Management case vs. analyst case variance analysis
- Key credit metrics over the projection period:
  - Total Debt / EBITDA and Net Debt / EBITDA
  - EBITDA / Interest Expense (cash and total)
  - FCF / Total Debt
  - Capex as percentage of revenue (maintenance vs. growth)
- Margin analysis: gross, EBITDA, and FCF margins with trend commentary
- Working capital dynamics and seasonality
- Liquidity analysis: cash, revolver availability, near-term maturities, covenant headroom
- Downside scenario: recession case or stress case with key assumptions stated

#### 1.6 Capital Structure & Covenants
- Complete debt stack table: instrument, amount outstanding, rate, maturity, ranking, security
- Maturity profile visualization description (wall chart)
- Weighted average cost of debt
- Recovery analysis by tranche (waterfall methodology for HY/loans)
- Covenant package:
  - Financial maintenance covenants (leveraged loans): levels, step-downs, current headroom
  - Incurrence covenants (HY bonds): restricted payments, debt incurrence, lien baskets
  - Key permitted baskets and carve-outs
  - EBITDA add-back provisions and their impact on reported vs. actual compliance
- Intercreditor dynamics (for multi-tranche structures)
- Call protection and refinancing optionality

#### 1.7 Relative Value
- Peer group definition (four to eight comps) with selection rationale
- Spread comparison: current spread vs. peer median and historical range
- Leverage-adjusted spread analysis (spread per turn of leverage)
- Quality-adjusted assessment: rating, size, liquidity, business quality differentials
- Historical spread chart with key events annotated
- New issue concession analysis (for primary market)
- Cross-market comparison where relevant (bonds vs. loans vs. CDS)
- Specific basis: quantify the spread pickup or discount and explain why it exists

#### 1.8 Key Risks & Mitigants
- Present in paired format: each risk has a corresponding mitigant
- Prioritize by probability-weighted impact
- Required risk categories to address:
  - Business/operational risks
  - Financial risks (leverage, liquidity, refinancing)
  - Industry/macro risks specific to this issuer
  - Structural risks (subordination, covenant leakage)
  - Event risks (M&A, LBO, shareholder activism)
- Quantify downside impact where possible (e.g., "A 200bps margin decline increases leverage from 4.5x to 5.8x")

#### 1.9 ESG Considerations
- Material environmental factors (carbon exposure, regulatory risk, transition costs)
- Social factors (labor practices, product safety, community impact)
- Governance assessment (board independence, executive compensation alignment, shareholder rights)
- Sustainability-linked bond/loan features if applicable
- ESG rating summary from major providers
- Impact on credit quality assessment (positive, neutral, negative)

#### 1.10 Recommendation
- **Action**: Buy / Sell / Hold / Increase / Reduce / Avoid
- **Conviction**: High / Medium / Low
- **Instrument**: Specific CUSIP or facility
- **Target Spread / Price**: Entry level and expected exit level
- **Position Size**: Percentage of portfolio or notional amount
- **Time Horizon**: Expected hold period
- **Catalysts**: Specific events with expected timing
- **Stop-Loss Triggers**: Spread, price, or fundamental levels that invalidate the thesis
- **Monitoring Triggers**: Quarterly metrics, rating actions, or market events to track

## 2. Private Credit / Direct Lending

Private credit memos serve a dual purpose: underwriting assessment for new originations and ongoing surveillance. The analytical focus shifts toward documentation terms, structural protections, and loss-adjusted return analysis, reflecting the illiquid and hold-to-maturity nature of the investment.

### Section Template

#### 2.1 Executive Summary
- Borrower name, sector, sponsor (if applicable)
- Facility: type (unitranche, first lien, second lien, mezzanine), size, spread, floor, OID, maturity
- Sponsor equity contribution and total capitalization
- Transaction context: LBO, refinancing, add-on acquisition, dividend recap
- Key metrics: Total Leverage, Secured Leverage, FCCR, Loan-to-Value
- Two-sentence thesis with return summary (gross yield, expected loss, net return)
- **Recommendation**: Approve / Decline / Approve with modifications

#### 2.2 Deal Overview
- Sponsor profile: fund vintage, track record in sector, equity at risk
- Transaction structure: sources and uses table
- Equity cushion analysis: sponsor equity as percentage of TEV
- Key terms summary: spread, floor, OID, call protection, mandatory amortization
- Syndicate structure (if applicable): hold amount, sell-down strategy
- Fee income: upfront, commitment, amendment/waiver history for existing credits

#### 2.3 Business Overview
- Revenue model and predictability (contractual, subscription, project-based)
- Customer diversification and retention metrics
- Competitive moat assessment for a private company context
- Management team depth and key person risk
- Technology and operational infrastructure maturity
- Historical ownership and transaction history

#### 2.4 Financial Analysis
- Three-year historical and three-year projected financials
- Quality of earnings adjustments: add-backs, run-rate adjustments, pro forma items
- Adjusted EBITDA bridge from reported to underwritten
- Credit metrics: leverage, coverage, FCF conversion, liquidity
- Revenue and margin sensitivity analysis
- Working capital and capex requirements
- Management case, base case, and downside case with clearly stated assumptions
- Breakeven analysis: revenue decline to breach maintenance covenants

#### 2.5 Underwriting Assessment
- Collateral valuation and asset coverage
- Enterprise valuation (multiple approaches): EV/EBITDA, DCF, precedent transactions
- Loan-to-value at entry and projected
- Recovery analysis under stress: liquidation scenario, going-concern scenario
- Comparable transaction terms: leverage, spread, structure benchmarking
- Quality of earnings report findings (if available)
- Insurance coverage adequacy

#### 2.6 Documentation & Covenants
- Financial maintenance covenants: type, level, step-downs, testing frequency
- Covenant headroom at closing and projected
- EBITDA definition: permitted add-backs, caps on synergies and cost savings
- Restricted payments and debt incurrence baskets
- Permitted acquisition and investment baskets
- Change of control provisions
- Reporting requirements and information rights
- Amendment and waiver mechanics
- Comparison to sponsor's precedent documentation

#### 2.7 Return Analysis
- Gross yield: spread + floor benefit + OID amortization + fee income
- Expected loss calculation: probability of default x loss given default
- Net return: gross yield minus expected loss minus cost of funding
- Return comparison to portfolio targets and asset class benchmarks
- Scenario returns: base, upside (early repayment), downside (restructuring)
- IRR sensitivity to prepayment timing
- Risk-adjusted return ranking within current pipeline

#### 2.8 Key Risks & Mitigants
- Sponsor behavior risk (dividend recaps, add-on leverage)
- Customer concentration and revenue fragility
- Management depth and succession
- Integration risk (for acquisition-driven transactions)
- Refinancing risk given maturity and market conditions
- Covenant effectiveness (can the borrower grow into loose covenants?)
- Each risk paired with a specific, credible mitigant

#### 2.9 Recommendation
- **Action**: Approve / Decline / Approve with modifications
- **Hold Amount**: Recommended exposure
- **Key Conditions**: Modifications required for approval (tighter covenants, lower leverage, equity cure limits)
- **Return Assessment**: Expected net return vs. portfolio hurdle rate
- **Monitoring Plan**: Key metrics, frequency of review, early warning indicators
- **Workout Considerations**: Preliminary assessment of recovery options if thesis fails

## 3. Commercial Real Estate (CRE)

CRE memos evaluate property-level credit risk, combining real estate fundamentals with debt structuring. The analytical framework centers on property cash flows, market dynamics, and loan sizing discipline. Physical asset quality and location-specific factors receive greater weight than in corporate analysis.

### Section Template

#### 3.1 Executive Summary
- Property name, type (office, multifamily, industrial, retail, hospitality), location
- Loan: type (senior, mezzanine, construction), size, rate, maturity, LTV, DSCR
- Sponsor/borrower profile and experience
- Key metrics table: In-Place NOI, Stabilized NOI, LTV (as-is/stabilized), DSCR, Debt Yield
- Two-sentence thesis referencing property quality and loan protection
- **Recommendation**: Approve / Decline / Approve with modifications

#### 3.2 Property Overview
- Physical description: size (SF/units), year built/renovated, class (A/B/C), condition
- Site and location assessment: access, visibility, proximity to demand drivers
- Capital expenditure history and planned improvements
- Environmental assessment summary (Phase I/II findings)
- Insurance coverage and natural disaster exposure
- Zoning, entitlements, and regulatory considerations
- Property operating history (three to five years)

#### 3.3 Market & Submarket Analysis
- MSA-level economic indicators: employment, population, GDP growth
- Property-type-specific market data: vacancy, absorption, rent growth, new supply pipeline
- Submarket drill-down: competitive set, micro-location advantages/disadvantages
- Supply pipeline: under construction and planned projects within competitive radius
- Demand drivers specific to the property type and location
- Rent comparison: subject property vs. market and submarket averages
- Cycle positioning: where the market sits in the real estate cycle

#### 3.4 Tenant Analysis
- Rent roll summary: occupancy, WALT (weighted average lease term), average rent per SF
- Top tenant profiles: credit quality, lease terms, renewal probability
- Lease expiration schedule and re-leasing risk assessment
- Tenant industry diversification
- Below-market vs. above-market lease analysis (mark-to-market)
- Tenant improvement and leasing commission obligations
- For single-tenant or highly concentrated: tenant credit deep-dive

#### 3.5 Financial Analysis
- Historical NOI trend (three to five years) with variance analysis
- In-place NOI vs. stabilized NOI bridge
- Revenue assumptions: occupancy trajectory, rental rate growth, concessions
- Operating expense analysis: controllable vs. non-controllable, per-SF benchmarking
- Capital reserve adequacy
- Cash flow projection (loan term): NOI, debt service, cash flow after debt service
- Sensitivity analysis: vacancy shock, rent decline, interest rate change impact on DSCR

#### 3.6 Valuation
- Cap rate analysis: implied cap rate, market cap rate comparables, historical range
- Direct capitalization approach (in-place and stabilized)
- Discounted cash flow valuation with terminal cap rate assumption
- Sales comparable analysis with adjustment grid
- Replacement cost analysis
- Valuation reconciliation and concluded value
- Appraisal summary (if available) and variance to internal valuation

#### 3.7 Loan Sizing & Structure
- Sizing constraints: LTV, DSCR, debt yield — which is the binding constraint?
- Loan terms: rate (fixed/floating), index, spread, maturity, extension options
- Amortization: IO period, amortization schedule, balloon at maturity
- Reserve requirements: interest, taxes, insurance, capex, leasing costs
- Prepayment provisions: lockout, defeasance, yield maintenance
- Recourse provisions and guarantor creditworthiness
- Intercreditor terms (if mezzanine or preferred equity in the stack)
- Structural protections: cash management triggers, sweep mechanisms

#### 3.8 Key Risks & Mitigants
- Lease rollover concentration and re-leasing risk
- Market supply/demand imbalance
- Interest rate risk (floating rate loans)
- Sponsor financial capacity and commitment
- Environmental and physical condition risks
- Refinancing risk at maturity
- Regulatory or zoning risks
- Each risk paired with specific mitigant and quantified impact where possible

#### 3.9 Recommendation
- **Action**: Approve / Decline / Approve with modifications
- **Loan Amount**: Recommended commitment
- **Key Sizing Rationale**: Which constraint drives the size
- **Structural Requirements**: Reserves, covenants, recourse requirements
- **Return Assessment**: Expected yield vs. portfolio targets
- **Monitoring Plan**: NOI tracking, occupancy triggers, site inspection schedule
- **Exit Strategy**: Expected refinancing or payoff path

## 4. Structured Finance (ABS / CLO / Project Finance)

Structured finance memos evaluate cash flow-based securities where credit risk depends on collateral performance, structural features, and credit enhancement rather than a single corporate obligor. The analytical framework emphasizes pool-level statistics, waterfall mechanics, and stress scenario performance.

### Section Template

#### 4.1 Executive Summary
- Transaction name, asset class, issuer/originator/servicer
- Tranche: class, rating, size, coupon/spread, WAL, attachment/detachment points
- Collateral summary: pool size, number of obligors/assets, WAC, WARF
- Credit enhancement: subordination, OC, excess spread, reserve accounts
- Key metric: breakeven default rate, expected loss, yield at pricing
- Two-sentence thesis on collateral quality and structural protection
- **Recommendation**: Buy / Sell / Hold with conviction level

#### 4.2 Transaction Overview
- Originator/sponsor profile and track record
- Servicer assessment: primary and backup servicer, servicing transfer triggers
- Legal structure: issuing entity, bankruptcy remoteness, true sale opinion
- Timeline: closing date, revolving period, amortization period, legal final maturity
- Key transaction parties and their roles
- Regulatory considerations (risk retention, Volcker, NRSRO requirements)

#### 4.3 Collateral / Asset Analysis
- Pool composition: stratification tables by key characteristics
  - ABS: obligor credit score, geography, vintage, loan size, LTV
  - CLO: industry, rating, spread, maturity, obligor concentration
  - Project Finance: technology, offtaker credit, jurisdiction, completion status
- Historical performance data: default rates, loss severity, prepayment speeds, delinquency trends
- Origination standards and underwriting criteria
- Vintage analysis: performance by origination cohort
- Concentration analysis: single obligor, geographic, industry
- Collateral quality trends: improving, stable, or deteriorating

#### 4.4 Structure & Waterfall
- Payment waterfall: priority of payments for interest and principal
- Diversion triggers and their current levels
- Sequential vs. pro rata pay mechanics and trigger flip conditions
- Reinvestment criteria and limitations (CLO/revolving)
- Clean-up call and optional redemption provisions
- Counterparty risk: swap providers, liquidity facilities, GIC providers
- Structural diagram with cash flow priorities

#### 4.5 Credit Enhancement
- Subordination: initial and current levels, target levels
- Overcollateralization: par and interest coverage tests, current cushion
- Excess spread: historical and projected levels under base and stress
- Reserve accounts: initial funding, target balance, release conditions
- Third-party support: wraps, guarantees, letters of credit (if any)
- Total credit enhancement composition and adequacy assessment
- Enhancement relative to historical loss experience and stress scenarios

#### 4.6 Stress Testing
- Base case assumptions: default rate, loss severity, prepayment speed, recovery timing
- Stress scenarios with clearly stated assumptions:
  - Mild stress: 1.5x historical losses
  - Moderate stress: 2.0x historical losses with delayed recoveries
  - Severe stress: 2.5-3.0x historical losses with elevated severity
- Break-even analysis: at what default/loss level does the tranche take principal loss?
- Interest rate stress (floating rate tranches): impact of rate changes on coverage
- Prepayment sensitivity: fast and slow pay scenarios
- Correlation stress (CLO): impact of industry concentration and systematic risk
- Cash flow model outputs under each scenario: principal loss, WAL extension, yield impact

#### 4.7 Relative Value
- Comparable new issue and secondary spread levels by rating and asset class
- Historical spread range for the asset class and rating tier
- Spread pickup relative to comparably rated corporate bonds (structural premium)
- Manager/originator tiering: premium or discount to market based on platform quality
- Seasoning benefit: how does pool performance to date inform relative value?
- Supply/demand technical factors
- Basis to benchmark index (if applicable)

#### 4.8 Key Risks & Mitigants
- Collateral deterioration beyond stress assumptions
- Servicer disruption or replacement risk
- Structural risks: trigger breaches, waterfall disruptions, WAL extension
- Counterparty risks: swap, liquidity, account bank
- Legal and regulatory risks: true sale, consolidation, regulatory change
- Liquidity risk: secondary market depth and price volatility
- Model risk: sensitivity to assumption inputs
- Each risk paired with specific structural protection or mitigant

#### 4.9 Recommendation
- **Action**: Buy / Sell / Hold
- **Conviction**: High / Medium / Low
- **Tranche**: Specific class and rating
- **Target Spread / Price**: Entry level
- **Size**: Notional or percentage of portfolio
- **Return Profile**: Expected yield, WAL, spread duration
- **Key Monitoring Metrics**: Trustee report items to track (delinquencies, OC test cushion, reinvestment quality)
- **Thesis Violation Triggers**: Performance thresholds that would trigger reassessment
