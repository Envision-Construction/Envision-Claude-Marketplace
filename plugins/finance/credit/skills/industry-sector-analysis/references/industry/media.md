# Media Credit Analysis

## Sector Overview

Media, entertainment, and content-driven businesses span **advertising-supported** models (broadcast, digital publishing, AVOD, social/video platforms), **subscription** models (SVOD, pay-TV bundles in transition, premium sports tiers), **affiliate and retransmission-fee** economics (MVPD carriage, network affiliate compensation), and **hybrids** (ad tiers plus subscription, wholesale plus DTC). Structural pressure on **linear television**—viewing shift to on-demand, sports fragmentation, and digital ad share gain—has compressed legacy affiliate and advertising pools while **streaming** platforms compete for subscribers, content rights, and engagement.

Film and studio operations, **content libraries** (licensing and syndication), **music and publishing** (royalties, catalog, streaming mechanicals), and adjacent **gaming / interactive** tie-ins sit on a continuum from **high fixed-cost IP creation** to **distribution and monetization** of third-party or catalog assets. Credit quality often hinges less on a single EBITDA print than on **audience scale**, **pricing power**, **rights obligations**, and **path of cash content investment** relative to amortization and maturities.

### Calibration lens: leverage, audience economics, and earnings quality

**Why media can support different leverage tolerance than generic corporate sectors**

- **Recurring or habitual revenue** (subscriptions, retransmission, long-dated sports or output deals) can stabilize cash flows when churn is controlled and pricing resets are credible—but **audience and advertising** revenue can retrench faster than industrial recurring revenue when the cycle turns.
- **High operating and financial leverage on content** means **scale** and **IP durability** matter: a platform that can spread fixed programming costs across a large base may tolerate higher nominal leverage than a thin distributor with no proprietary rights.
- **Accounting-heavy sectors**: **content amortization**, **participations and residuals**, **minimum guarantees**, and **sports rights** can make **headline EBITDA** and **reported leverage** diverge from **cash available for debt service** and **refinancing flexibility**.

Media therefore warrants **sector-adjusted** interpretation of shared rating bands: start from `references/rating-agency-thresholds.md` and apply the hierarchy in `references/sector-toolkit.md` rather than treating media as a single uniform block.

**When subscriber and audience metrics are more informative than headline EBITDA**

- **Helpful** when the economic engine is **audience monetization** (subs, ARPU, ad impressions, engagement) and **near-term profitability** is depressed by **intentional content investment** or **transition costs** from linear to streaming.
- **More informative than EBITDA** when **subscriber net adds**, **ARPU trajectory**, **ad load and CPM trends**, or **affiliate/retrans revenue per sub** explain **forward cash generation** better than current amortization-loaded earnings.
- **Misleading** if management **changes KPI definitions**, **bundles** obscure underlying trends, or **adjusted EBITDA** adds back **economically recurring** content cash needs, **sports or output minimums**, or **GAAP-only** items that still bind liquidity.

Always pair audience and subscription views with **cash content spend**, **covenant definitions**, **maturity walls**, and **rights-payment schedules**—never substitute a single adjusted ratio for full liquidity and refinancing analysis.

**How content accounting, rights obligations, and amortization can distort debt-capacity analysis**

- **Content amortization** spreads past spend through the P&L; **cash content spend** can lead or lag, so EBITDA can overstate or understate near-term cash available for interest and debt amortization.
- **Sports rights, output deals, and minimum guarantees** create **contractual cash floors** that do not always move in sync with revenue; stress **payment timing** and **escalators**, not only LTM margins.
- **Participations, residuals, and profit participations** can **accelerate** in hit-driven years, compressing **realized margins** after success.
- **Library and IP** can support **downside recovery** when **licensable**, **long-lived**, and **not encumbered**—but **franchise concentration** and **single-title risk** can dominate recovery when hits fade or talent costs spike.

### Cross-sector lens: cable and ad tech

When **broadband utility economics**, **network capex**, and **subscriber churn** drive the credit story, treat those names as telecom-style analyses rather than core media underwritings. When **software workflow** or **infrastructure** (not audience monetization) dominates, use `skills/industry-sector-analysis/references/industry/technology.md`. This file focuses on **content, distribution, and audience economics**.

## Dimension 1: Cash Flow Quality and Predictability

### 1.1 Revenue / Income Visibility

Revenue visibility varies sharply across media sub-segments. Metrics must be combined with **trend**, **definition**, and **cash vs. accrual** discipline.

**Subscription and DTC video / audio**
- **Paid subscribers or members** — Level and **net adds**; distinguish **promotional** vs. **paid**, **bundle-included** vs. **standalone**, and **regional** mix.
- **ARPU / ARPPU** — Average revenue per user or per paying user; separate **price increases**, **mix** (tiering), **foreign exchange**, and **discounting**.
- **Churn and engagement** — Voluntary churn, **viewing hours**, **completion rates** where disclosed; engagement supports **retention** and **ad inventory** in hybrid models.
- **Content spend as % of revenue** and **cash content spend growth** — Compare to **subscriber growth** and **ARPU**; **cash** vs. **amortization** bridge is essential.
- **Average revenue per account (where relevant)** — For multi-user plans, ARPU can mask **account-level** economics.

**Advertising-supported (linear, digital, AVOD, audio)**
- **Advertising revenue** and **ad revenue per user / per hour** — Tie to **CPM**, **fill rate**, **impressions**, **seasonality**, and **macro ad demand**.
- **Audience reach and demographics** — **Share**, **ranking**, **dayparts** (linear); **DAU/MAU** and **session length** (digital)—fragmentation raises **selling costs** and **pricing pressure**.
- **National vs. local** exposure — Local spot and **auto / retail** cyclicality; national **brand** vs. **performance** mix.

**Affiliate, retransmission, and carriage**
- **Affiliate and retrans revenue per sub** — Trends vs. **linear sub decline**; **blackout** and **carriage dispute** risk.
- **Contract renewal calendar** — **Step-ups**, **reverse comp**, and **minimum penetration** clauses.

**Studios, film, and episodic production**
- **Theatrical vs. streaming windowing** — **Box office** (where relevant), **PVOD**, **licensing** fees; **greenlight** cadence and **slate concentration**.
- **Backlog and committed production spend** — **Greenlights**, **order volumes**, **customer** or **platform** concentration for third-party production.

**Libraries, music, and publishing**
- **Catalog revenue stability** — **Streaming** mechanicals and **publishing** performance income; **sync** and **licensing** volatility.
- **Royalty obligations and advances** — **Recoupment** status; **minimum delivery** requirements.

**Revenue and cash flow drivers:**
- **Linear TV decline and streaming transition** — **Cord-cutting** and **viewing migration** reduce legacy **affiliate** and **linear ad** pools; **DTC** investment can **depress** near-term margins while building **scale** or **data** advantages.
- **Ad-supported vs. subscription vs. affiliate-fee models** — **Ads** are **cyclical** and **CPM-sensitive**; **subscriptions** depend on **pricing power** and **churn**; **affiliate/retrans** depends on **MVPD** economics and **contract terms**. **Hybrids** (ad tiers) trade off **ARPU** and **ad yield**.
- **Content spend and rights inflation** — **Sports**, **premium scripted**, and **exclusive** windows drive **bidding** behavior; **scale** buyers may **outspend** smaller peers on **per-sub** economics.
- **Content amortization vs. cash** — Investment cycles in originals can front-load cash relative to smoothed P&L expense; sports and output deals can invert this (cash-heavy upfront).
- **Franchise and IP concentration** — **Sequel** economics, **universe** building, and **tentpole** slates can **concentrate** success risk; **diversified libraries** can **smooth** cash flows when **licensing** is active.
- **Audience fragmentation** — More **services** and **channels** compete for **time**; **CAC** for subscribers and **sales cost** for ad inventory can **rise** without **share** gains.
- **Music and publishing** — **Streaming** growth has supported **catalog** valuations; **royalty** rates, **platform** policy, and **AI-related** usage are evolving **risk factors**.

**Cross-model cash and leverage**
- **Free cash flow after content cash** — Not the same as **EBITDA minus capex** when **working capital** and **rights payments** swing.
- **Net leverage** — Reconcile to **covenant EBITDA**; watch **addbacks** for **content**, **restructuring**, and **non-recurring** production.

### 1.2 Cyclicality and Macro Sensitivity

Media cyclicality varies by revenue model:

- **Low-moderate cyclicality**: Subscription-based models with strong retention; catalog/publishing royalties
- **Moderate cyclicality**: Affiliate and retransmission fees (tied to linear sub trends); blended models
- **High cyclicality**: Advertising revenue (brand budgets cut faster in downturns; CPM compression); hit-driven studio slates; touring and live events

**Advertising cyclicality** — **Macro** softness hits **brand** budgets; **performance** ads may hold up better but still **slow** in recessions; **local** ad tied to **auto**, **retail**, and **political** cycles.

### 1.3 Seasonality and Cash Flow Timing

- **Advertising**: Q4 advertising surge (holiday season); political advertising cycles for broadcast; summer softness in some markets
- **Content production**: Production spending can be lumpy and front-loaded; post-production and release schedules create cash timing gaps
- **Sports rights**: Payment schedules tied to league calendars; escalators at renewal
- **Music/publishing**: Catalog revenue relatively stable; touring and live events seasonal (summer festivals, holiday concerts)
- **Studios**: Theatrical release windows cluster around holidays and summer; streaming release cadence more evenly distributed

### 1.4 Concentration Risk

- **Franchise and content concentration** — Over-reliance on **one series**, **sport**, **talent**, or **channel** creates **renewal** and **cost** risk; **hit-driven** slates increase **earnings volatility**.
- **Customer/platform concentration (studios)** — Orders from few streamers or networks create renewal and repricing risk.
- **Distribution concentration** — Dependence on distributors (MVPDs, app stores, aggregators) for reach and revenue.
- **Roster concentration (music)** — Concentration in a small roster or few superstars raises renewal and behavioral risk.

## Dimension 2: Margin and Cost Structure Resilience

### 2.1 Pricing Power and Rate-Setting Ability

- **Subscription platforms**: Testing pricing power through tier introduction and price increases; churn response is the key test. Scale platforms with differentiated content have more room than niche services.
- **Advertising**: CPMs subject to supply/demand, audience quality, and macro conditions. Performance advertising (search, social) more resilient than brand advertising.
- **Affiliate/retransmission**: Negotiated rates subject to carriage disputes and blackout risk; secular pressure from linear sub declines.
- **Music/publishing**: Royalty rates set by regulation, negotiation, or platform policy; catalog pricing power depends on depth and hit density.

### 2.2 Operating Leverage and Cost Flexibility

- **Streaming platforms**: High operating leverage on content; fixed programming costs spread across subscriber base. Scale advantages are real but content cost inflation can offset them.
- **Broadcast/cable networks**: High fixed-cost structure (programming, talent, distribution infrastructure); margins compress sharply when advertising or affiliate revenue declines.
- **Studios/production**: Project-based cost structure with some flexibility in greenlight cadence; labor costs (talent, crew, guild requirements) create cost floors.
- **Music/publishing**: Relatively lean operating structures; royalty obligations are largely variable. Advance recoupment creates some fixed-cost characteristics.

### 2.3 Input Cost and Inflation Exposure

- **Content costs**: The dominant input for most media businesses. Sports rights inflation has been particularly acute. Premium scripted content costs have escalated with streaming competition. Talent costs (actors, showrunners, athletes) subject to market dynamics and labor agreements.
- **Labor**: Union negotiations and guild actions (writers, actors, directors) can disrupt production and inflate costs. Below-the-line production costs affected by location competition and crew availability.
- **Technology and distribution**: Platform and infrastructure costs for streaming delivery; app store fees (typically 15-30% of subscription revenue) for mobile distribution.
- **Marketing and subscriber acquisition**: CAC for subscribers and sales cost for ad inventory; rising competition increases acquisition costs.

### 2.4 Through-Cycle Margin Stability

- **Subscription models**: Moderate stability when churn is controlled; content investment cycles can create margin volatility even with stable revenue
- **Advertising models**: Significant margin volatility from ad cycles; fixed cost base amplifies revenue swings
- **Affiliate/retransmission**: Relatively stable margins on existing contracts; renewal resets can shift economics
- **Studios**: Hit-driven economics create wide margin ranges; participations and residuals compress margins after success
- **Music/publishing**: Catalog generates stable margins; new releases and advances create volatility

## Dimension 3: Capital Requirements and Asset Profile

### 3.1 Capital Expenditure / Investment Intensity

- **Content spend** is the dominant investment for media businesses and must be analyzed separately from traditional capex. Content spend as % of revenue is the key metric—compare to subscriber growth and ARPU trajectory.
- **Cash content spend vs. amortization**: The gap between cash outlays and P&L expense is critical. Cash content can run ahead of amortization during investment phases or lag during harvesting phases. Build bridges from reported EBITDA to cash available for debt service.
- **Sports and output rights**: Long-term contractual commitments that function like capex with fixed escalators; payment schedules may not align with revenue timing.
- **Traditional capex**: Relatively modest for pure content/distribution businesses; more material for businesses with production facilities, broadcast infrastructure, or theme parks.

### 3.2 Working Capital and Liquidity Demands

- **Content production**: Creates significant working capital demands; production advances, participations, and residuals can be lumpy.
- **Rights payments**: Sports and output deal payment schedules create fixed cash obligations that may not sync with revenue.
- **Advertising receivables**: Seasonal patterns follow advertising cycles; collection risk increases in downturns.
- **Music advances**: Artist and catalog acquisition advances tie up cash; recoupment timeline depends on commercial performance.

### 3.3 Asset Quality, Tangibility, and Liquidation Value

- **Content libraries**: Can support recovery when licensable, long-lived, and unencumbered. Franchise value is subjective. Sports media rights are among the most valuable and defensible content categories.
- **Library stress signals**: Titles with long commercial life, clear chain of title, geographic diversity, and active third-party licensing support recovery when encumbrances are limited. Stress when catalog is stale, heavily encumbered, or dependent on a few buyers facing budget cuts; also when a single franchise represents most exploitable hours or licensing revenue.
- **Music catalogs**: Durable cash-generating assets when rights are clear and deep; valuations depend on streaming growth assumptions and royalty rate trajectory.
- **Broadcast infrastructure and licenses**: FCC licenses and broadcast spectrum have value; physical infrastructure less so as distribution shifts to digital.
- **Brand and IP**: Intangible but can carry significant going-concern value; franchise IP can support sequels, spinoffs, licensing, and merchandising revenue.

### 3.4 Useful Life, Obsolescence, and Stranded Asset Risk

- **Content**: Variable useful life. Evergreen library titles (classic films, iconic series) can generate cash for decades. Topical, trend-driven, or format-dependent content depreciates rapidly. Franchise IP can extend useful life through sequels and spinoffs.
- **Linear distribution infrastructure**: Facing secular obsolescence as viewing shifts to streaming; broadcast spectrum retains value but traditional distribution assets depreciate.
- **Technology and distribution shifts** — **AI** in **content creation** and **discovery algorithms** may **alter** cost curves and **audience** routing; **platform** dependency (app stores, aggregators) affects **margin** and **data**.

## Dimension 4: Structural Protection and Leverage Capacity

### 4.1 Leverage and Coverage Calibration

Media leverage tolerance depends heavily on sub-segment, revenue mix, and content economics. Start from `references/rating-agency-thresholds.md` and apply the hierarchy in `references/sector-toolkit.md`.

**Illustrative Debt/EBITDA conversation (confirm vs. peers, cash bridges, and agency criteria):** diversified media platforms with more stable affiliate, retransmission, and advertising mixes often anchor **BB**-style discussions around roughly **3.5–5.0x**; content-heavy, streaming-led, or concentrated slates may sit higher (**~5.0–7.0x** in **B** contexts) when rights economics and liquidity still support refinancing—always reconcile to **cash** content, **rights** payments, and **covenant** definitions.

### 4.2 Through-Cycle Normalization

- Normalize for content investment cycles and advertising cyclicality; assess mid-cycle advertising revenue and sustainable content spend ratios.
- Headline EBITDA can improve from amortization timing while cash content still pressures liquidity—prioritize cash after content, rights payments, and covenant definitions.
- Sports and output deal economics may not normalize neatly; map contractual escalators and payment schedules against revenue assumptions.

### 4.3 Structural Features and Creditor Protections

- **EBITDA and content adjustments** — Covenants may permit addbacks for development, non-recurring production, or stock-based comp; assess whether cash content still constrains liquidity.
- **Content amortization vs. cash** — Leverage tests on EBITDA can look compliant while cash content and rights payments stress liquidity; build bridges to free cash after content.
- **Permitted indebtedness and liens** — Production financing, receivables facilities, and SPV structures may ring-fence assets; map guarantees and upstream cash flow.
- **Restricted payments and investments** — Dividends, buybacks, and M&A in content can compete with deleveraging; weigh basket capacity against the maturity wall.
- **Carriage and affiliate revenue** — Change-of-control or carriage termination clauses in key agreements can affect continuity assumptions.
- **Sports and output agreements** — Minimum payment schedules may sit outside standard capex; treat as fixed charges in stress.

### 4.4 EBITDA Addback and Adjustment Quality

- **Accounting and cash timing** — Amortization schedules, participations, and capitalized development can make reported leverage look manageable while cash stress builds in downside scenarios.
- **Content amortization vs. cash content spend**: The most important adjustment in media credit analysis. EBITDA calculated after amortization may diverge materially from cash available for debt service.
- **Participations and residuals**: Can accelerate in hit-driven years, compressing realized margins after success. Rising participations as a percentage of revenue on hit content is a margin risk.
- **Non-recurring production charges**: Evaluate whether "non-recurring" production or development charges are truly one-time or reflect ongoing strategic investment.
- **Covenant headroom tightening while management emphasizes "non-cash" content charges**: Reconcile to cash interest, rights cash, and maturities.

## Dimension 5: External and Systemic Risk

### 5.1 Regulatory and Legal Environment

- **Content regulation**: Kids' content rules, sports betting adjacency, advertising standards, and emerging AI content regulations
- **Copyright and IP protection**: Foundation of media value; enforcement varies by jurisdiction and is challenged by digital distribution
- **Labor and union rules**: Guild and union agreements (WGA, SAG-AFTRA, DGA) affect production costs, schedules, and creative flexibility. Strike risk can delay releases and impair windows.
- **FCC and broadcast regulation**: Spectrum allocation, ownership rules, and content standards for broadcast licensees
- **International regulatory variation**: Content quotas, local production requirements, and data localization rules in key markets

### 5.2 Technology Disruption and Secular Displacement

- **Secular decline of linear TV** — **Affiliate** and **linear ad** revenues erode as viewing shifts; **retrans** and **carriage** disputes can cause **blackouts** and **revenue** volatility.
- **Streaming economics and competition** — **Subscriber growth** deceleration in mature markets, **content arms race**, and **price competition** pressure **margins** and **cash** conversion.
- **AI in content creation and discovery** — May alter cost curves for content production, change audience routing and discovery algorithms, and raise IP and licensing questions.
- **Platform dependency** — App store fees, aggregator bundling, and algorithmic discovery affect margin, data ownership, and customer relationship control.
- **Distribution disintermediation** — DTC models bypass traditional distributors but require scale and marketing investment.

### 5.3 Environmental, Climate, and ESG Exposure

- **Production environmental impact**: Location shooting, set construction, and travel create carbon footprint; industry initiatives for sustainable production practices
- **Content-related social considerations**: Misinformation, content moderation, diversity and representation in content
- **Labor practices**: Fair compensation, working conditions, and inclusion in production workforce
- **Limited direct physical climate exposure**: Media businesses generally have low physical climate risk compared to other sectors

### 5.4 Common Underwriting Traps and Red Flags

- Subscriber net adds turning negative or decelerating sharply while content cash spend rises or stays elevated.
- ARPU stagnant or down excluding FX despite list-price increases—often signals mix downshift, bundling dilution, or discounting.
- Advertising revenue down for two consecutive reporting cycles without a macro explanation; CPM or fill deterioration versus peers.
- Content spend consistently above an illustrative ~50–60% of revenue for general-entertainment streaming (confirm versus peers and strategy) without a clear path to margin or free cash flow.
- A single franchise, sport, or show representing disproportionate engagement or revenue without hedged costs, insurance, presales, or a credible successor slate.
- Sports or output rights payments rising faster than revenue; frequent impairments or write-downs on content assets.
- Carriage or affiliate renewals at worse economics, or public disputes with large distributors.
- Management changing KPI emphasis (e.g., engagement only, new subscriber definitions) without transparent bridges.
- Rising participations and residuals as a percentage of revenue on hit content—can compress margins after success.
- Covenant headroom tightening while management emphasizes "non-cash" content charges—reconcile to cash interest, rights cash, and maturities.

## Sub-Segments: Key Distinctions

| Sub-segment | Economic character | Typical credit emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Broadcast and cable networks** | Affiliate, retrans, ad; linear decline | Carriage terms, sports rights, cost structure, DTC pivot |
| **SVOD / DTC streaming** | Scale, content spend, sub economics | Net adds, ARPU, churn, cash content vs. amortization |
| **AVOD / FAST / ad-supported streaming** | Ad yield and inventory | CPM, engagement, ad load, macro ad cycle |
| **Studios and film / episodic production** | Project and slate risk, windowing | Greenlights, concentration, customer/platform reliance |
| **Music and publishing** | Royalties, catalog, advances | Recoupment, platform policy, concentration of hits |
| **Sports rights aggregators** | Long-dated fixed costs | Rights cost vs. distribution revenue, renewal risk |
| **Interactive / gaming-adjacent media** | Hit-driven IP and audience monetization | Release pipeline, franchise concentration, platform fees |

**Broadcast / cable networks** — Affiliate and retransmission fees can feel recurring but face secular pressure; sports rights can stabilize ratings at high fixed cost.

**SVOD / DTC** — Scale and IP matter; subscriber trends and ARPU often lead EBITDA in importance during heavy investment phases.

**AVOD / FAST** — More exposed to ad cycles; engagement drives ad inventory and pricing power.

**Studios / production** — Hit risk and customer concentration (streamers, networks) can dominate; backlog quality and diversity matter.

**Music / publishing** — Catalog can support recovery when rights are clear and durable; advances and recoupment tie cash to artist and roster performance.

### Media Sub-Sector Deep Dives

For **rating-level leverage and coverage bands**, start from `references/rating-agency-thresholds.md` and apply **sector-adjusted interpretation** using `references/sector-toolkit.md`. Benchmarks below are **illustrative**—not independent rating tables.

#### Subscription video and DTC platforms

##### Key metrics

| Metric | Definition | Credit relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Paid subscribers / net adds | Ending subs and period net change | Demand and competitive position; watch definition changes |
| ARPU | Revenue per user or per paying account | Pricing power, tier mix, discounting, regional blend |
| Churn / engagement | Voluntary churn, hours viewed, completion | Retention and future pricing room; ad tier engagement if hybrid |
| Cash content spend | Cash outlays for licensed and produced content | Often the binding cash flow driver versus amortization |
| Content spend % of revenue | Total content costs vs. sales | Strategy-dependent; compare to net adds and ARPU trajectory |
| Free cash flow | CFO less capex, after content cash where separable | Ultimate deleveraging capacity |

##### Subscriber trends vs. ARPU (credit interpretation)

- A healthy credit story often combines positive net adds (or a stable large base) with stable-to-rising ARPU from tier mix and pricing power—not only discount-driven volume.
- Stress signals include net adds turning negative while ARPU rises only from forced price increases that may accelerate churn, or ARPU compression from mix shift to lower tiers and promotional bundles.
- Headline EBITDA can improve from amortization timing while cash content still pressures liquidity—prioritize cash after content, rights payments, and covenant definitions.

##### Cash content spend versus amortization (credit interpretation)

- **Lead/lag** — Cash production and licensing outlays can run ahead of amortization when a platform invests in originals; conversely, amortization can continue on a declining revenue base after spend is cut, flatteringly extending EBITDA while cash has already turned.
- **Sports and output deals** — Long-term rights may front-load cash versus revenue; GAAP expense recognition may not align with when cash leaves the building—map **contractual** payment schedules beside reported content expense.
- **Covenant bridge** — Build a reconciliation from reported EBITDA to cash available for debt service: working capital in production, participations, and minimum guarantees often sit below the line in casual EBITDA talk but above the line for liquidity.

##### Scale advantages in content production and distribution

- Larger platforms can amortize marketing, technology, and overhead across a bigger subscriber or viewer base and may negotiate better terms on some licenses or co-productions.
- Scale does not guarantee returns if content cost inflation outpaces pricing, or if audience fragments faster than share consolidates.
- Vertical integration (studio plus distribution) can capture more margin but concentrates execution risk on one balance sheet.

##### Red flags (SVOD / DTC)

- Persistent negative free cash flow with no credible timeline before material maturities.
- Content spend growing faster than revenue and subscribers for multiple periods without a clear strategic explanation.
- Rising churn and declining engagement metrics where disclosed.

#### Ad-supported media (linear, digital publishing, AVOD)

##### Key metrics

| Metric | Credit relevance |
|---|---|
| Ad revenue and yield | CPM, fill, seasonality, macro exposure |
| Reach and share | Competitive position in fragmented markets |
| Cost per engagement / sales efficiency | Monetization efficiency vs. peers |
| Operating leverage | Fixed cost base vs. ad volatility |

**Advertising cyclicality** — Stress revenue and EBITDA at ad-market trough assumptions; digital properties may still see CPM pressure in recessions.

##### Red flags

- Ad revenue underperforming nominal GDP or industry growth for multiple quarters without audience or share gains.
- Rising traffic acquisition cost or content cost without matching RPM, ARPU, or yield improvement.

#### Film, studio, and episodic production

- **Slate concentration** — Single-title dependence without mitigants (insurance, presales, co-financing) raises binary outcomes.
- **Customer concentration** — Orders from few streamers or networks create renewal and repricing risk.
- **Windowing** — Theatrical, PVOD, and SVOD exclusivity windows affect timing of cash versus recognition and hit risk.

#### Content libraries, licensing, and syndication

##### When library value and IP strength help the downside case

- **Licensable depth** — A deep catalog with renewable syndication and international licensing can generate cash when new production is curtailed, supporting liquidity through a downturn if rights are not tied up in exclusive output deals elsewhere.
- **Franchise durability** — Proven multi-film or multi-season IP can reduce greenlight risk for sequels and spinoffs; credit recovery improves when downside scenarios still assume continuing exploitation of core IP.
- **Encumbrances matter** — Libraries help less when titles are pledged to lenders, subject to complex profit participations, or distributed through platforms that can renegotiate or delist content.

##### Library stress signals

- Titles with long commercial life, clear chain of title, geographic diversity, and active third-party licensing support recovery analysis when encumbrances are limited.
- Stress when the catalog is stale, heavily encumbered, or dependent on a few buyers facing budget cuts; also when a single franchise represents most exploitable hours or licensing revenue.

#### Music and publishing (credit lens)

- Catalog and publishing can produce durable cash streams with different cyclicality than advertising or subscription video—but advances, acquisitions, and royalty disputes can consume liquidity.
- Concentration in a small roster or few superstars raises renewal and behavioral risk.

#### Sports rights and live events

- Rights costs are often fixed and escalating; revenue depends on affiliate, advertising, and DTC take rates.
- Credit analysis should map payment schedules, escalators, and outcomes under ratings stress, carriage loss, or partial blackouts.

### Cross-sub-sector comparison

| Attribute | SVOD / DTC | Ad-supported | Studios / production | Music / publishing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue visibility | Moderate (subs), churn risk | Low-moderate (cyclical ads) | Project and backlog driven | Mix of catalog stability and new releases |
| Cyclicality | Moderate (consumer, pricing) | Higher (ad cycle) | High (hit-driven) | Moderate (catalog) vs. high (touring/advances) |
| Cash vs. EBITDA gap | Often large (content cash) | Smaller in pure digital; WC can swing | Can be large around releases | Advances and recoupment drive gaps |
| Main downside risk | Content spend vs. subs | Ad recession | Slate / customer concentration | Roster, royalty policy, M&A integration |

### Analyst prompts

Use these prompts before concluding that a media credit deserves a favorable sector adjustment:

- Is headline EBITDA aligned with cash content, rights payments, and liquidity, or only with accounting conventions?
- Do subscriber and audience metrics support the leverage story, or is growth buying time with unsustainable spend?
- How concentrated is success on franchises, sports, talent, or platform customers?
- What happens to affiliate and ad revenue in a macro ad recession or accelerated linear decline?
- Are covenant definitions hiding fixed content obligations or liquidity drains?

## Rating Agency Focus Areas

**S&P (illustrative themes)** — Often emphasizes scale, diversity of revenue, content cost trajectory, and leverage through-cycle; may scrutinize EBITDA adjustments for non-cash items versus cash content obligations. Sports and long-term rights may receive explicit liquidity and refinancing scrutiny.

**Moody's (illustrative themes)** — Frequently stresses free cash flow, interest coverage on a cash basis, contractual commitments, and liquidity headroom when content spend is discretionary in name but strategic in practice. Carriage and affiliate concentration may factor into competitive position.

**Shared tension** — Agencies balance recurring-looking subscription revenue against churn, pricing, and content renewal risk; advertising exposure triggers cyclical frameworks. Debates often center on how much nominal leverage studios or scaled platforms can carry versus single-asset or pure-play linear names—verify against current criteria and issuer-specific reports.
