## Income Statement Analysis

### Revenue Trends: Organic vs. Acquisition-Driven

Start at the top line and assess quality of growth.

**Organic growth** = reported revenue growth minus contribution from acquisitions minus impact of divestitures.

**Analysis approach:**
1. Identify large acquisitions and divestitures during period
2. Remove acquired revenue for less-than-full-year contribution
3. Calculate organic growth % = (Revenue growth - acquired growth) / prior year revenue
4. Assess business fundamentals: pricing, volume, market share

**Credit assessment:** Organic growth from market share gain or price increases is more sustainable than growth from acquisitions (integration risk).

### Gross Margin Analysis

```
Gross Margin = (Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue
```

**Assessment:**
- **Trend:** Is margin stable, improving, or deteriorating?
- **Peers:** How does margin compare to competitors?
- **Inflation sensitivity:** Does COGS move with input prices (commodity exposure)?
- **Operating leverage:** Higher gross margin provides more cushion for EBITDA decline

**Example:** Company A: Revenue $100M, COGS $40M → Gross Margin = 60%
Company B: Revenue $100M, COGS $60M → Gross Margin = 40%
Company A has more buffer if revenue declines.

### EBITDA Margin: The Key Profitability Metric

```
EBITDA Margin = EBITDA / Revenue
```

EBITDA margin reveals how much operating profit the company generates per dollar of revenue.

**Interpretation:**
- **Trend matters most:** Is margin expanding or contracting?
- **Cycle matters:** Are we at peak demand or trough?
- **Leverage matters:** At stressed EBITDA (down 20%), what margin applies?
- **Maturity matters:** Mature, competitive businesses have lower margins; niche/monopolistic have higher

**Example:**
- Company: Revenue $500M, Adjusted EBITDA $100M → 20% margin
- If revenue drops 20% to $400M and margin contracts to 18%
- Stressed EBITDA = $400M × 18% = $72M
- Vs. non-stressed base case EBITDA of $100M
- This is important for debt service coverage analysis (see credit-ratio-analysis skill)

### Operating Leverage: Sensitivity to Revenue Changes

Operating leverage describes how sensitive EBITDA is to revenue changes.

**Higher operating leverage businesses:**
- Have high gross margins and low incremental operating costs
- EBITDA grows/falls faster than revenue grows/falls
- Example: Software/SaaS, consulting, brand-driven CPG

**Lower operating leverage businesses:**
- Have lower gross margins or high fixed operating costs
- EBITDA growth tracks closer to revenue growth
- Example: Commodity manufacturing, distribution

**Implication for credit:** High operating leverage is good in good times (EBITDA grows fast) but dangerous in downturns (EBITDA falls fast). Assess the downside scenario carefully.

**Calculation example:**
- Base case: Revenue $100M, EBITDA $20M (20% margin)
- 10% revenue decline: Revenue $90M
  - With high operating leverage: EBITDA $17M (19% margin) ← only 5% fall
  - With low operating leverage: EBITDA $15M (17% margin) ← 25% fall
- Stress case scenario impacts leverage ratios and covenant cushion

### Interest Expense: Cash vs. Non-Cash Components

Interest expense on the income statement has two components:

**Cash interest:**
- Paid in cash currently (coupon)
- Flows through P&L and must be covered by FCF
- Example: $50M of 8% senior secured debt = $4M annual cash interest

**Non-cash interest (PIK):**
- Paid-In-Kind: accrues but is added to principal
- Flows through P&L but not cash
- Example: $20M of 12% PIK toggle note = $2.4M annual accrual (no cash paid now)
- Increases total debt over time and creates refinancing risk

**Credit assessment:**
- High PIK interest lowers near-term cash interest burden but increases refinancing risk
- In stress scenario, PIK may reset to full cash; includes in FCF waterfall
- Track total debt growth from PIK accrual

### One-Time vs. Recurring Items: Requires Judgment

Line items labeled "one-time" or "non-recurring" require critical assessment.

**Red flags:**
- "One-time" items appear every year (check last 3 years of filings)
- Charges increase in downturns (e.g., restructuring charges spike as business weakens)
- Management includes conflicting adjustments (can't add back "transition costs" if also claiming "cost synergies")

**Assessment framework:**
1. Is the item truly non-recurring or cyclical?
2. Will it recur in a stress scenario?
3. What does repeated inclusion suggest about management quality?
4. How do covenant definitions address this item?
