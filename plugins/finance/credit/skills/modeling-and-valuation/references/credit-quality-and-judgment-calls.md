## Credit Quality Assessment: Improving vs Deteriorating

### Improving Credit (Rising Rating Potential)

**Indicators:**
- Declining leverage (Debt / EBITDA trending down)
- Expanding coverage (Interest coverage improving)
- Positive FCF trends (FCF growing, deleveraging accelerating)
- Revenue growth (organic or market-driven)
- Margin expansion (EBITDA margins improving)
- Reduced refinancing risk (debt maturity ladder extended)
- Improving peer comparison (outperforming industry)

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Technology Services
Year 1 Metrics            Year 2 Metrics        Direction
Leverage: 4.5x            4.2x                  Improving
Coverage: 2.8x            3.2x                  Improving
EBITDA Margin: 18%        20%                   Expanding
Revenue Growth: 3%        8%                    Accelerating
Trends:                   Positive across board

Credit assessment: Improving. Likely to be upgraded over 12-18 months
if trends continue.
```

---

### Deteriorating Credit (Falling Rating Potential)

**Indicators:**
- Rising leverage (Debt / EBITDA trending up)
- Declining coverage (Interest coverage declining)
- Negative FCF trends (FCF shrinking, refinancing needed)
- Revenue decline (same-store sales down, market share loss)
- Margin compression (EBITDA margins squeezed)
- Covenant violations or waivers
- Refinancing risk (maturity walls, capital needs)
- Deteriorating peer comparison (lagging competitors)

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Specialty Retail
Year 1 Metrics            Year 2 Metrics        Direction
Leverage: 4.2x            4.9x                  Deteriorating
Coverage: 3.5x            2.8x                  Deteriorating
EBITDA Margin: 22%        19%                   Contracting
Revenue Growth: 5%        -2%                   Declining
FCF / Debt: 15%           8%                    Slowing
Trends:                   Negative across board

Credit assessment: Deteriorating. Risk of downgrade. May need
covenant waiver or restructuring if trends continue.
```

## Credit Analysis Judgment Calls

### When to Be Skeptical of Adjustments

1. **Same adjustments every period**: If "restructuring charges" appear in 3 consecutive years, they're not one-time.
2. **Adjustments larger in stress**: If adjustments jump 50% as business weakens, they mask declining fundamentals.
3. **Conflicting add-backs**: Don't add back "transition costs" while claiming "synergy savings" from same transaction.
4. **Non-arm's-length amounts**: Sponsor fees, related-party transactions, management comp—verify they're competitive.
5. **Opaque language**: If management calls something "normalized" without detail, ask for specifics.

### When FCF Waterfall Doesn't Match CFO

**Common reconciliation issues:**

| FCF Calculation | Typical Variance | Explanation |
|----------------|-----------------|----|
| EBITDA - CapEx | Overstates FCF | Ignores interest, taxes, WC changes |
| CFO - CapEx | Should match | If not, investigate P&L vs. cash timing |
| EBITDA method vs. CFO method | Large difference | Suggests large working capital, deferrals, or other items |

**Action:** Reconcile and understand. Large variance suggests non-recurring items or accounting issues.

### When to Worry About Liquidity

1. **Available liquidity < 12-month maturities**: No buffer for business disruption
2. **Covenant-restricted revolver access**: If leverage is near ceiling, revolver may not be fully available
3. **Large one-time cash needs**: Refinancings, CapEx peaks, acquisition closes, licensing payments
4. **Seasonal businesses without revolver**: Seasonal peak funding needs cannot be met
5. **Negative FCF in base case**: Not sustainable; indicates structural imbalance
