## Bond & Loan Fundamentals

### Core Financing Types

#### Revolver (Revolving Credit Facility)

- Committed line that can be borrowed, repaid, and redrawn during the availability period.
- Usually supports liquidity, working capital, letters of credit, and general corporate purposes.
- Often carries a commitment fee on undrawn amounts and a floating rate when drawn.
- Commonly sits at the front of the capital structure and may include the tightest ongoing financial covenant package.

#### Term Loan

- Non-revolving funded debt with a stated maturity and repayment profile.
- Usually floating rate and typically paired with a credit agreement rather than a bond indenture.
- May amortize over time or rely heavily on a maturity balloon.

Common distinctions:

| Instrument | Typical Investor Base | Structural Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Term Loan A** | Relationship banks and similar lenders | Higher amortization, stronger bank-style hold profile |
| **Term Loan B** | Institutional loan investors | Lower amortization, wider distribution, capital-markets style placement |

#### High-Yield Bond

- Usually fixed-rate debt governed by an indenture.
- Often has longer-dated capital structure positioning than revolving bank debt.
- Documentation is typically incurrence-based rather than maintenance-covenant-based.

### Rate and Pricing Mechanics

#### Floating-Rate Loans

Floating-rate loans are generally quoted as:

`Reference Rate + Applicable Margin`

Key components:

- **Reference rate**: The benchmark base rate for the instrument.
- **Applicable margin / spread**: The contractual credit spread over the reference rate.
- **Floor**: Minimum base-rate assumption used if the benchmark falls below a stated level.
- **Pricing grid**: Margin that can step up or down based on leverage or another negotiated test.

#### Fixed-Rate Bonds

Fixed-rate bonds generally pay a stated coupon set at issuance. Their market value then moves based on:

- changes in risk-free rates;
- changes in credit spread; and
- changes in embedded option value, if callable or putable.

### Amortization, Prepayment, and Call Protection

- **Amortization**: Scheduled principal repayment over the life of the instrument.
- **Bullet maturity**: Little or no principal repayment before maturity, with a larger final repayment.
- **Mandatory prepayment**: Required paydown from specified proceeds such as asset sales, insurance recoveries, or excess cash flow.
- **Optional prepayment**: Borrower-elected repayment, often with fewer restrictions in the loan market than in callable bond structures.
- **Call protection**: Economic or legal protections that compensate lenders if the issuer refinances early.

### Documentation Framework

#### Credit Agreement (Loans)

The core governing document for loans generally covers:

- facility size and commitment mechanics;
- interest and fee calculations;
- covenants, baskets, and events of default;
- lender voting thresholds; and
- transfer, assignment, and agency mechanics.

#### Prospectus / Offering Memorandum (Bonds)

The bond marketing and disclosure document generally covers:

- issuer and business description;
- use of proceeds;
- risk factors;
- summary terms of the securities; and
- a description of the governing indenture.

#### Indenture (Bonds)

The indenture is the operative legal agreement for bondholders. It generally governs:

- payment terms;
- covenants and events of default;
- amendment thresholds;
- lien and guarantee package, if any; and
- trustee rights and remedies.

### Bond vs. Loan Orientation

| Feature | Loans | Bonds |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical rate style** | Floating-rate | Fixed-rate is more common |
| **Primary governing document** | Credit agreement | Indenture plus offering materials |
| **Control structure** | Lender vote mechanics through the agreement and agent | Bondholder rights through the trustee and indenture |
| **Covenant style** | Maintenance and/or incurrence depending on market and product | Usually incurrence-based in HY |
| **Prepayment behavior** | Often more flexible | Often defined by call schedule or make-whole mechanics |

### Transfer Mechanics

#### Assignment

- Transfer of legal ownership of a loan position to a new lender.
- The assignee becomes a lender of record and usually receives direct voting rights.

#### Participation

- Transfer of economic exposure while the original lender remains lender of record.
- The participant typically has fewer direct control rights.

Assignments matter more when voting control, amendment rights, and lender-of-record status are important.

### Underwriting Relevance

The instrument choice matters because it shapes:

- where the debt sits in the capital structure;
- which investor base must clear the transaction;
- how flexible the borrower is after closing; and
- what remedies or controls creditors retain when performance weakens.
