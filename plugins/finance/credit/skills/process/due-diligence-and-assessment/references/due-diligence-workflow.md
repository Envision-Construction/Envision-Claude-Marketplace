# Data Room Review and Due Diligence Workflow

## Philosophy: Time Is Limited, Prioritize Ruthlessly

Credit due diligence operates under severe time constraints. In a broadly syndicated leveraged loan, the commitment deadline may be 2-3 weeks after data room access. In a club deal or private credit transaction, the timeline may be 4-6 weeks. In either case, the data room may contain hundreds or thousands of documents, and the analyst cannot review every page.

The objective is not completeness — it is sufficiency. The analyst must identify the 20% of documents that contain 80% of the credit-relevant information, review those thoroughly, and make a principled decision about the remaining material. Missing a critical risk factor is a failure of prioritization, not a failure of effort.

## Standard Workflow

### Phase 1: Initial Screen

**CIM / Teaser Review**
Start with the Confidential Information Memorandum (CIM) or investment teaser. This provides the arranger's presentation of the credit story and establishes the baseline narrative. Read critically — the CIM is a marketing document prepared by the sell-side. It will emphasize strengths and minimize weaknesses.

Key CIM elements to extract:
- Business description and competitive positioning
- Historical and projected financials (usually 3 years historical, 5 years projected)
- Capital structure and terms summary
- Management team overview
- Industry overview and market size claims
- Key risk factors (often buried in the back or appendix)

**Initial Screen Decision**
After CIM review, make a preliminary assessment: does this credit meet basic portfolio criteria (sector, size, leverage, rating, return)? If not, decline early and save the diligence time for other opportunities. Roughly 30-50% of opportunities should be screened out at this stage.

### Phase 2: Financial Deep Dive

**Financial Statements**
- 3 years of audited financial statements — read the full audit opinion, notes, and MD&A, not just the income statement
- Quarterly or monthly management accounts for the trailing 12-18 months
- Compare management accounts to audited financials — material differences indicate potential reporting quality issues

**Quality of Earnings Report**
- The QoE is the single most important third-party document in the data room
- Focus on: EBITDA adjustments, working capital normalization, pro forma adjustments, one-time items
- Challenge management addbacks — are they truly non-recurring? Would you give credit for these adjustments?
- See the third-party report assessment reference for detailed QoE evaluation guidance

**Financial Model / Projections**
- Review management's base case projections and the arranger's projections (they may differ)
- Build or update an independent financial model — do not rely solely on management projections
- Stress test revenue, margins, and capex independently
- Focus on free cash flow generation and deleveraging trajectory

**Working Capital Analysis**
- Detailed working capital analysis (DSO, DIO, DPO trends)
- Seasonality patterns — does the business have peak working capital needs that strain liquidity?
- One-time working capital items that inflate or deflate the normalized level

### Phase 3: Legal and Structural Review

**Credit Agreement / Indenture**
- Read the full credit agreement (or at minimum, the definitions section, financial covenants, restricted payments, incremental facilities, events of default, and amendments sections)
- Identify covenant structure: maintenance vs incurrence, testing frequency, EBITDA definition
- Assess restricted payment capacity — how much can the sponsor extract?
- Review incremental facility provisions — how much additional debt can be layered in?
- Check for anti-LME protections (uptier, dropdown restrictions)

**Intercreditor Agreement (if applicable)**
- Priority of claims and lien subordination
- Waterfall provisions in enforcement
- Standstill periods and voting thresholds
- Purchase option mechanics

**Security Documents**
- Scope of collateral — all assets or limited?
- Guarantor coverage — is the guarantor structure comprehensive or are material subsidiaries excluded?
- Jurisdictional considerations for international borrowers

### Phase 4: Business and Commercial

**Customer Analysis**
- Revenue concentration — top 10 customers by percentage
- Customer retention rates and contract terms
- Customer credit quality (are they paying on time? Are any in financial distress?)
- Contract renewal schedule — are material contracts up for renewal during the loan term?

**Supplier and Vendor Analysis**
- Key supplier concentration and relationships
- Supply chain vulnerability assessment
- Raw material cost exposure and hedging
- Alternative supplier availability

**Market Studies and Competitive Analysis**
- Independent market studies (read critically — these are often optimistic)
- Competitive landscape — market share, barriers to entry, technology risk
- Regulatory environment and outlook

### Phase 5: Risk and Compliance

**Legal Due Diligence**
- Litigation summary — identify cases with material quantifiable exposure
- Regulatory compliance status — any pending investigations or consent orders?
- IP protection — are key patents/trademarks adequately protected?
- Contract assignment — will change of control trigger contract terminations?

**Environmental**
- Phase I Environmental Site Assessment for each material property
- Recognized Environmental Conditions (RECs) and their estimated remediation cost
- Regulatory compliance history (EPA, state agencies)
- PFAS and emerging contaminant exposure

**Insurance**
- Coverage summary and adequacy assessment
- Key exclusions and sub-limits
- Business interruption coverage and adequacy
- D&O, cyber, and environmental insurance

**Tax**
- Tax structure and efficiency
- NOL carryforwards and their usability post-transaction (Section 382 limitations)
- Transfer pricing risks for international operations
- Sales tax, property tax, and other tax compliance

### Phase 6: Management and Qualitative

**Management Presentation**
- If a management presentation is scheduled, prepare specific questions based on diligence findings
- Focus on areas where the data room has gaps or where the financial narrative is unclear
- Assess management credibility, industry knowledge, and candor

**Employee and Culture**
- Key personnel and retention risk
- Labor agreements, union contracts, pension obligations
- Employee turnover data and trends

## Time Allocation Guidance

Spend most diligence time where the downside case can change fastest:

- Financial quality, cash conversion, and adjustment credibility usually come first.
- Legal and structural review moves up quickly when documentation leakage or creditor-rights risk is material.
- Business and commercial work matters most when concentration, customer durability, supplier dependence, or market disruption drive the thesis.
- Environmental, tax, insurance, and HR work should take priority when the sector or fact pattern makes those issues potentially loss-defining.

## Working with Bankers: What to Ask For

Standard data rooms often exclude material information. Key items to request if not provided:
- Monthly (not just quarterly) financial data for the trailing 24 months
- Management's internal budget for the current year
- Customer-level revenue detail (not just top 10 percentages)
- Historical covenant compliance certificates (for refinancings)
- Detailed EBITDA addback support with underlying documentation
- Capex breakdown by maintenance vs growth, with support for categorization
- Working capital detail by component (AR aging, inventory aging, AP aging)
- Lender presentation or management presentation deck
- Draft or final credit agreement (not just the term sheet)

Be specific in requests. "Please provide customer detail" is less effective than "Please provide annual revenue by customer for the top 20 customers for the trailing three fiscal years, including customer sector and contract expiration dates."

## Dealing with Incomplete Data Rooms

### "Good Enough" vs Dealbreaker Gaps
- **Good enough**: Missing minor items (IT assessment, minor subsidiary financials, historical insurance certificates) — these create information risk but are rarely dealbreakers
- **Dealbreaker gaps**: Missing QoE report, missing recent financial statements, refusal to provide customer detail, missing credit agreement draft — these prevent fundamental credit assessment

### Adjusting for Incomplete Information
- Where data is incomplete, widen the range of scenarios in the financial model
- Document information gaps explicitly in the credit memo
- Consider whether incomplete disclosure itself is a red flag (is the sponsor or company hiding something?)
- Apply a spread premium for information risk — an opaque credit should price wider than a transparent one

## Confidentiality Considerations

### NDA Scope
- Review the NDA before accessing the data room — understand restrictions on use, sharing, and retention
- Material Non-Public Information (MNPI): once you access the data room, you may be restricted from trading in the issuer's public securities
- Wall-crossing procedures for firms that manage both public and private credit portfolios
- Data room access logs are maintained — treat all materials with appropriate confidentiality

## Deal-Type Variations

### LBO / New Acquisition
Full diligence process as described above. Most comprehensive review required because the buyer (sponsor) and the management team may be relatively unknown to the lender. Emphasis on QoE validation, business quality assessment, and sponsor evaluation.

### Refinancing
Shorter process — the credit has an existing track record. Focus on: performance vs original underwriting, credit agreement comparison (old vs new terms), reason for refinancing (deleveraging vs opportunistic vs distressed). Historical covenant compliance certificates are critical.

### Add-On Acquisition
All LBO items PLUS target company diligence, synergy analysis, integration plan, combined entity pro forma, and updated leverage analysis. Key question: does the add-on improve or dilute the credit profile of the platform?

### Distressed / Restructuring
Different diligence focus: emphasis on liquidity analysis, recovery valuation, creditor rights, documentation provisions (voting thresholds, assignment mechanics, anti-LME protections), and restructuring feasibility. Legal diligence is paramount.

### CRE
Property-centric diligence: rent roll, lease abstracts, property condition report, Phase I environmental, appraisal, market study. Financial diligence focuses on NOI stability and DSCR rather than EBITDA and leverage. See document request lists for CRE-specific requirements.

### Priority Indicators
- **Critical**: Must review before making a credit decision. Absence is a potential dealbreaker.
- **Important**: Should review; absence is a notable information gap but not necessarily a dealbreaker.

### Information Gap Tracking

When documents are missing or incomplete, record:
1. **Document**: What is missing
2. **Criticality**: Critical / Important / Nice-to-Have
3. **Requested**: Date requested, from whom
4. **Alternative**: Can the analysis be completed without it? What assumptions must be made?
5. **Resolution**: Date received, or decision to proceed without it
