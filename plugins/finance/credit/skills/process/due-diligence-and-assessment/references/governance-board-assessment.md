# Governance & Board Assessment

Framework for evaluating corporate governance and board quality as credit risk factors, with particular focus on sponsor-controlled and leveraged companies.

## Board Composition Assessment

### Composition Metrics

| Factor | Stronger Governance | Weaker Governance |
|---|---|---|
| Independence Ratio | >50% independent directors | Board controlled by sponsor/management with no independent voice |
| Industry Expertise | Multiple directors with relevant operating experience | Directors without sector knowledge |
| Financial Expertise | At least 1 director qualifies as "financial expert" (SEC definition) | No financial expertise on board |
| Board Size | 7-11 members (adequate oversight without diffusion) | <5 (too small for effective committees) or >15 (unwieldy) |
| Tenure Diversity | Mix of newer and experienced directors | Entirely long-tenured (>10 years) or entirely new |
| Board Refreshment | Regular rotation with succession planning | No turnover in 5+ years |

### Director Quality Assessment

| Indicator | Positive Signal | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Active Participation | Attends >90% of meetings, serves on committees | Frequent absences, passive participation |
| Relevant Experience | Operating role in same or adjacent industry | "Professional director" with no operational background |
| Number of Boards | Serves on 1-3 boards (adequate time commitment) | Serves on 5+ boards (overextended) |
| Conflict of Interest | No material business relationships with company | Consulting, legal, or business relationships with company |
| Restructuring Experience | Has overseen a credit stress period or restructuring | No experience managing through financial difficulty |
| Investment | Meaningful personal investment in company | No personal financial stake |

## Committee Structure

### Audit Committee

| Assessment Area | Best Practice | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | All independent; financial expert as chair | Non-independent members; no financial expert |
| Scope | Financial reporting, internal controls, risk management, compliance | Narrow scope limited to financial reporting only |
| Meeting Frequency | Quarterly minimum; additional as needed | Less than quarterly; no ad-hoc meetings for material issues |
| External Auditor Relationship | Committee selects/retains auditor; approves fees; reviews independence | Management controls auditor relationship |
| Whistleblower Oversight | Committee oversees whistleblower program | No whistleblower program or committee oversight |

### Compensation Committee

| Assessment Area | Credit-Relevant Focus | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Incentive Alignment | Metrics tied to credit-relevant outcomes (FCF, deleveraging, ROIC) | Metrics tied solely to equity value (stock price, EBITDA multiple) |
| Payout Caps | Reasonable cap on variable compensation | Uncapped bonuses with aggressive growth targets |
| Clawback Provisions | Compensation recoverable if results restated or misconduct discovered | No clawback provisions |
| Change of Control | Reasonable severance (1-2x base + bonus) | Excessive golden parachutes (>3x) |
| Retention | Vesting schedules that align with debt maturity | Immediate vesting or short-term horizon |

## Auditor Assessment

### Auditor Quality Indicators

| Factor | Higher Quality | Lower Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Firm | Big Four (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC) | Regional/local firm for large or complex company |
| Tenure | 5-15 years (established relationship without excessive familiarity) | First year (learning curve) or >20 years (independence concern) |
| Opinion | Unqualified/clean opinion | Qualified opinion, going concern language, or emphasis of matter |
| Restatements | No restatement history | Prior restatements, particularly revenue or asset-related |
| Fee Structure | Audit fees > non-audit fees from same firm | Non-audit fees exceed audit fees (independence concern) |

### Auditor Transition Red Flags

| Event | Potential Concern | Investigation Required |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary auditor change (company-initiated) | Disagreement on accounting treatment | Review 8-K disclosure of auditor change |
| Auditor resignation | Auditor concerned about company practices | HIGH priority — review reasons carefully |
| Downgrade from Big Four to regional | Cost reduction or Big Four unwilling to continue | Assess whether quality concern or legitimate cost management |
| Auditor change coincident with restatement | Accounting irregularity suspected | Review restatement scope and auditor dispute |

## Governance Red Flags

### Critical Red Flags

| Red Flag | Description | Credit Severity |
|---|---|---|
| No independent directors | Board entirely controlled by sponsor/management | HIGH — no independent oversight of creditor-relevant decisions |
| No audit committee | Or audit committee without financial expertise | HIGH — financial reporting quality unverified |
| Combined CEO/Chairman | Without lead independent director | MEDIUM-HIGH — concentrated authority |
| Related-party transactions | Material transactions without independent review/approval | HIGH — potential self-dealing |
| Dual-class share structure | Controlling shareholder with disproportionate voting rights | MEDIUM — limited accountability |
| Excessive executive compensation | Compensation disconnected from performance | MEDIUM — misaligned incentives |
| Lack of risk oversight | No formal risk committee or risk management framework | MEDIUM — may not identify emerging risks |

### Documentation Red Flags

| Red Flag | Signal | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Proxy statement lacks detail on director qualifications | Governance is performative, not substantive | Request board member bios; assess directly |
| No code of ethics/conduct disclosed | Weak compliance culture | Flag as governance concern |
| Board meeting frequency <4x/year | Insufficient oversight | Flag as governance concern |
| No annual board self-evaluation | No accountability mechanism | Note in governance assessment |

## Credit-Specific Governance Concerns

### Key Questions for Credit Analysts

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Has the board overseen a credit stress period or restructuring? | Boards without stress experience may make poor decisions when creditor interests diverge from equity |
| Do independent directors have access to management presentations and financial data? | Information asymmetry between sponsor-appointed and independent directors can undermine oversight |
| Is there a formal process for board review of material credit agreement amendments? | Amendments affect creditor rights; board should formally approve |
| Does the board review and approve related-party transactions independently? | Related-party transactions are a primary mechanism for value extraction |
| Is there a dedicated risk committee (separate from audit)? | Risk committee provides focused oversight of credit-relevant risks |

### Board Behavior Under Financial Stress

| Phase | Typical Board Behavior | Credit Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Early stress (leverage rising) | Focus on operational improvement and cost reduction | Generally positive for creditors |
| Moderate stress (covenant pressure) | Consider amendment requests, evaluate strategic alternatives | Mixed — amendment terms matter |
| Severe stress (equity impaired) | Sponsor influence may diminish; independent directors gain voice | Potentially positive if independent directors prioritize going concern |
| Distress (restructuring likely) | Board fiduciary duty shifts from shareholders toward all stakeholders | Generally positive for creditors (zone of insolvency) |

## Sponsor-Controlled Board Assessment

### PE Sponsor Board Dynamics

| Phase | Sponsor Behavior | Creditor Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Post-acquisition (Year 1-2) | Active oversight, value creation plan execution | LOW — interests aligned (protecting investment) |
| Growth phase (Year 2-4) | May pursue add-on acquisitions, expansion capex | MEDIUM — execution risk on growth strategy |
| Value extraction window | Dividend recaps, management fees, sale-leasebacks | HIGH — cash leaving the business |
| Pre-exit (Year 4-7) | Focus on EBITDA maximization for exit multiple | MEDIUM — short-term focus may defer maintenance |
| Equity underwater | Reduced engagement, limited follow-on support | HIGH — sponsor may disengage; board may lack independent leadership |

### Assessing Sponsor-Board Independence

| Indicator | Independent Influence Present | Sponsor-Dominated |
|---|---|---|
| Independent director appointment | Separate nomination process or creditor consent right | Sponsor appoints all directors |
| Board information flow | Independent directors receive same information as sponsor directors | Information filtered through sponsor-appointed management |
| Advisory independence | Board retains independent legal/financial advisors | Relies on sponsor's advisors |
| Compensation decisions | Independent compensation committee | Sponsor determines management compensation |
| Strategic decisions | Independent directors have meaningful vote on material transactions | Sponsor controls all strategic decisions |
