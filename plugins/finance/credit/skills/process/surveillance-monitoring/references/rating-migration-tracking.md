# Rating Migration Tracking Methodology

Rating actions are one input into surveillance, not the surveillance process itself. They are most useful when interpreted as externally published views on deterioration, stabilization, or improvement and then mapped back to internal thesis, market signals, and structural risk.

> Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current rating-event escalation cutoffs and `references/data-pipeline-specification.md` for current monitoring cadence.

## Rating Action Types

Across agencies, the labels differ slightly, but the surveillance meaning is broadly consistent:

| Action Type | Surveillance Meaning |
|---|---|
| Upgrade | Positive external confirmation; reassess upside and relative value |
| Downgrade | Negative external confirmation; reassess downside and required governance response |
| Positive outlook / positive watch | Constructive directional signal, but not yet a realized upgrade |
| Negative outlook / negative watch | Warning that deterioration is now visible to the agency |
| Affirmation | Confirms status quo, sometimes after uncertainty |
| Withdrawal | Removes external coverage and increases reliance on internal monitoring |

Key distinctions:
- outlooks are directional and medium-term
- watch or review placements signal a nearer-term rating event
- multi-notch actions imply more acute change than ordinary one-notch drift
- split ratings should be treated conservatively for risk and carefully for relative value

## How to Use Ratings Well

- Treat rating actions as confirmation or contradiction of your internal view.
- Do not wait for agencies to tell you a credit is weak if market and fundamental evidence already says so.
- Do not overreact to a single agency action without understanding the rationale, committee language, and sector context.
- Pay special attention when rating actions arrive after a long period of market underperformance; the agency may be confirming what the market already priced.

## Leading Indicators of Downgrades

Downgrades are often preceded by a cluster of signals:
- persistent spread or CDS widening
- rising leverage or weaker cash generation
- covenant cushion erosion
- liquidity pressure or revolver dependence
- management or auditor disruption
- negative peer developments in the same sector
- credit watch or review placement

Interpretation guidance:
- market-based signals are earlier but noisier
- fundamental deterioration is slower but usually more durable
- clustering matters more than any single indicator
- rapid deterioration matters more than slow drift

## Escalation Integration

Rating events should map into the normal surveillance framework rather than sit in a separate process:
- an outlook change usually prompts heightened monitoring
- a downgrade should trigger an explicit thesis refresh
- a severe downgrade or move toward default territory should force action framing, not passive observation
- a withdrawal should increase reliance on internal monitoring and disclosure of reduced external coverage

## Fallen Angel and Rising Star Review

Crossing the investment-grade / high-yield boundary matters because the technical buyer base changes, not just because the rating label changes.

### Fallen Angel Risk

Elevated fallen-angel risk exists when several of the following appear together:
- lowest-investment-grade rating level with negative outlook or watch
- split ratings with one agency already below investment grade
- weakening leverage, coverage, or free cash flow
- acquisition-driven or event-driven pressure
- sector-wide downgrade trend
- weakening market access

Key implication:
- the downgrade can create forced selling, weaker liquidity, and wider spreads beyond the purely fundamental move.

### Rising Star Potential

A potential rising star usually combines:
- highest high-yield rating level
- positive outlook or positive watch
- deleveraging and stronger free cash flow
- reduced refinancing risk
- improving business quality or scale

Key implication:
- spread tightening and a broader buyer base may arrive before the formal upgrade.

## Portfolio Use

At the portfolio level, rating migration review should answer:
- are downgrades or negative outlooks clustering in a sector, sponsor group, or vintage
- is internal risk drifting faster than agency ratings suggest
- which names sit near important mandate, vehicle, or index boundaries
- where technical selling or buying could amplify price movement

Useful portfolio outputs include:
- net upgrades versus downgrades
- watch or negative-outlook exposure
- boundary-crossing exposure such as fallen-angel candidates
- concentration of deeply stressed ratings in vehicles with structural limits
