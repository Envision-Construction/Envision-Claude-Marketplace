# Quarterly Credit Review Process

The quarterly review is the standard operating review for a performing or mildly stressed credit. Its purpose is to refresh the evidence, compare actual performance to the original underwriting case, and decide whether the thesis, risk posture, or required action has changed.

> Use `references/data-pipeline-specification.md` for current timing expectations and `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current escalation cutoffs.

## Standard Review Workflow

### 1. Gather the Evidence

Collect the minimum set of inputs needed to test the thesis:
- current financial statements or management reporting
- covenant compliance information
- current market pricing and rating actions
- management commentary, filings, and major news flow
- relevant sector and peer context

### 2. Refresh Core Metrics

Update the metrics that matter most for repayment capacity:
- revenue, EBITDA, margins, and cash flow
- leverage, coverage, liquidity, and debt service burden
- covenant status and headroom
- price, spread, and relative-value positioning

Do not stop at the number itself. Ask:
- is the change temporary or structural
- was it already anticipated in the model
- does it alter forward projections or only quarter-to-quarter timing

### 3. Reconcile Covenant Reality

The covenant review should answer:
- is the borrower currently in compliance
- is cushion expanding, stable, or compressing
- what happens if current operating trends continue
- are covenant-defined earnings diverging materially from economic earnings

Repeated dependence on aggressive addbacks, recurring "non-recurring" adjustments, or amendment-driven flexibility should be treated as surveillance signals, not merely technical footnotes.

### 4. Interpret Market Signals

Use market data to test whether the external market agrees with the internal view:
- are price and spread moves issuer-specific or market-wide
- is the name underperforming peers with similar leverage and sector exposure
- is there a divergence between fundamentals and trading levels that needs explanation

For illiquid positions, stale marks and sparse trading should be disclosed rather than over-interpreted.

### 5. Review Events and Qualitative Change

Screen for developments that could alter repayment capacity, optionality, or governance:
- management turnover
- acquisitions, divestitures, or strategic shifts
- litigation or regulatory developments
- customer, supplier, or tenant changes
- sponsor actions, amendment requests, or refinancing activity

### 6. Re-Test the Thesis

The thesis check is the center of the review:
1. What were the original reasons to own the credit?
2. Which of those reasons still hold?
3. What changed, and is the change temporary, cyclical, structural, or governance-related?
4. What would change the recommendation before the next cycle?

If the thesis no longer holds, the review should move from monitoring to action framing.

## Suggested Output Structure

### Credit Snapshot
- instrument, maturity, position relevance, and current status

### Metric Bridge
- prior period versus current period versus underwriting case

### Key Developments
- the few changes that matter most, not a full news transcript

### Covenant and Liquidity View
- current compliance, trend, and what the forward path implies

### Thesis Status
- intact, pressured, or broken

### Action Recommendation
- maintain, watchlist, reduce, hedge, exit, or gather more information

### Forward Monitoring Plan
- what must be watched before the next review and why

## Escalation Standard

Escalation should occur when:
- current evidence implies the thesis is no longer the right base case
- multiple warning signals cluster across financials, market data, and qualitative developments
- covenant, liquidity, rating, or structural stress is becoming action-relevant
- portfolio, vehicle, or governance considerations now matter as much as issuer fundamentals

The quarterly review should therefore end with a decision question, not just a summary of data.
