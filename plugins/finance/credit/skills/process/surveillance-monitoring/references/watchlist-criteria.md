# Watchlist Criteria and Management Process

The watchlist is the operating bridge between routine monitoring and formal escalation. A credit belongs on the watchlist when the analyst can no longer describe it as "performing normally," even if the position has not yet reached the point of forced action.

> Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current numeric watchlist and escalation thresholds.

## When to Add a Credit to the Watchlist

Add a name when one or more of the following is true:
- the thesis is drifting and requires enhanced monitoring
- the credit is still financeable, but the downside case is gaining probability
- a qualitative event introduces uncertainty that normal quarterly review would underreact to
- a structural, covenant, documentation, or refinancing issue now matters to the investment case
- the market is signaling concern that internal work has not yet fully explained

The watchlist should be early enough to be useful. It is not only for names already in severe trouble.

## Watchlist Categories

### Improving
- The credit was stressed, but the original issue is being worked through constructively.
- Evidence should show real repair, not just one better quarter or a temporary market bounce.

### Stable Watch
- The key issue is unresolved, but not clearly worsening.
- The purpose of this category is to keep uncertainty visible and documented.

### Deteriorating
- The thesis is weakening in a way that now requires explicit action evaluation.
- The analyst should be preparing for reduce, hedge, engage, or exit discussions.

### Imminent Risk
- The position is approaching a breach, default path, restructuring, or similarly acute event.
- Monitoring becomes event-driven and recovery-aware.

## Watchlist Documentation Standard

### At Addition
- identify the trigger
- explain why routine monitoring is no longer sufficient
- state the current thesis view
- define what would move the credit toward improvement or toward further escalation

### While on Watchlist
- keep a short written record of what changed each cycle
- update the thesis status, not just the metrics
- record whether action options are becoming more urgent
- note any governance, trading, or legal follow-ups

### At Removal
- document why the original concern is resolved or no longer relevant
- show why improvement is durable rather than anecdotal
- note any ongoing conditions if the name is removed but not fully normalized

## Removal Principles

A name should come off the watchlist only when:
- the trigger has genuinely resolved or reversed
- the thesis is again the best base case
- the improvement is durable enough to survive normal scrutiny

A name should not be removed simply because:
- one quarter looked better
- spreads bounced with the market
- management gave optimistic guidance without evidence
- an accounting or addback presentation made metrics look cleaner than economics justify

## Link to Governance

The watchlist is not separate from escalation. It should make the next governance step easier by showing:
- what changed
- how the risk is trending
- what decision may soon be required
