# Asset-Class Surveillance Lenses

This file consolidates the four asset-class-specific surveillance lenses into a single reference. Each section covers the monitoring priorities, escalation questions, and review structure for its asset class.

## Corporate Credit Surveillance

Corporate surveillance focuses on whether the issuer can keep converting enterprise value into debt service and refinancing access. The most important question is not whether one quarter was weak, but whether the issuer's earnings power, liquidity, and capital-structure flexibility remain consistent with repayment.

> Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current quantitative cutoffs.

### Core Surveillance Lens

#### Financial Performance
Track:
- revenue and EBITDA versus the underwriting case
- margin trajectory and operating leverage
- free cash flow conversion and working-capital behavior
- capex intensity and whether cash generation is being deferred rather than solved

#### Leverage, Coverage, and Liquidity
Track:
- total leverage and how it is changing
- secured leverage where structural subordination matters
- interest or fixed-charge coverage
- revolver usage, cash availability, and debt maturity profile

Interpretation guidance:
- leverage caused by weaker EBITDA is usually more concerning than leverage caused by deliberate value-accretive deployment of debt
- refinancing risk should be judged by market access and debt capacity, not maturity date alone

#### Covenant and Structural Position
Track:
- maintenance-covenant cushion where relevant
- forward compliance under base and downside cases
- incremental debt capacity, basket usage, and amendment history
- whether documentation flexibility is increasing the issuer's ability to move risk onto existing lenders

#### Market and External Signals
Track:
- relative price and spread behavior
- CDS where available
- rating actions and watch placements
- peer underperformance or sector-wide stress

### Corporate-Specific Escalation Questions

When a corporate credit weakens, the analyst should be able to answer:
- is this a normal cyclical miss or a broken operating model
- is liquidity a temporary bridge issue or the beginning of a refinancing problem
- does the sponsor or management team have both willingness and capacity to stabilize the situation
- is documentation becoming more important than earnings because amendment, priming, or liability management risk is rising

### Suggested Review Structure

1. Financial update versus underwriting case
2. Liquidity and covenant path
3. Capital-structure or documentation changes
4. Sector and peer context
5. Market signal interpretation
6. Thesis status and action recommendation

## CRE Credit Surveillance

CRE surveillance is collateral-first and cash-flow-first. The central question is whether the property can still support debt service and refinance or sale takeout under realistic in-place conditions rather than sponsor pro forma assumptions.

> Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current quantitative cutoffs and `references/market-benchmarks.md` when current cap-rate or pricing context matters.

### Core Surveillance Lens

#### Property Cash Flow
Track:
- NOI versus underwriting and recent history
- occupancy, collections, concessions, and leasing velocity
- how much of current performance is in-place versus still dependent on business-plan execution
- recurring capital needs that reduce true debt service capacity

#### Lease Rollover and Tenant Risk
Track:
- exposure to near-term rollover
- concentration to major tenants
- tenant credit quality and non-renewal risk
- downtime, releasing cost, and rent reset risk

#### Loan and Collateral Protection
Track:
- DSCR and debt yield in the context of current in-place cash flow
- mark-to-market collateral value and refinanceability
- reserve adequacy, including interest and capital reserves where relevant
- whether the loan is becoming dependent on sponsor support, extension, or amendment rather than ordinary takeout

#### Market Context
Track:
- submarket vacancy and rent direction
- competing new supply
- cap-rate movement and valuation liquidity
- asset-level obsolescence or property-type stress

### CRE-Specific Escalation Questions

When a CRE loan weakens, the analyst should ask:
- is the issue temporary leasing friction or a property-level impairment
- is the borrower losing time or losing value
- can the asset still refinance without depending on optimistic assumptions
- are reserves and sponsor support buying time toward stabilization or merely delaying an eventual restructuring

### Suggested Review Structure

1. In-place property performance
2. Tenant and rollover update
3. Loan compliance and reserve position
4. Market and valuation update
5. Refinanceability assessment
6. Thesis status and action recommendation

## Private Credit Surveillance

Private credit surveillance differs from broadly syndicated monitoring because the lender usually has tighter covenants, better information rights, and a closer relationship with both management and sponsor. That additional access should produce earlier detection, not just more reporting.

> Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current quantitative cutoffs. For BDC-held private credit positions, also use `skills/private-credit-middle-market/references/bdc-regulatory.md` to assess leverage, qualifying-asset, and distribution implications.

### Core Surveillance Lens

#### Maintenance Covenant Discipline
Private credit surveillance should focus on:
- headroom on maintenance tests
- how step-downs or tightening tests change forward risk
- whether covenant-defined earnings are drifting away from real operating economics
- whether recurring amendments or waivers are resetting the original deal rather than merely accommodating timing noise

#### Information Rights Quality
Track not only the content of reporting but the quality of access:
- timeliness of financial delivery
- completeness and reliability of management information
- willingness to discuss weak performance openly
- whether the lender learns material events from the borrower or from outside sources

Deterioration in reporting quality is itself a surveillance signal.

#### Sponsor Behavior
Sponsor-backed private credit requires close attention to:
- dividend or recap behavior
- pace and financing of add-on acquisitions
- willingness to support the borrower with equity or operational intervention
- whether the fund lifecycle creates pressure to extract value rather than protect the credit

#### PIK, Fair Value, and Non-Cash Performance
Private credit can hide weakness through non-cash economics. Watch for:
- rising PIK reliance
- accretion of balances without equivalent enterprise-value support
- fair-value drift that is not yet visible in headline company metrics
- repeated valuation support based more on model assumption than executable market reality

### Amendment and Waiver Interpretation

Treat amendment activity as a pattern, not a single event:
- one amendment may reflect ordinary flexibility
- repeated amendments often indicate that the original underwriting case is no longer the right base case
- equity cures can buy time, but they can also confirm that operating performance is below plan
- covenant resets and documentation loosening should be analyzed as risk transfer, not just relationship maintenance

### Suggested Review Structure

1. Performance versus underwriting case
2. Maintenance covenant path and forward compliance
3. Reporting quality and information-rights usage
4. Sponsor behavior and support assessment
5. Fair value, PIK, and unrealized impairment signals
6. Documentation changes and amendment history
7. Portfolio and vehicle implications
8. Thesis status and action recommendation

## Structured Finance Surveillance

Structured finance surveillance is structure-first. The central question is whether collateral performance, trigger-test cushion, and deal mechanics still protect the tranche or financing position as originally expected.

> Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` for current quantitative cutoffs. Where CLO mechanics affect required action, use the governing deal documents and `skills/securitization-and-clos/references/clo-structure-and-economics.md`.

### Core Surveillance Lens

#### Structural Protection
Track:
- test cushion rather than just pass/fail status
- whether cash-flow diversion, reserve trapping, or early amortization risk is rising
- whether credit enhancement is building, stable, or eroding
- whether optionality is narrowing because the deal is becoming structurally constrained

#### Collateral Quality
Track:
- migration in collateral quality
- delinquency, default, and recovery behavior
- concentration drift by issuer, geography, vintage, or asset type
- whether current collateral behavior remains consistent with the deal's original assumptions

#### Manager or Servicer Performance
Track:
- whether the manager or servicer is behaving consistently with mandate and documentation
- reinvestment quality, workout effectiveness, and trading discipline where relevant
- whether reporting quality or servicing behavior is masking deterioration rather than resolving it

#### Product-Specific Focus

For CLOs:
- OC and IC cushion
- CCC migration and par behavior
- manager behavior and reinvestment quality

For ABS and RMBS:
- delinquency pipeline
- cumulative loss performance
- prepayment behavior and trigger interactions

For CMBS:
- special servicing migration
- property-level stress in major loans
- maturity default and appraisal-reduction dynamics

### Structured-Finance Escalation Questions

When a structured product weakens, ask:
- is the issue collateral deterioration, structural leakage, or both
- how much cushion remains before cash flow or principal behavior changes materially
- is the servicer or manager improving the path or worsening it
- does the tranche still behave like the original underwriting case, or has the attachment point effectively moved closer

### Suggested Review Structure

1. Pool or collateral performance trend
2. Structural-test and credit-enhancement position
3. Concentration and migration update
4. Manager or servicer assessment
5. Cash-flow implications for the held security
6. Market liquidity and relative-value view
7. Recommendation and required escalation
