## Part 1: Equity Analysis for Debt Investors

### Why Equity Matters to Debt Investors

Debt investors must understand equity because it:
- **Drives asset valuation**: Market perception of the company's equity directly affects the assets backing your bonds
- **Signals financing options**: Healthy equity means management has refinancing flexibility; distressed equity signals constrained options
- **Provides a cushion**: The equity cushion protects debt holders—it's what equity holders lose before bondholders take a hit

### Enterprise Value Framework

**Enterprise Value (TEV) = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash (+ Preferred Stock if applicable)**

This single metric connects equity and debt fundamentals:
- Market Cap: what the market pays for equity
- Total Debt: all interest-bearing liabilities (bonds, loans, leases)
- Cash: reducing TEV by cash reflects that it can be used to pay down debt

### Leverage Ratios and the Equity Cushion

**TEV/EBITDA**: The total enterprise value relative to earnings. This is the valuation multiple the market assigns to the entire enterprise.

**Net Debt/EBITDA**: The leverage of the actual debt, accounting for cash on the balance sheet.

**The key insight**: The gap between these two ratios is the equity cushion—how much of the enterprise value goes to equity holders versus debt holders.

**TEV Cushion Formula:**
```
TEV Cushion (%) = (TEV/EBITDA - Net Debt/EBITDA) / (TEV/EBITDA) × 100
```

Higher TEV cushion = more of the enterprise value belongs to equity holders = safer for bondholders because there's more equity to be wiped out before debt is impaired.

### Cross-Industry Comparison: Why Leverage Alone Doesn't Tell the Story

**Example: Cable vs. Chemical Company**

- **Cable Company**: 2.4x net debt / EBITDA, but 7.2x TEV/EBITDA multiple
  - TEV Cushion = (7.2 - 2.4) / 7.2 = 4.8 / 7.2 = **67% cushion** (or stated as 303% if calculated as 4.8 ÷ 1.6)
  - High multiple = market values the business, so equity cushion is substantial

- **Chemical Company**: 4.7x net debt / EBITDA, and only 6.3x TEV/EBITDA multiple
  - TEV Cushion = (6.3 - 4.7) / 6.3 = 1.6 / 6.3 = **25% cushion** (or stated as 136% if calculated as 1.6 ÷ 1.6)
  - Lower multiple = market is less optimistic, so equity cushion shrinks despite being on a "per turn" basis

**The lesson**: A chemical company at 4.7x net debt can be riskier than a cable company at 2.4x net debt if the market values cable companies at a much higher multiple.

### Factors Affecting Equity Valuation (and thus debt safety)

1. **Float**: Insiders holding shares vs public float affects control premium and execution risk
2. **Takeover probability**: If acquisition is likely, equity gets a control premium and refinancing risk drops
3. **Operator quality**: Good operators attract premium valuations; poor operators trade at discounts
4. **Competition**: Fragmented markets support higher valuations; consolidated markets may trade cheaper
5. **Capital allocation**: Aggressive dividends and buybacks reduce equity cushion (capital leaves the company)
6. **Leverage discount**: High-leverage companies trade at lower multiples than stable peers—equity investors demand higher returns for risk

### Control Premium

When an acquirer seeks control, they typically pay a premium to the market price. This premium reflects:
- Synergy value the buyer can extract
- Cost of assembling shareholders to agree
- Competition from other bidders

Monitor equity prices for unusual moves suggesting change-of-control activity.

### Monitoring Equities

**For portfolio credit positions, track equities by:**

1. **Group by peer set**: Compare the company's equity performance vs its competitors
2. **Watch for unusual volume/price moves**: Could signal upcoming news (earnings, financing, M&A)
3. **Earnings season signals**: Revenue and margin trends often precede covenant or liquidity issues
4. **Dividend/buyback activity**: Capital returns reduce the equity cushion; suspension signals trouble
5. **Insider trading**: Unusual insider selling can precede negative announcements
