## Participation Agreements — Rights, Limitations, and Use Cases

### What a Participation Is
A **participation** transfers economics without transferring lender-of-record status. The seller remains the contractual lender under the credit agreement, while the buyer receives rights only through the seller.

### Participation vs. Assignment — Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | Assignment | Participation |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **Legal status** | Direct lender under credit agreement | Unsecured creditor of seller |
| **Lender of record** | Buyer | Seller |
| **Agent register** | Buyer appears | Seller remains |
| **Voting rights** | Direct rights under the credit agreement | Typically limited or indirect |
| **Consent rights** | Amendment consent | No direct consent rights |
| **Enforcement** | Direct against borrower | Through seller only |
| **Credit risk** | Borrower only | Borrower + Seller |
| **Documentation** | Assignment agreement | Participation agreement |
| **Unfunded commitments** | Often transfers with the position | May remain with seller unless expressly transferred |

### When Participations Are Used
Participations are most useful when direct assignment is impractical or undesirable, including when:

- Consent or eligibility issues block an assignment.
- The buyer wants economics but not the full legal lender role.
- Unfunded commitments or other obligations make direct transfer unattractive.
- Regulatory, operational, or timing considerations favor a contractual economic transfer instead of a formal assignment.

### LSTA Standard Terms and Conditions for Participations
Participation documentation usually addresses:

- How cash received by the seller is passed through to the participant.
- Which votes or amendment instructions the participant can direct.
- What notices and information the seller must forward.
- How defaults, prepayments, and expenses are allocated.
- What happens if the seller itself becomes insolvent or defaults on its obligations to the participant.

### The Double Credit Risk Problem
The central trade-off is that the participant takes two layers of risk:

- The underlying borrower may default.
- The seller may fail to perform, become insolvent, or otherwise break the contractual chain between borrower cash flow and participant recovery.

That extra counterparty layer is why participations should not be treated as economically identical to assignments.

### Detailed Comparison and Election Mechanics

#### Economic Rights Comparison

**Assignment**:
- Buyer receives borrower's entire payment stream for assigned portion
- Buyer bears all credit risk from borrower
- Buyer's recovery depends on borrower performance and collateral value
- Buyer has security interest in collateral (if secured loan)

**Participation**:
- Buyer receives seller's payment stream (seller obligated to pass through all borrower payments)
- Buyer bears credit risk from borrower AND from seller (seller counterparty risk)
- Buyer's recovery depends on borrower performance, collateral value, AND seller solvency
- Buyer has no direct claim on collateral

#### Voting and Control Rights

**Assignment**:
- Buyer has full voting rights on ALL matters
- Buyer can consent or withhold consent on amendments, waivers, enforcement decisions
- Buyer can direct administrative agent (e.g., on acceleration, release of collateral)
- Buyer participates in lender meetings and voting on credit decisions

**Participation**:
- Buyer has limited voting rights
- Seller obligated to vote buyer's instructions on "money terms": principal amount, interest rate, maturity date, collateral releases
- Seller retains discretion on other matters: covenant waivers, amendment of operational covenants, enforcement decisions
- Buyer receives notice but no vote on non-money matters

#### Enforcement and Default Management

**Assignment**:
- Buyer can take enforcement action against borrower (even without seller agreement)
- Buyer can demand acceleration, foreclosure, or claim setoff
- Buyer can negotiate directly with borrower on workouts
- Buyer's interests and seller's interests are aligned (both hold direct claims)

**Participation**:
- Buyer must work through seller
- Seller controls enforcement decisions; buyer cannot unilaterally demand acceleration
- If seller and buyer disagree on enforcement strategy, seller's position prevails
- Buyer's interests may diverge from seller's (e.g., seller willing to waive default; buyer wants enforcement)

#### How the Election Works in Trade Confirmations

Trade confirmations include language electing assignment or participation:

**Assignment language**:
> "Buyer elects to purchase a direct assignment of Seller's interest in the Loan facility. This transaction shall be evidenced by an Assignment and Assumption in form attached as Exhibit A, executed and delivered at or before Settlement Date."

**Participation language**:
> "Buyer elects to purchase a participation interest in Seller's interest in the Loan facility. This transaction shall be evidenced by a Participation Agreement in form attached as Exhibit B, executed and delivered at or before Settlement Date."

Either party can typically elect participation (though buyer usually prefers assignment). If consent is expected to be withheld, buyer proactively elects participation in the trade confirmation.
