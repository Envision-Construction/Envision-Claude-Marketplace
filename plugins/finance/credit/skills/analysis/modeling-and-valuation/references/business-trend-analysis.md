## Part 5: Business Trend Analysis

Beyond ratios, analyze the underlying business trends to assess sustainability of credit metrics.

### Revenue Trends

**What to track:**
- Quarterly year-over-year (YoY) revenue growth
- Sequential quarter-over-quarter growth
- Organic growth vs acquired growth
- Growth rate trending: accelerating or decelerating?

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: SaaS Subscription Platform

Quarter 1: $50M revenue (10% YoY), +2% sequential
Quarter 2: $53M revenue (12% YoY), +6% sequential
Quarter 3: $57M revenue (14% YoY), +8% sequential
Quarter 4: $62M revenue (16% YoY), +9% sequential

Trend: Organic growth accelerating. Positive for leverage trajectory.
```

### EBITDA Margin Trends

**What to track:**
- EBITDA margin (EBITDA / Revenue) % over time
- Expanding margins = improving leverage (EBITDA grows faster than debt)
- Contracting margins = rising leverage risk

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Manufacturing

        Revenue    EBITDA    Margin
Q1:     $200M      $40M      20%
Q2:     $210M      $46M      21.9%
Q3:     $225M      $51M      22.7%
Q4:     $245M      $57M      23.3%

Expanding margins. For every $1 of new revenue, company keeps
increasing % as EBITDA. Positive trend for credit quality.
```

### Operating Leverage

**What to track:**
- Fixed vs variable cost structure
- When revenue changes, how much does EBITDA change?
- High fixed costs = high operating leverage (risky in downturns)

**Practical Example:**
```
Company A (High Fixed Costs - Airline):
Revenue decline 10%:  EBITDA declines 30%
High operating leverage = risky if revenue drops

Company B (High Variable Costs - Call Center):
Revenue decline 10%:  EBITDA declines 8%
Low operating leverage = more stable through cycles

In credit analysis: Company B is safer due to lower operating leverage.
```

### Expense Analysis

**What to track:**
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) as % of revenue: trending up or down?
- Selling, General & Admin (SG&A) as % of revenue
- Distinguish one-time vs recurring expenses
- Identify areas where costs can be controlled in downturns

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Industrial Services

              Year 1    Year 2    Year 3    Trend
Revenue:      $400M     $450M     $500M     +12.5% CAGR
COGS %:       65%       64%       62%       Improving
SG&A %:       20%       19%       18%       Improving
EBITDA %:     15%       17%       20%       Expanding

Both COGS and SG&A improving as % of revenue (operating leverage).
Credit quality improving.
```

### CapEx as % of Revenue

**What to track:**
- Is company investing enough to maintain operations?
- Growth CapEx vs maintenance CapEx
- Undercapitalization risk: too little investment

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Infrastructure Provider

Year 1: Revenue $500M, CapEx $75M = 15% (healthy for industry)
Year 2: Revenue $550M, CapEx $60M = 10.9% (declining investment)
Year 3: Revenue $600M, CapEx $45M = 7.5% (risky - undercapitalizing?)

Risk: Declining CapEx may indicate cost-cutting that harms future growth.
```

### Working Capital Efficiency

**Key metrics:**

**Days Sales Outstanding (DSO):**
```
DSO = (Accounts Receivable / Revenue) × 365
Interpretation: How many days to collect cash from sales?
Increasing DSO = collection problems (negative cash flow impact)
Decreasing DSO = faster collections (positive)
```

**Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO):**
```
DIO = (Inventory / COGS) × 365
Interpretation: How many days does inventory sit before sale?
Rising DIO = inventory buildup (negative cash flow)
Falling DIO = efficient inventory turns (positive)
```

**Days Payable Outstanding (DPO):**
```
DPO = (Accounts Payable / COGS) × 365
Interpretation: How many days does company delay paying suppliers?
Rising DPO = extending payables (positive cash flow short-term,
  but may indicate liquidity stress if stretched)
Falling DPO = paying faster (negative cash flow)
```

**Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC):**
```
CCC = DSO + DIO - DPO
Lower = more efficient (working capital gets recycled faster)
```

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Specialty Retailer

           Year 1    Year 2    Trend
DSO:       40 days   42 days   Worsening (collection issues?)
DIO:       35 days   38 days   Worsening (inventory building?)
DPO:       25 days   28 days   Improving (paying slower)
CCC:       50 days   52 days   Slightly worse

Working capital is becoming less efficient. This will impact FCF.
```

### Business Line Detail (Segment Analysis)

**What to track:**
- Revenue and margin by business segment
- Which segments are growing / declining?
- Which are high-margin / low-margin?
- Diversification: concentration risk?

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Diversified Industrial

Segment           Revenue      Margin    Trend
Manufacturing:    $300M (60%)   28%       Flat / mature
Services:         $150M (30%)   22%       +15% growth
New Tech:         $50M (10%)    -5%       Investing, not profitable yet

Analysis: Service segment driving growth. New Tech is R&D investment.
Manufacturing is cash-generative base. Overall health good.
```

### Company Guidance vs Actual Results

**What to track:**
- Does management meet, beat, or miss guidance?
- Track record over multiple quarters
- What's the pattern? Over-promise, under-deliver? Or conservative?

**Practical Example:**
```
Company Management Track Record:

Guidance      Actual        Result
Q1: $50M      $51M          Beat (+2%)
Q2: $55M      $53M          Miss (-4%)
Q3: $60M      $62M          Beat (+3%)
Q4: $65M      $62M          Miss (-5%)

Pattern: Management guidance is aggressive; actual results trail.
Risk factor: Cannot rely on optimistic guidance for credit analysis.
Use conservative assumptions.
```

### Peer Comparison

**What to track:**
- How do leverage, coverage, and margins compare to peers?
- Is company stronger or weaker than industry?
- Are trends better or worse than competitors?

**Practical Example:**
```
Company: Software Services
Our analysis           Peer Avg      Peer Range
Leverage:    3.2x      3.5x          2.8x - 4.5x
Coverage:    6.5x      5.2x          4.0x - 7.5x
EBITDA Margin: 35%    32%           28% - 38%

Assessment: Better leverage, better coverage, better margin than peers.
Credit quality is above-average for the sector.
```
