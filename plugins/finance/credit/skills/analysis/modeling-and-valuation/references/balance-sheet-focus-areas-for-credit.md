## Balance Sheet Focus Areas for Credit

### Cash and Equivalents: Available Liquidity

Cash on hand is the first source of liquidity.

**Assessment:**
- Absolute level: Is $10M much for a $500M revenue company? Yes. For a $5B revenue company? No.
- Typical range: 30-60 days of operating expenses (business-dependent)
- Restricted cash: Some cash may be restricted (held in escrow, collateral for credit cards, etc.); adjust accordingly
- Foreign cash: Repatriation may have tax or regulatory consequences

### Total Debt: Comprehensive Accounting

```
Total Debt = Short-Term Debt + Current Portion of Long-Term Debt + Long-Term Debt
  + Deferred financing fees (subtract from debt in calculating net debt)
  + Capital leases (now mostly on-balance-sheet under ASC 842)
  + Off-balance-sheet obligations (securitizations, guarantees)
```

**Common debt instruments in leveraged finance:**
- **Senior Secured Loans:** first lien on assets, lowest interest rate
- **Senior Unsecured Bonds:** second claim after secured debt
- **Subordinated (Sub) Debt:** junior to senior debt, higher coupon
- **PIK Toggle Notes:** paid-in-kind or cash coupon at issuer's election
- **Convertible Bonds:** bond with equity conversion option
- **Preferred Equity:** hybrid between debt and equity

### Net Debt: Debt Minus Cash

```
Net Debt = Total Debt - Cash and Equivalents
```

Net debt is the true debt burden (cash can pay down debt).

**Example:**
- Gross Debt: $500M
- Cash: $25M
- Net Debt: $475M

**Implication:** Company has $475M net borrowing net of cash available. See credit-ratio-analysis skill for Net Debt / EBITDA leverage calculation.

### Working Capital: Operating Assets and Liabilities

```
Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities
              = (Cash + Receivables + Inventory) - (Payables + Accrued Expenses + Current Debt)
```

**Assessment:**
- Positive WC is normal for operating businesses
- Negative WC may indicate efficient operations (payables > receivables/inventory) or financial distress
- Required WC level is business-specific; compare to peers and historical levels

**Example:**
- Current Assets: $150M (Cash $20M, Receivables $80M, Inventory $50M)
- Current Liabilities: $120M (Payables $70M, Accrued $30M, Current Debt $20M)
- Working Capital: $30M

### Goodwill and Intangibles: Acquisition Premiums

**Goodwill** represents the amount paid for an acquisition above book value of identifiable net assets.

**Intangibles** include separately identifiable intangible assets:
- Trademarks and brand names
- Customer lists
- Technology patents
- Non-compete agreements
- Favorable contracts

**Credit considerations:**
- Goodwill is highest risk in stress; often written down first in bankruptcy
- Intangible assets with finite life are amortized (add-back in EBITDA calculation)
- Large goodwill relative to equity suggests overpayment or aggressive leverage
- In covenant tests, some agreements allow goodwill to be added back in calculating tangible net worth

**Example:**
- Company acquired ABC Corp for $150M cash when book value was $80M
- Goodwill = $70M
- If asset values decline 20%, goodwill may be impaired entirely
- This would reduce equity by $70M with no cash impact, but triggers covenant testing

### Off-Balance-Sheet Items

**Operating Leases (ASC 842):**
- As of 2019, most operating leases are now on-balance-sheet
- Right-of-use (ROU) asset and lease liability recorded
- Impact: Increases both assets and liabilities; affects leverage and interest coverage ratios

**Securitizations:**
- Company sells receivables to SPV (special purpose vehicle)
- SPV issues securities to investors
- Company continues to service receivables
- Effect: Removes receivables from balance sheet but company retains servicing obligations
- In credit analysis, add back securitized receivables for true credit assessment

**Guarantees:**
- Company guarantees debt of subsidiaries or affiliates
- May not appear as liability if contingent and unlikely
- In default scenario, may become primary obligor
- Always disclose in credit summary
