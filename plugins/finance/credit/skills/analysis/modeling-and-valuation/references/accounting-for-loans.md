## Accounting for Loans

### Loan Classification Under ASC 310

**Held for Investment (HFI):** Loans management intends and has ability to hold for foreseeable future. Carried at amortized cost (principal outstanding minus unearned income, net deferred fees, unamortized premium/discount). Subject to credit loss allowance under CECL.

**Held for Sale (HFS):** Loans management intends to sell. Carried at lower of cost or fair value (LOCOM). Write-downs charged to earnings. Common for warehouse lenders and loan originators.

**Fair Value Option (FVO):** Election under ASC 825 to measure loans at fair value through earnings. Once elected, irrevocable. Common for hedge funds and CLO entities.

**Trading:** Actively traded loan positions carried at fair value with changes through P&L. Used by dealer trading desks.

### CECL (Current Expected Credit Losses) — ASC 326

**Replaced:** Incurred loss model (old ASC 310-10) — could only recognize losses when "probable and estimable"

**New standard:** Requires recognition of LIFETIME expected credit losses from Day 1

**Measurement:** Based on historical loss experience, current conditions, and reasonable/supportable forecasts

**Impact on loan portfolios:**
- Banks must estimate expected losses over entire remaining life of each loan
- Allowance typically increases significantly vs. incurred loss model (especially for long-dated loans)
- Pro-cyclical concern: Allowance should build in good times (when forecasts are benign) — but models tend to lag

**Methodologies:** Weighted-average remaining maturity (WARM), vintage analysis, probability of default / loss given default (PD/LGD), discounted cash flow

**Disclosure requirements:** Disaggregated by portfolio segment and class; credit quality indicators; vintage analysis; collateral-dependent loans

### Purchase Accounting for Acquired Loans

**Purchased Credit-Deteriorated (PCD) loans:** Loans acquired with more-than-insignificant credit deterioration since origination

**Initial measurement:** Fair value at acquisition + allowance for credit losses = new amortized cost basis (gross-up approach)

**Non-PCD acquired loans:** Recorded at fair value; no day-1 allowance gross-up

**Accretable yield:** Difference between expected cash flows and acquisition price — accreted into interest income over loan life

**Non-accretable difference:** Excess of contractual cash flows over expected cash flows — represents expected credit losses, NOT accreted

### Fair Value Measurement for Loans (ASC 820)

**Level 1:** Quoted prices in active markets — rarely applicable to loans (no exchange)

**Level 2:** Observable inputs — dealer quotes, LSTA mark-to-market prices, comparable loan pricing. Most traded institutional loans fall here.

**Level 3:** Unobservable inputs — model-based valuation (DCF with credit-adjusted discount rates). Used for illiquid or bespoke loans.

**Key inputs for Level 3:** Discount rate (risk-free + credit spread), expected cash flows (timing and amount), probability of default, loss given default, prepayment assumptions

### Loan Fee and Discount Accounting

**Origination fees (net of costs):** Deferred and amortized as yield adjustment over loan life using effective interest method (ASC 310-20)

**Commitment fees:** If likelihood of funding is remote — recognized as service fee income. If funding is expected — deferred and amortized as yield adjustment.

**OID (Original Issue Discount):** Difference between par and issue price. Accreted into interest income using effective interest method.

**Prepayment:** If loan prepays, unamortized fees/discount recognized immediately in income

**Modification vs. extinguishment:** If loan terms are substantially modified (>10% change in PV of cash flows), treat as extinguishment of old loan and origination of new loan. Below threshold = modification (adjust effective interest rate prospectively).

### Regulatory Capital Treatment (Basel III / US Implementation)

**Risk-weighted assets (RWA):** Loans contribute to bank's required capital based on risk weighting
- Corporate loans: 100% risk weight (standardized approach) or model-based (advanced approach)
- Senior secured with strong collateral: May receive lower risk weight under IRB approach

**Capital requirements:** Banks must hold minimum CET1 (Common Equity Tier 1) against RWA
- Minimum CET1 ratio: 4.5% + capital conservation buffer (2.5%) = 7.0% effective minimum
- G-SIB surcharge: Additional 1-3.5% for systemically important banks

**Impact on loan pricing:** Higher capital requirements → higher cost of capital → wider spreads demanded by banks

**Significant Risk Transfer (SRT):** Banks can reduce RWA by selling loan risk via CLOs, CDS, or sub-participations — but must demonstrate genuine risk transfer
