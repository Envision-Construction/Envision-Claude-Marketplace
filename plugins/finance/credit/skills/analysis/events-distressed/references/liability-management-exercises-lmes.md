## Liability Management Exercises (LMEs)

An LME is an out-of-court transaction that changes creditor economics, priority, or collateral position without a formal bankruptcy filing. The central underwriting question is not whether the transaction is creative; it is whether the documentation allows value to be shifted from one creditor group to another.

### Why Borrowers Use LMEs

- Extend runway without filing Chapter 11
- Improve near-term liquidity or maturity profile
- Reward a cooperative creditor group and isolate non-participants
- Preserve management control and avoid broader court oversight

### Common LME Forms

**Uptier or priming exchange**
- A participating creditor group exchanges into a more senior or better-protected instrument
- Main risk to others: effective subordination or collateral dilution

**Dropdown or asset transfer**
- Valuable assets move outside the existing collateral package and support new financing elsewhere
- Main risk to others: collateral migration and weaker recovery support

**Discounted pari or exchange transaction**
- Existing claims are replaced with longer-dated or different paper on revised economics
- Main risk to others: weaker holdout position if enough creditors participate

**Other value-transfer structures**
- Back-to-back claims, circular guarantees, or other arrangements can change who really sits first in line
- Main risk to others: nominal ranking stays the same while economic ranking worsens

### What to Review in the Documents

- Restricted payments and investment capacity
- Debt incurrence and lien flexibility
- Open-market purchase or exchange provisions
- Sacred rights and amendment thresholds
- Pro rata sharing language
- Collateral release and unrestricted subsidiary mechanics

### Questions for Underwriting

1. Which assets or claims can be moved, exchanged, or re-ranked under the existing documents?
2. What percentage of lenders is needed to authorize the path being discussed?
3. Are key protections explicit, or only assumed from convention?
4. Does the company have both incentive and need to pursue a coercive transaction?
5. Which creditor group has the practical blocking position?

### Common Mistakes

- Treating covenant labels as protection without reading the actual mechanics
- Assuming pari passu means equal economics after an exchange or priming transaction
- Ignoring structural changes because headline leverage looks unchanged
- Failing to distinguish documentation risk from ordinary credit deterioration
