## Distressed Loan Investing (LSTA Standards)

### Par vs. Distressed Trading Distinction
- **Par trading**: Loans trading close to full expected repayment. Standard par-trade conventions generally apply.
- **Distressed trading**: Loans trading at levels where recovery, transfer mechanics, or legal complexity become central. Distressed-trade conventions generally apply.
- **Why the distinction matters**: Distressed trades have longer settlement because they involve more complex documentation review, potential bankruptcy considerations, and assignment/participation complications
- **Important**: Confirm the applicable settlement convention, price-band treatment, and documentation package against current LSTA standards and any deal-specific transfer restrictions rather than relying on static thresholds.

### Buy-Side Operational Considerations for Distressed Loans
- **Due diligence requirements**: Distressed loan buyers must review full credit agreement, intercreditor agreements, amendment history, agent notices, all side letters
- **Agent coordination**: Must contact administrative agent to confirm outstanding amounts, accrued interest, any pending amendments or waivers
- **Consent requirements**: Many credit agreements require borrower and/or agent consent for assignments. In distress, borrower may withhold consent strategically (to block hostile creditors)
- **Disqualified lender lists**: Credit agreements may contain "DQ lists" — specific institutions (often competitors or activist funds) barred from acquiring loans via assignment
- **Participation as workaround**: If assignment is blocked (consent withheld, DQ list), buyer may acquire economic exposure via participation instead — but loses direct voting rights and direct relationship with agent
- **MNPI (Material Non-Public Information)**: Loan investors often receive MNPI through lender group communications. Creates "wall" complications if fund also trades public securities of same issuer. Must have MNPI policies and procedures.

### Claims Trading in Loans
- **Pre-petition claims**: Loans outstanding before bankruptcy filing. Traded as "claims" post-filing under Bankruptcy Rule 3001
- **Post-petition interest**: Secured creditors are entitled to post-petition interest if oversecured (collateral value exceeds claim). Unsecured creditors generally are NOT entitled to post-petition interest.
- **Accreted value**: For traded claims, purchase price becomes new basis. Recovery measured against purchase price, not par.
- **Section 363 sales**: Quick asset sales in bankruptcy; claims traders must act fast to participate in credit bidding
- **Credit bidding**: Secured creditors can bid their claim amount (at face value) in asset sales, avoiding need for cash. Powerful tool for loan holders in bankruptcy.

### Distressed Loan Investment Strategies
1. **Loan-to-own**: Buy discounted loans with intent to convert to equity through restructuring. Target: fulcrum security or near-fulcrum. Control the restructuring to maximize recovery.
2. **Trading / Relative value**: Buy distressed loans expected to recover in value as company stabilizes or restructuring progresses. Shorter-term, less activist.
3. **DIP financing**: Provide new money post-petition (super-priority status). Earn high fees and spreads; gain influence over restructuring process. Roll-up: convert pre-petition claim into DIP position.
4. **Blocking position**: Accumulate 33.4%+ of a class to block an unfavorable reorganization plan (requires 2/3 in amount to approve). Forces better treatment.
5. **Par build**: Buy loans at a meaningful discount where the underwriting suggests full or near-full recovery through refinancing, amendment, or restructuring. Lower risk than equity conversion strategies.

### Loan-Specific Bankruptcy Considerations
- **Adequate protection**: Secured loan holders can demand adequate protection payments during bankruptcy (to protect against collateral depreciation). May include cash payments, replacement liens, or administrative priority claims.
- **Revolver draws pre-petition**: "Last-minute" draws on revolver before bankruptcy filing. Can be challenged as preferences; agents may refuse to fund. Borrowers rely on MAC carve-outs.
- **Pro rata sharing in bankruptcy**: Lenders within same tranche still share pro rata. However, different tranches (first lien vs second lien) have different priority.
- **Assignment restrictions in bankruptcy**: Bankruptcy court can override contractual restrictions on assignment, allowing transfers that would otherwise be blocked.
- **Intercreditor disputes**: First lien vs second lien conflicts intensify in bankruptcy. Intercreditor agreement provisions (standstill, release, purchase option) become critical. Second lien's "purchase right" at par can shift control.

### Valuation in Distressed Loan Context
- **Loan trading prices as market signal**: Secondary loan prices reflect real-time market assessment of recovery prospects
- **Comparable transaction analysis**: Recovery rates from similar industry bankruptcies provide valuation benchmarks
- **Historical recovery data**: See `references/default-recovery-rates.md` for first-lien term loans, second-lien, and unsecured bonds average recovery rates
- **Present value of recovery**: Discount expected recovery for time to realization and process uncertainty rather than using headline recovery alone
