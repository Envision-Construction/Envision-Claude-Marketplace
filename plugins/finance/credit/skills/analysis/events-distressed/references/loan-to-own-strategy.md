# Loan-to-Own Strategy

Framework for evaluating and executing loan-to-own (LTO) strategies in distressed credit investing, including positioning, execution mechanics, and return analysis.

## LTO Decision Framework

### When to Pursue Loan-to-Own

| Factor | Favorable for LTO | Unfavorable for LTO |
|---|---|---|
| Asset Quality | Strong underlying assets, identifiable value | Deteriorating assets, secular decline |
| Discount to Recovery | Debt trading at 50-70 cents, recovery >80 cents | Debt near par or recovery highly uncertain |
| Operational Improvement | Clear turnaround plan, identifiable cost savings | No path to operational improvement |
| Capital Structure | Clean structure, identifiable fulcrum | Complex multi-tranche, multiple fulcrums |
| Management | Capable team or identifiable replacement | Key person dependency, no succession |
| Competitive Landscape | Limited competing acquirers | Multiple strategic/financial buyers |
| Timeline | Reasonable path to resolution (6-18 months) | Extended timeline risk (>24 months) |

### LTO vs. Other Distressed Strategies

| Strategy | Entry Point | Objective | Typical Return Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loan-to-Own | Fulcrum security at discount | Control through restructuring | 20-30% IRR on equity |
| Trading (par recovery) | Any tranche at discount | Par recovery through amendment/restructuring | 15-25% total return |
| DIP Lending | New money in bankruptcy | Priority claim + fees + warrants | 10-15% cash yield + upside |
| Liquidation Play | Senior secured at deep discount | Asset liquidation recovery | 10-20% total return |

## Positioning

### Building a Control Position

| Position Size | Strategic Value | Practical Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| >50% of tranche | Control — can direct restructuring | Requires significant capital; may trigger regulatory/disclosure obligations |
| >33.3% of tranche | Blocking position — can block unfavorable plans | Minimum for meaningful influence over plan confirmation |
| 25-33% | Significant influence | Can negotiate but cannot unilaterally block |
| <25% | Limited influence | Following, not leading, the restructuring |

### Target: Fulcrum Security

The fulcrum security is the tranche where enterprise value "breaks" — partially impaired, receiving a mix of new debt and equity in restructuring:

1. **Estimate enterprise value** (going-concern basis) using EBITDA multiples, DCF, and comparable transactions
2. **Build claims waterfall** from administrative claims through junior debt
3. **Identify the tranche** where cumulative claims exceed enterprise value
4. **Target that tranche** for accumulation at a discount

### Accumulation Tactics

- **Stealth accumulation**: Buy gradually through multiple dealers to avoid signaling interest
- **Timing**: Accumulate during maximum uncertainty (post-default, pre-plan) when selling pressure is highest
- **Liquidity events**: Leverage forced selling by CLOs (CCC bucket breaches), mutual funds (redemptions), and banks (regulatory pressure)
- **MNPI considerations**: LTO investors often wall themselves by joining restricted groups to access confidential information; this creates trading blackout periods

## Credit Bidding

### Section 363 Sale Mechanics

Under Section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code, assets can be sold free and clear of liens, claims, and encumbrances:

| Concept | Definition | Strategic Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Credit Bid Right | Secured creditors can bid up to the face amount of their secured claim | No cash required — debt converts to ownership at face value |
| Cash Bid | Third-party cash offer | Competes with credit bid; may exceed recovery expectations |
| Qualified Bid | Bid meeting minimum requirements set by bidding procedures | Must demonstrate financing, regulatory clearance |

**Credit Bid Advantage**: A secured lender holding $200M face value of first lien debt (purchased at $140M) can credit bid $200M — effectively acquiring assets for $140M cash investment. Cash bidders must raise $200M+ to compete.

**Credit Bid Limitation**: Courts can limit credit bidding under certain circumstances (e.g., RadLAX Gateway Hotel — plan can require secured creditors to accept the "indubitable equivalent" of their claims rather than credit bidding).

## Stalking Horse Process

| Element | Description | Typical Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Stalking Horse Bid | Initial bid that sets the floor for auction | Negotiated pre-filing or early in case |
| Break-Up Fee | Compensation to stalking horse if outbid | 1-3% of bid value |
| Expense Reimbursement | Stalking horse's DD and legal costs reimbursed | $1-5M+ |
| Bid Protections | Minimum overbid increment, auction rules | $1-5M increment per round |
| Auction Timeline | Period for competing bids | 30-60 days from bid deadline |

**Strategy**: Serving as stalking horse provides informational advantage (extensive due diligence access) and economic protection (break-up fee covers costs if outbid). Disadvantage: sets a floor that may attract competing bids.

## Execution Mechanics

### Plan Support Agreement (PSA) / Restructuring Support Agreement (RSA)

| Element | Purpose | Key Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Support Commitment | Creditors agree to vote for and support the plan | Binding on signatories |
| Lockup Provisions | Restrictions on transferring claims during support period | Transfer only to parties who agree to PSA |
| Milestones | Timeline for filing plan, solicitation, confirmation | Typically 60-120 day process |
| Fiduciary Out | Allows board to consider superior proposals | Limited to materially better alternatives |
| Termination Rights | Conditions under which parties can exit PSA | Milestone failure, material adverse change |

### Co-Investment Structures

Large LTO transactions often require co-investors:
- **Ad hoc group formation**: Align with other significant creditors on restructuring terms
- **Equity co-investment**: Backstop commitment for rights offering in plan
- **Management incentive plan (MIP)**: Reserve 5-15% of post-emergence equity for management retention and incentive

## Key Risks

| Risk | Description | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation Uncertainty | Enterprise value may be lower than estimated | Conservative valuation; multiple methodologies |
| Timeline Risk | Bankruptcy may take 12-24+ months; professional fees erode value | Negotiate milestones; pre-packaged plan if possible |
| Management Retention | Key management may depart during restructuring | Key Employee Retention Plans (KERPs), MIP |
| Regulatory Risk | Industry-specific regulatory approvals for change of control | Early regulatory engagement; backup plans |
| Environmental Liability | Successor liability for contamination at acquired sites | Phase I/II environmental due diligence |
| Competing Bids | Strategic or financial buyers may outbid credit bid | Build blocking position; serve as stalking horse |
| Execution Risk | Post-emergence operational turnaround may fail | Detailed 100-day plan; experienced operating partners |

## Return Analysis

### LTO Return Framework

| Component | Calculation | Typical Range |
|---|---|---|
| Entry Price | Purchase price of debt claim | 40-70 cents on the dollar |
| Recovery Value | Estimated value of post-emergence equity + new debt received | 80-120 cents on pre-petition face |
| Gross Return | (Recovery - Entry) / Entry | 30-100%+ |
| Timeline | Filing to emergence to value realization | 12-36 months |
| Professional Fees | Legal, financial advisor, management consultant costs | 5-15% of estate value |
| Net IRR | Gross return adjusted for fees and timeline | Target: 20-30%+ |

### Post-Emergence Equity Upside

The ultimate LTO return includes post-emergence equity appreciation:
- **Year 1-2**: Operational improvements, cost rationalization, deleveraging
- **Year 2-4**: Growth initiatives, margin expansion, multiple expansion
- **Exit**: IPO, strategic sale, or secondary LBO at improved multiple
- **Target**: 2.0-3.0x MOIC over 3-5 year holding period
