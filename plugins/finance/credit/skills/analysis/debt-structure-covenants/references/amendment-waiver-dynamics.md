# Amendment & Waiver Dynamics

Framework for analyzing amendment and waiver mechanics, negotiating leverage, and the way repeated document changes alter credit quality over time.

## Core Definitions

| Term | Meaning | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| Waiver | Temporary forgiveness of a specific breach or default | Single covenant miss, technical default, one-off event |
| Amendment | Permanent change to the agreement | Covenant reset, basket change, reporting change |
| Amendment and extension | Amendment combined with maturity pushout | Refinance alternative when capital markets are not fully open |
| Amendment and restatement | Broad rewrite of the agreement | Major recapitalization, acquisition, or structural redesign |

## Consent Structure

The first question in any amendment analysis is who can approve the change.

| Amendment Category | Usual Protection Level | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Routine covenant or reporting changes | Majority or required lenders | Operational flexibility can move without every lender agreeing |
| Collateral release, guarantee release, or waterfall changes | Supermajority or all affected lenders | Can impair recoveries for non-consenting creditors |
| Maturity, principal, or coupon changes | All affected lenders | Core economic rights are usually sacred |
| Voting-threshold or pro rata sharing changes | All affected lenders or supermajority | Prevents a coalition from rewriting future control rules |
| Lien or payment subordination | All affected lenders | Protects rank and recovery position |

**Working rule:** Never rely on market convention. Read the actual sacred-rights language and confirm whether the agreement protects lien priority, collateral release, guarantee release, and pro rata treatment explicitly.

## Negotiating Leverage

Amendment outcomes usually follow bargaining power more than abstract covenant logic.

### Borrower leverage is stronger when:
- Liquidity is still adequate and the breach is technical rather than existential.
- The borrower has alternative financing, asset-sale options, or sponsor support.
- The syndicate is concentrated, relationship-driven, or wants to avoid a disruptive default.
- The amendment request solves a temporary timing issue rather than a broken capital structure.

### Lender leverage is stronger when:
- The borrower is near a liquidity wall or must draw on restricted sources of cash.
- The lender group is aligned and recovery value is still preservable.
- The amendment asks for structural concessions rather than short-term breathing room.
- Prior amendments have already consumed most of the available covenant flexibility.

## What Usually Changes in an Amendment

Amendments should be read as a transfer of value, not just a legal clean-up exercise.

Common borrower asks:
- Higher covenant levels or looser definitions.
- Larger debt, lien, investment, or restricted-payment baskets.
- Longer cure periods or broader equity-cure rights.
- Reduced reporting, delayed delivery, or softer event-of-default language.
- Maturity extension without a full refinancing.

Common lender asks in return:
- Better economics, tighter reporting, or more frequent information.
- Additional collateral, guarantors, or reserve requirements.
- Tighter baskets, stronger blockers, or restricted distribution capacity.
- Shorter review periods and earlier lender visibility into stress.

## Cumulative Erosion Framework

One amendment rarely tells the full story. The real analysis is the cumulative change log.

Review each amendment for:
1. **Definition drift**: Has EBITDA, debt, or available amount become more permissive?
2. **Capacity expansion**: Have incremental, RP, investment, or unrestricted-subsidiary pathways widened?
3. **Control dilution**: Have consent thresholds, sacred rights, or blocker provisions weakened?
4. **Transparency loss**: Have reporting duties, certificates, or lender-information rights been reduced?
5. **Extension risk**: Has maturity been pushed out without solving the underlying leverage or business problem?

## Equity Cure Interaction

Equity cure rights often sit at the boundary between temporary underperformance and formal amendment.

- A cure right can postpone the need for an amendment, but repeated cures are a warning that the capital structure no longer fits the business.
- If an amendment expands cure rights, assess whether the change improves recoverability or simply delays recognition of stress.
- Use `references/equity-cure-mechanics.md` for the detailed cure framework before concluding that cure availability meaningfully protects lenders.

## Red Flags

- [ ] Multiple amendments over a short period, especially when each change weakens a different protection.
- [ ] Sacred rights or consent thresholds narrowed through amendment.
- [ ] EBITDA definitions, addbacks, or basket capacities broadened after performance deterioration.
- [ ] Reporting obligations relaxed when transparency should be increasing.
- [ ] Amendment and extension that pushes out maturity but leaves leverage, collateral, or liquidity issues unresolved.
- [ ] Selective economics or consent mechanics that pressure minority lenders to join.
- [ ] Amendment followed quickly by dividend extraction, new debt, or asset transfers.

## Credit Analysis Integration

When a credit has amendment history:

1. Build a change log from original agreement to current agreement.
2. Re-underwrite the current document package, not the original covenant set.
3. Identify what protections remain usable in the next downturn or refinancing event.
4. Treat serial amendments as evidence about business quality, sponsor behavior, and lender leverage.
5. Feed the revised structure into liquidity, refinancing, and recovery analysis rather than treating the amendment as an isolated legal event.
