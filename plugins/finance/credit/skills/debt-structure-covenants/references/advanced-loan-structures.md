## Advanced Loan Structures

### Incremental Facilities (Accordion Feature)
- **Definition**: Pre-agreed right in credit agreement to add additional debt (term loans or revolving capacity) without full lender consent
- **Incremental term loan**: New tranche added to existing credit facility, typically pari passu with existing term loans
- **Capacity**: Specified as fixed dollar amount (e.g., "up to $200M incremental") and/or ratio-based (e.g., "additional amounts so long as pro forma first-lien leverage ≤ 4.0x")
- **"Free and clear" basket**: Fixed dollar amount available without any leverage test (also called "freebie basket")
- **MFN (Most Favored Nation) protection**: If incremental term loan spread exceeds existing by more than threshold (typically 50 bps), existing term loan spread "steps up" to match (minus threshold)
  - MFN sunset: Protection often expires after 6-12 months, allowing higher-spread incremental without step-up
- **Use cases**: Fund add-on acquisitions, finance capital expenditure, provide additional liquidity
- **Covenant implications**: Incremental debt may not require same financial maintenance covenants; "incremental equivalent debt" may be issued outside credit facility entirely (secured notes)

### Delayed Draw Term Loans (DDTL)
- **Definition**: Committed but unfunded term loan that can be drawn during a specified availability period
- **Availability period**: Typically 12-24 months from closing
- **Ticking fee**: Fee accrues on undrawn committed amount (typically 50% of applicable spread)
- **Draw conditions**: May require satisfaction of conditions precedent (e.g., acquisition completion, leverage test)
- **Use case**: Committed financing for identified but not-yet-closed acquisitions; provides certainty of funding
- **Once drawn**: Converts to regular term loan with same amortization and maturity

### First Lien / Second Lien Structures
- **First lien**: Senior secured with first-priority lien on all collateral. Includes revolver, TLA, TLB.
- **Second lien**: Secured but with second-priority lien — subordinated lien position, not subordinated in right of payment
- **Intercreditor agreement**: Governs relationship between first and second lien holders
  - **Standstill period**: Second lien lenders must wait (typically 90-180 days) before exercising remedies after default
  - **Release provisions**: Second lien lenders must release collateral if first lien directs asset sale
  - **Voting rights**: Second lien may be deemed to vote with first lien on certain matters
  - **Purchase option**: Second lien lenders may have right to purchase first lien debt at par (to control restructuring)
- **Pricing**: Second lien typically 300-500+ bps wider than first lien (compensating for subordinated lien position)
- **Recovery**: First lien recovers from collateral first; second lien gets residual. In severe downturns, second lien recovery can approach unsecured levels (20-40%)

### Unitranche Facilities
- **Definition**: Single-tranche loan that blends senior and subordinated economics into one facility
- **Structure**: One credit agreement, one lien, one agent — but often with an Agreement Among Lenders (AAL) that splits economics between "first out" and "last out" participants
- **First out / Last out**: "First out" lenders get paid first from collateral (lower spread); "last out" lenders get remaining (higher spread). Blended rate to borrower.
- **Advantage for borrower**: Simplified documentation, single lender relationship, faster execution
- **Advantage for lenders**: "First out" gets better-than-senior economics; "last out" gets better-than-mezz economics
- **Common in**: Middle market lending, direct lending, private credit
- **Example**: $200M unitranche at L+600 blended. AAL splits: $120M first out at L+400, $80M last out at L+900

### Split Collateral Structures
- **Definition**: Different lender groups hold liens on different pools of collateral
- **Example**: ABL (asset-based lending) revolver has first lien on current assets (A/R, inventory); term loan has first lien on fixed assets (PP&E, IP). Each is "second lien" on the other's primary collateral.
- **Intercreditor**: ABL/term loan intercreditor agreement governs collateral priority waterfall
- **Advantage**: Maximizes total borrowing capacity by matching debt types to asset characteristics
- **Common in**: Retail, manufacturing, and companies with significant working capital needs

### Bridge Loans
- **Definition**: Short-term financing intended to be replaced by permanent financing (bonds or term loans)
- **Typical tenor**: 6-12 months, with 1-2 extensions
- **Roll-over**: If not refinanced, typically converts to term loan with escalating pricing (increasing spread every 90 days)
- **"Bridge to bond"**: Provides certainty of financing for acquisition; replaced by high-yield bond issuance post-closing
- **Fee structure**: Commitment fee during commitment period; increasing "duration fee" if bridge stays outstanding
- **Risk to arranger**: If capital markets are closed and bridge cannot be refinanced, arranger is stuck holding expensive loan ("hung bridge")
