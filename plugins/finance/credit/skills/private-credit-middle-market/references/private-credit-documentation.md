# Private Credit Documentation Analysis

## Documentation Lens

Private credit documents should be read as control documents, not just legal artifacts. The core question is whether the lender can detect deterioration early, limit value leakage, and negotiate from strength before enterprise value disappears.

## Private Credit vs. Broadly Syndicated Documentation

| Feature | Private Credit | Broadly Syndicated Context |
|---|---|---|
| Covenant style | Maintenance-oriented and lender-controlled | More often incurrence-oriented and market-standardized |
| Information rights | More direct and more frequent | More periodic and agent-mediated |
| Amendment process | Small lender group, concentrated leverage | Wider lender set, greater coordination burden |
| Documentation objective | Preserve control and early intervention | Preserve distribution and marketability |

For current documentation drift and market conventions, use `references/credit-agreement-trends-documentation-risk.md`.

## Core Areas to Underwrite

### Financial Covenants

Focus on whether covenants are:

- Measured on definitions the lender can trust
- Set tightly enough to give early warning
- Designed to catch the actual risk in the business
- Paired with the right cure limits and reporting obligations

Common covenant families:

1. **Leverage tests** for overall indebtedness tolerance
2. **Coverage tests** for cash debt service pressure
3. **Minimum earnings or liquidity tests** where headline leverage understates risk
4. **Capex or acquisition controls** where reinvestment or expansion can impair coverage

### EBITDA Definition Quality

The EBITDA definition often determines whether the document is truly lender-protective.

Review:

- What counts as non-recurring
- Whether synergies require evidence and a clear time limit
- Whether projected benefits can be counted before they are realized
- Whether equity cures affect only covenant compliance or also expand debt and leakage capacity
- Whether recurring operating costs are being reframed as adjustments

### Value Leakage Pathways

Read every provision that can move value away from the lender:

- Restricted payments
- Incremental debt and additional liens
- Investments and acquisitions
- Unrestricted subsidiary designations
- Asset sales and reinvestment rights
- Affiliate transactions

The right question is not whether a basket exists, but whether the combined package can recreate leverage or transfer collateral outside the original underwrite.

### Information Rights

Private credit documentation should require:

- Timely financial statements
- Detailed compliance calculations
- Annual budget or operating plan delivery
- Prompt notice of material events
- Escalated reporting in stress
- Governance access where appropriate, such as board materials or observer rights

Without reliable information rights, even a strong covenant package becomes reactive rather than preventive.

## Common Documentation Failure Modes

### Equity Cure Abuse

- Repeated cures can delay recognition of a broken underwrite
- Cure mechanics should not quietly expand downstream debt or distribution capacity
- Treat cure usage as a surveillance event, not only a legal compliance point

### EBITDA Addback Creep

- Adjusted EBITDA that drifts farther from reported EBITDA is often the earliest sign of document misuse
- Track the composition of addbacks over time, not just the total number

### Leakage Through Structural Flexibility

- Unrestricted subsidiaries
- Broad investment baskets
- Loose affiliate transaction language
- Distribution capacity detached from true deleveraging

### Amendment Creep

- Serial amendments can transform a strong original document into a weak current one
- Always underwrite the as-amended agreement, not the original term sheet

## Documentation Review Checklist

- [ ] Covenant package matches the real business risks rather than a generic template
- [ ] EBITDA definition clearly distinguishes recurring costs from permitted adjustments
- [ ] Cure rights are limited and do not expand other capacities beyond covenant compliance
- [ ] Debt, lien, investment, and restricted payment baskets are read together for leakage risk
- [ ] Information rights are frequent enough to support early intervention
- [ ] Change-of-control and guarantor-release language preserves lender control
- [ ] Amendment consent thresholds are clear on economic and structural protections
- [ ] The current as-amended agreement is the one being underwritten
