# Amendment, Waiver, and Forbearance Framework

## Why This Matters

Private credit underwriting should assume that stressed credits are more likely to be amended than immediately sold. The key question is not whether a document is theoretically strong, but whether the lender can convert early warning into better economics, tighter control, or a safer exit path.

## Core Definitions

- **Amendment**: Permanent change to the agreement
- **Waiver**: Limited forgiveness of a specific breach without rewriting the underlying provision
- **Forbearance**: Temporary agreement not to enforce remedies while default remains outstanding

Use the instrument that matches the problem. Treating a structural underwrite miss as a one-off waiver or treating an acute default as a routine amendment weakens lender control.

## Selection Guide

| Situation | Preferred Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Business model changed or original terms no longer fit reality | Amendment | Terms need to be reset, not excused once |
| One-time breach with otherwise intact credit thesis | Waiver | Preserves the original protection framework |
| Default exists and the lender needs time to evaluate options | Forbearance | Protects remedy rights while creating a controlled negotiation period |

## Amendment Analysis Framework

### 1. Severity of the Problem

- Is the issue technical, temporary, or structural?
- Does it affect one covenant, multiple covenants, or enterprise value itself?
- Is the borrower asking for time, economics, or new flexibility?

### 2. Source of Weakness

- Operational underperformance
- Rate or liquidity pressure
- Acquisition or integration miss
- EBITDA definition abuse
- Cash leakage or sponsor behavior

### 3. Borrower and Owner Credibility

- Has management been transparent?
- Has ownership supported the business or extracted value?
- Is the amendment the first sign of stress or part of a pattern?

### 4. Lender Leverage

- How concentrated is the lender group?
- Does the requested change touch core economic or control rights?
- What alternatives does the borrower realistically have?

### 5. Downside Path if the Amendment Fails

- Can the business refinance?
- Is there enough collateral or enterprise value to underwrite recovery?
- Would failure likely lead to repeated amendment requests, a workout, or forced sale?

## Private Credit Process

### Step 1: Diagnose the Request

Read the current agreement, identify the exact provision at issue, and separate:

- What the borrower says is needed
- What the business actually needs
- What the lender should require in return

### Step 2: Preserve the Original Underwrite

Ask whether the credit still works if the request is granted:

- Does the lender still receive timely information?
- Are covenants still early-warning tools?
- Has value-leakage capacity expanded?
- Has the amendment changed who really bears downside risk?

### Step 3: Negotiate on More Than Price

Consider:

- Tighter future covenant levels
- Better reporting cadence
- Additional collateral or guarantors
- Stronger cure limitations
- Restrictions on distributions, acquisitions, or additional debt
- Clear milestones if the situation is nearing workout territory

### Step 4: Convert Amendment History Into Surveillance

Track:

- Frequency of amendments
- Drift in EBITDA definitions
- Changes to covenant headroom
- Ownership support or lack of support
- New reporting requirements
- Whether each amendment solved the problem or delayed it

Amendment history belongs in ongoing surveillance, not only in the legal file.

## Red Flags

- Repeated covenant resets without business improvement
- Definition creep that steadily inflates adjusted EBITDA
- Waivers used to excuse recurring underperformance
- New flexibility granted while the borrower still seeks relief
- Sponsor support framed as optional despite clear need
- Amendment requests that coincide with management turnover, liquidity stress, or collateral erosion

## Practical Output

When analyzing an amendment request, summarize:

1. What provision is changing and why
2. Whether the issue is temporary or structural
3. What the lender is giving up
4. What the lender should receive in exchange
5. What this means for future monitoring and recovery assumptions
