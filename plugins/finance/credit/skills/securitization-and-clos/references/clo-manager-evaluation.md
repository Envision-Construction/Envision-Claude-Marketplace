## CLO Manager Evaluation

Selecting the right CLO manager is as important as selecting the right tranche. A good manager protects par, manages migration before defaults crystallize, and behaves rationally when the credit cycle turns. A weak manager can consume structural protection even when the original deal looked sound.

### Track Record Analysis

**1. Relative default control**
- Measure defaults relative to the market environment, not in isolation.
- A useful manager is one that performs better than peers under similar starting conditions.
- Ask whether outperformance persisted in stress periods or only in benign vintages.

**2. Recovery discipline**
- Review how the manager handled real problem credits.
- Strong managers usually cut losses early when the thesis breaks and defend recoveries when a workout is justified.
- Do not confuse a low default count with good recovery skill; both matter.

**3. Par maintenance and trading results**
- Track whether the manager tends to create or destroy par over time.
- Par build is valuable only if it is repeatable and not simply the result of taking hidden tail risk.
- A manager who frequently realizes losses to chase current spread can weaken the structure even while current carry looks attractive.

**4. Portfolio migration management**
- Review how WARF, CCC exposure, and concentration evolved over time.
- A manager who regularly lives near indenture limits should be paid for that additional volatility.
- Improvement in quality metrics is more meaningful when it occurs without sacrificing too much spread or diversification.

**5. Test-cushion management**
- The most useful managers preserve room under OC and IC tests rather than relying on last-minute repairs.
- Review the path of test cushion through both good and bad periods.
- A stable cushion often tells you more about discipline than a single-period return figure.

**6. Cross-vintage consistency**
- Vintage-level returns matter, but only when interpreted with cycle context.
- Ask whether the manager wins because it consistently makes good decisions, or because it happened to launch deals into favorable conditions.
- Consistency across multiple cycles is more valuable than one standout vintage.

### Platform Quality

- **Stability**: Team continuity, succession depth, and repeatability of the investment process matter more than headline scale alone.
- **Research depth**: Sector expertise and credit resources should be proportionate to the breadth of the portfolio.
- **Primary and secondary access**: Better market access helps, but only if the manager uses it with discipline.
- **Operational robustness**: Reporting, surveillance, and workout infrastructure should support the stated investment style.

### Investment Process & Credit Culture

- **Research intensity**: Does the manager actually generate original credit views or mostly follow the market?
- **Sell discipline**: Strong managers admit mistakes early rather than hiding behind recovery optionality.
- **Turnover behavior**: High turnover is not automatically good or bad; what matters is whether trading improves par and risk-adjusted return.
- **Concentration discipline**: Managers should have a clear philosophy on position size, sector concentration, and weaker credits near CCC buckets.
- **Approach to distressed names**: Selective use of stressed credits can be intelligent; habitual dependence on them usually signals a fragile equity case.

### Alignment of Interest
- **Co-investment**: Real capital at risk aligns behavior better than marketing language.
- **Fee design**: Prefer fee structures that reward durable performance rather than maximizing current distributions.
- **Seasoning incentives**: Ask whether the manager remains economically motivated after the deal is no longer new.

### Manager Style Classification

| Style | Typical behavior | What it means for the tranche investor |
|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | Preserves test cushion, trims weaker names early, accepts lower current carry | Lower structural volatility, but less upside for equity in benign markets |
| **Balanced** | Mixes spread capture with risk control, uses flexibility selectively | Often the easiest profile to underwrite because behavior is more repeatable |
| **Aggressive** | Lives close to limits, rotates into weaker spread-heavy collateral, relies more on favorable conditions | Higher tail risk and greater dependence on continued market access |

### Manager Evaluation Integration

When evaluating a CLO tranche investment:

1. **Classify the manager** based on actual behavior, not branding.
2. **Adjust required spread or return** upward when behavior depends on tight cushion, high turnover, or weak credits near structural limits.
3. **Stress the deal under the manager's likely behavior**, not just under static collateral assumptions.
4. **Ask how the manager behaves when optionality disappears**: weak markets reveal whether the process is real.

### Red Flags

- Rapid platform growth without matching research or workout resources
- High turnover with no clear evidence of repeatable par preservation
- Persistent operation near CCC or test limits without a strong historical repair record
- Equity-holder pressure to maximize current distributions at the expense of long-term par protection
