## Quality of Earnings (QoE) Report Interpretation

### What a QoE Report Contains

A Quality of Earnings report is a financial due diligence deliverable typically commissioned by the buyer (or lender) to validate the seller's reported financial performance. Key sections include:

| Section | Purpose | What to Look For |
|---|---|---|
| **Normalized EBITDA Bridge** | Reconciles reported EBITDA to adjusted EBITDA | Size and nature of adjustments; gap between management and QoE-adjusted EBITDA |
| **Revenue Quality Analysis** | Assesses sustainability and composition of revenue | Recurring vs. one-time; customer concentration; contract backlog |
| **Working Capital Analysis** | Determines normalized working capital level (the "peg") | Seasonal adjustments; one-time items; peg reasonableness for purchase price adjustment |
| **Cost Structure Analysis** | Evaluates fixed vs. variable costs; identifies above/below-market items | Owner compensation; related-party costs; run-rate adjustments |
| **Pro Forma Adjustments** | Projects EBITDA impact of recent or planned changes | Synergy estimates; new hires/facilities; contract wins/losses |
| **Accounting Policy Review** | Identifies aggressive or unusual accounting treatments | Revenue recognition policies; capitalization practices; reserve adequacy |
| **Tax Analysis** | Reviews tax positions and potential exposures | NOLs, state tax issues, transfer pricing, audit exposure |

### Key Sections to Scrutinize

#### EBITDA Bridge: The Most Important Page

The EBITDA bridge reconciles reported to management adjusted to QoE adjusted EBITDA:

```
Reported Net Income                           $XX
+ Interest, Taxes, D&A                        $XX
= Reported EBITDA                             $XX
+ Management Adjustments (add-backs)          $XX
= Management Adjusted EBITDA                  $XX
+/- QoE Adjustments (accepted/rejected)       $XX
= QoE Adjusted EBITDA                         $XX
```

**What to Assess:**
- **Gap between Management and QoE EBITDA:** If QoE EBITDA is >10% below management EBITDA, the seller's add-backs are aggressive. Red flag.
- **Which adjustments did the QoE accept vs. reject?** Accepted add-backs are validated as truly non-recurring. Rejected add-backs are either recurring or unsubstantiated.
- **Partially accepted adjustments:** These require judgment. The QoE provider may accept 50% of a restructuring add-back (acknowledging some cost is real but questioning the full amount).
- **Trend in adjustments:** Are add-backs growing as a percentage of EBITDA over time? Growing add-backs suggest the business is increasingly dependent on "non-recurring" items to hit EBITDA targets.

#### Revenue Quality

| Revenue Attribute | Strong Quality | Weak Quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurring %** | >80% contractual/subscription | <50%; project-based or episodic |
| **Customer Concentration** | Top customer <10% of revenue | Top customer >20% of revenue |
| **Contract Backlog** | 12+ months visibility | <6 months visibility |
| **Growth Source** | Organic growth >5% | Growth primarily from acquisitions |
| **Revenue Recognition** | Conservative (recognized on delivery) | Aggressive (percentage of completion, bill-and-hold) |
| **Channel Mix** | Diversified across channels | Single-channel dependent |

**QoE Obligation:** The QoE should explicitly break out organic vs. acquired revenue growth. If not provided, request it — acquired growth inflates headline numbers.

#### Working Capital: The Peg

The working capital "peg" is the normalized level of net working capital the business needs to operate. It's critical because:
- The purchase price is adjusted for actual WC vs. the peg at closing
- Sellers want a LOW peg (minimizes purchase price adjustment)
- Buyers/lenders want a REALISTIC peg (prevents cash extraction at close)

**Red Flags in WC Analysis:**
- Peg set at seasonal low (flatters the seller)
- Peg excludes items that should be included (e.g., accrued liabilities, deferred revenue)
- WC trend shows declining peg year-over-year without operational justification
- Large positive or negative WC adjustment at closing (suggests peg was wrong)

#### Cost Structure

**Owner Compensation Assessment:**
- For non-sponsored (owner-operated) businesses, owner compensation is often above-market
- QoE should assess reasonable compensation level (using market surveys, comparable public company data)
- Excess compensation is a valid add-back (reduces EBITDA on paper but wouldn't exist post-close)
- But: if buyer retains the owner, compensation may not change — the add-back is phantom

**Related-Party Costs:**
- Identify all transactions with owner-related entities (rent from owner's real estate LLC, services from owner's consulting firm)
- QoE should assess whether pricing is at arm's length
- If related-party arrangements will continue post-close, no add-back is justified
- If they will be eliminated, full add-back is appropriate

### Assessing QoE Provider Quality

| Factor | Higher Confidence | Lower Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Provider** | Big 4 / national accounting firm | Regional or boutique firm |
| **Commissioner** | Buyer/lender-commissioned | Seller-commissioned |
| **Scope** | Full-scope QoE with fieldwork | Agreed-upon procedures (limited scope) |
| **Access** | Full access to books, records, management | Limited access; remote review only |
| **Sample Size** | Statistical sample of transactions | Judgmental/convenience sample |
| **Management Interviews** | Conducted with CEO, CFO, controller | Limited or no management access |

**Seller-Commissioned QoE Bias:**
Seller-commissioned QoE reports ("sell-side QoE") tend to:
- Accept more management add-backs than buyer-commissioned reports
- Set working capital pegs at levels favorable to the seller
- Downplay revenue quality concerns
- Include optimistic pro forma adjustments (synergies, new contracts)

**Lender Response:** If only a sell-side QoE is available, apply a 5-10% haircut to QoE-adjusted EBITDA for modeling purposes. Request key supporting schedules (add-back detail, WC build, revenue breakout) for independent verification.

### Red Flags in QoE Reports

| Red Flag | Concern | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Pro forma adjustments >25% of reported EBITDA** | Business fundamentally different from reported | Challenge each adjustment; require documentation |
| **Synergies included without detailed execution plan** | Unsubstantiated value creation | Exclude from credit model; only include in upside scenario |
| **WC peg that minimizes purchase price adjustment** | Potential cash extraction at close | Independent WC analysis; request seasonal WC data |
| **Revenue quality section glosses over concentration** | Hidden customer concentration risk | Request customer-level revenue data |
| **Cost normalizations assume above-market owner comp** | Phantom add-back if owner stays | Verify post-close compensation arrangements |
| **Scope limitations in engagement letter** | Limited reliability | Request expanded scope; flag as information gap in memo |
| **Significant footnotes or caveats in appendices** | Hidden qualifications | Read appendices carefully; flag material caveats |
| **EBITDA add-backs exceed 30% of base EBITDA** | Underlying business performance may be weak | Stress test EBITDA assuming 50% of add-backs don't materialize |
| **Inconsistent data between QoE and management presentation** | Data integrity concern | Reconcile; investigate discrepancies |
| **QoE completed in <2 weeks** | Insufficient diligence time | Request confirmation of scope; may have skipped fieldwork |

### How QoE Feeds Credit Analysis

| QoE Output | Credit Model Input | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| **QoE-Adjusted EBITDA** | Starting point for credit model | Apply additional lender haircut if sell-side QoE (5-10%) |
| **Revenue quality assessment** | Revenue stress scenario calibration | Higher concentration → larger stress decline |
| **Working capital analysis** | Revolver sizing; ABL borrowing base | Use QoE WC peg for normalized WC in model |
| **Cost structure findings** | Operating leverage assumptions | Identify fixed vs. variable for scenario analysis |
| **Pro forma adjustments** | Upside scenario only | Never include in base case; require execution evidence |
| **Accounting policy findings** | EBITDA definition in credit agreement | Align covenant EBITDA definition with QoE findings |
| **Tax positions** | Cash tax rate assumptions | Adjust for NOL utilization, audit exposure |

**Critical Rule:** Use QoE-adjusted EBITDA (not management EBITDA) as the starting point for credit modeling. Then apply additional lender judgment on any adjustments the analyst disagrees with. Never model from management EBITDA without first running through QoE validation.
