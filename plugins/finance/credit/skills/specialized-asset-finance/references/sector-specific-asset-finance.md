## Project Finance by Sector

Project finance uses a common toolkit across sectors, but the true underwriting question changes with the source of repayment. This section highlights what to model by sector and what should make a lender more skeptical.

## Thermal Power

Thermal projects are driven by dispatch economics, fuel supply, and plant performance.

### Focus Areas

- reliability of the offtake structure
- fuel supply continuity and pass-through mechanics
- heat-rate and efficiency risk
- outage risk and maintenance burden

### Key Lender Concern

The plant can be physically sound yet economically weak if dispatch falls or fuel economics move against it.

## Renewable Energy

Renewable projects are driven by resource variability, equipment performance, and the quality of the contracted revenue package.

### Focus Areas

- PPA structure and merchant tail exposure
- resource assessment methodology and downside cases
- curtailment and grid-connection risk
- technology maturity, warranty support, and spare-parts strategy

### Key Lender Concern

Forecast error often arrives through lower generation, curtailment, or a weaker post-contract revenue tail rather than obvious operating failure.

## Toll Roads and Demand-Risk Infrastructure

Demand-risk transport assets are driven by traffic or throughput assumptions rather than contracted availability payments.

### Focus Areas

- realism of traffic ramp-up and competing-route assumptions
- tariff escalation and political enforceability
- downside elasticity in recession or route substitution
- whether support mechanisms genuinely transfer demand risk

### Key Lender Concern

These assets often fail through forecast optimism rather than legal-document weakness.

## Social Infrastructure and Availability-Based PPP

Availability-based projects are closer to contract underwriting than traffic underwriting because payment depends on service availability rather than end-user volume.

### Focus Areas

- government or public-counterparty credit
- deduction regime for service-performance failures
- handback obligations and lifecycle maintenance
- interface between construction contractor and facilities manager

### Key Lender Concern

The largest risk is often not demand but whether deductions, handback obligations, or public-counterparty behavior erode what looks like a stable revenue stream.

## Cross-Sector Project Finance Use

For any sector, use this reference to ask:

1. Is repayment driven by contracted payment, regulated tariff, merchant exposure, or user demand?
2. Which variable is hardest to control: price, volume, availability, fuel, or regulation?
3. Does the downside case come from operating underperformance, weaker contracts, or a false assumption about exit or refinance value?

## Sector-Specific Asset Finance

Asset finance is not one market. The same lender protections can behave very differently depending on whether the collateral is an aircraft, a vessel, or rolling stock. Use this section to identify what is truly sector-specific and what is just generic leverage translated into a new asset class.

## Aircraft Finance

Aircraft underwriting depends on three things at once:

- operator credit and route economics
- maintenance condition and technical status
- global remarketing depth for the specific aircraft type and configuration

### Focus Areas

- **Type liquidity**: Standard, widely used platforms are easier to remarket than niche or aging configurations.
- **Maintenance status**: Upcoming heavy checks, engine overhauls, and return-condition obligations can materially change value.
- **Jurisdiction and repossession**: Registration, local enforcement, and treaty effectiveness matter in real recovery scenarios.
- **Lease or charter structure**: Revenue support is stronger when contract terms and counterparties survive stress and align with debt tenor.

### Typical Risks

- airline credit deterioration
- type obsolescence
- maintenance underinvestment
- regulatory or environmental restrictions
- remarketing delays after repossession

## Shipping Finance

Shipping finance is highly cycle-sensitive because vessel values, charter rates, and borrower liquidity can all deteriorate at the same time.

### Focus Areas

- **Charter coverage**: Distinguish contracted charter income from spot-rate exposure.
- **Vessel quality**: Age, specification, efficiency, and maintenance condition all affect both earnings power and resale value.
- **Refinancing path**: Balloon structures are especially exposed if maturity arrives during a downcycle.
- **Regulatory compliance**: Technical and environmental rules can accelerate obsolescence or require retrofit capex.

### Typical Risks

- freight-rate cyclicality
- vessel value volatility
- charterer default
- sanctions and geopolitical disruption
- technical casualty or prolonged off-hire periods

## Rolling Stock and Rail Finance

Rolling stock often has longer useful lives and steadier secondary markets than aircraft or vessels, but it still requires asset-specific discipline.

### Focus Areas

- **Utilization and lease placement**: Idle assets may retain residual value yet still fail to support debt service.
- **Standardization**: Generic fleets are easier to re-lease than assets tailored to a single operator or route.
- **Maintenance planning**: Long-lived assets can hide underinvestment for years before value suddenly deteriorates.
- **Regulatory compatibility**: Safety, braking, tank, or routing rules can segment the secondary market.

### Typical Risks

- prolonged idle time
- maintenance backlog
- mismatch between debt tenor and lease tenor
- concentration in one car type, operator, or commodity flow

2. Does maintenance preserve value, or just delay value impairment?
3. Does the end market remain liquid when the obligor is weak?
4. Are debt tenor and residual assumptions matched to real asset behavior rather than headline useful life?
