## Asset Valuation for Lending

Valuation for lending is not the same as valuation for sale. The lender cares about what can be realized under the expected enforcement path, after time, cost, and execution risk are considered.

## Valuation Hierarchy

### Fair Market Value

Use fair market value when the asset can be sold through an ordinary marketing process with reasonable time and competitive tension. FMV is useful as a reference point, but it is not automatically the right lending value.

### Orderly Liquidation Value

Use orderly liquidation value when the lender expects a controlled sale with some marketing time but without full strategic-buyer optionality. OLV is often the most relevant starting point for asset-backed advance-rate analysis.

### Forced Liquidation Value

Use forced liquidation value when the likely recovery path involves speed, distress, limited buyer outreach, or significant uncertainty. FLV is the right lens when the lender could be selling into stress rather than through a normal process.

### Scrap or Salvage Value

Use scrap or salvage value only when the asset's operating value has largely disappeared. This is the floor case, not the base case.

## Lending Implications

### Advance Against Realization Value, Not Headline Value

Advance rates should be applied to the value base that best matches the expected workout path. The more specialized, volatile, or execution-sensitive the asset, the less weight the lender should place on FMV.

### Match Amortization to Value Decay

Collateral support is dynamic. A sound starting LTV can become weak if the asset depreciates or the loan amortizes too slowly. Underwrite the path of LTV, not just the day-one point.

### Separate Asset Value from Recovery Value

Recovery is not just appraised value. It is:

`recoverable value = estimated sale proceeds - realization costs - timing drag - condition risk`

The wider the gap between appraised value and recoverable value, the less comfort the appraisal provides.

## Appraisal Discipline

Use appraisals to inform judgment, not replace it.

### When to Require More Rigor

Increase appraisal rigor when:

- the asset is large or concentrated
- value is highly cyclical
- condition materially affects remarketing
- covenant or availability tests depend on valuation updates

### What to Test in Every Appraisal

- appraiser expertise for the asset class
- valuation basis used and why it fits the enforcement case
- assumptions on market depth, marketing time, and condition
- sensitivity to age, maintenance, and regulation
- dispersion across independent opinions, where multiple appraisals are available

## Underwriting Questions

Use this reference to answer:

1. Which value basis matches the lender's likely downside path?
2. How quickly can the asset be sold without destroying value?
3. What conditions or expenditures are required to realize the appraised value?
4. Does the debt amortize fast enough to stay inside a defensible recovery range?
