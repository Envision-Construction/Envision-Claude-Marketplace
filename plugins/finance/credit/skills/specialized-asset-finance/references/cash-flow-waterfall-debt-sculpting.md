## Cash Flow Waterfall and Debt Sculpting

The waterfall is the operating rulebook for project cash. It decides whether cash first protects the asset, the lender, or the equity story. A sound structure should preserve operations, keep debt current, and trap cash automatically when the project weakens.

## Core Waterfall Logic

In most project structures, cash is applied in this order:

1. **Essential operating costs** needed to keep the asset legal, safe, and functional
2. **Senior debt service** in the order required by the finance documents
3. **Reserve funding and reserve top-ups**
4. **Mandatory sweeps or prepayments** triggered by coverage, reserve, or default tests
5. **Junior debt service**, if permitted
6. **Equity distributions** only after all prior conditions are satisfied

The underwriting issue is not just whether each item exists, but whether the order changes lender outcomes during stress.

## Reserve Design Principles

Reserve accounts are part of the waterfall, not side notes.

### Debt Service Reserve Account

Use a debt service reserve account to protect against timing gaps or temporary underperformance between scheduled payment dates. Focus the analysis on:

- what events allow a draw
- how the account is replenished after use
- whether it is funded at close, built over time, or both
- whether current market practice supports the proposed sizing

### Maintenance Reserve Account

Use a maintenance reserve when the asset requires periodic heavy upkeep or lifecycle capex to preserve both operations and collateral value. Focus the analysis on:

- whether the engineering plan and reserve plan are consistent
- who controls reserve draws
- whether deferred maintenance can be hidden inside the forecast
- whether the reserve protects the exit value, not just the next operating period

### Other Reserves

Depending on the structure, the lender may also require:

- contingency reserves
- insurance deductibles or premium reserves
- tax reserves
- working-capital or ramp-up support reserves

The question is always the same: does the reserve absorb a real failure mode, or is it only documentary comfort?

## Distribution Lock-Up and Sweep Logic

Equity should receive cash only when the structure remains sound. Distribution tests usually examine:

- coverage ratios
- reserve sufficiency
- absence of default
- compliance with key project contracts

Sweeps and mandatory prepayments should be analyzed as automatic deleveraging tools. If a sweep activates only after the project is already impaired, it may provide less protection than the documentation suggests.

## Debt Sculpting

Debt sculpting aligns scheduled principal repayment with projected CFADS so the structure can target a desired level of debt service coverage through the loan life.

Conceptually:

`Principal in period t = debt service capacity in period t - interest in period t`

Use sculpting when projected cash flows ramp, fluctuate, or seasonally vary. It can improve debt capacity, but it also increases dependence on forecast accuracy.

### Compare Repayment Profiles

| Approach | What It Does | Strength | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sculpted | Varies principal with projected CFADS | Matches debt to project economics | Over-relies on forecast precision |
| Level debt service | Keeps total payments broadly stable | Easier to monitor and explain | May not fit weak early cash flow |
| Straight-line amortization | Pays similar principal each period | Faster deleveraging and simple tracking | Can overburden ramp-up periods |

## Underwriting Questions

Use this reference to answer:

1. Does the waterfall preserve the asset before it preserves equity?
2. Are reserve accounts designed around real risks rather than documentary habit?
3. Do lock-up and sweep mechanics activate early enough to matter?
4. Does the debt profile match the project's cash-generation pattern, or is the model borrowing from a future that may not arrive?
