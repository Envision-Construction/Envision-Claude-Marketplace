## Construction & Development Lending

Construction underwriting should separate two questions that are often blurred together:

1. **Can the project be completed and stabilized?**
2. **Will stabilized value and cash flow justify the capital invested and the debt proposed?**

This note covers both value-add and ground-up situations. Use `references/typical-deal-parameters.md` for current leverage, rate, reserve, and lender-term conventions. Use `references/stress-scenario-framework.md` for current scenario severity.

### 1. Deal Type Framing

| Deal Type | Existing Cash Flow | Main Value Driver | Primary Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light Value-Add** | Strong but improvable | Rent mark-to-market, modest capex, expense cleanup | Execution risk on renovations and lease rollover |
| **Heavy Value-Add / Repositioning** | Present but weak or unstable | Lease-up, re-tenanting, major capex, operating reset | Business-plan risk and prolonged stabilization |
| **Ground-Up Development** | None | Deliver project on budget and lease from zero | Construction, lease-up, and take-out risk |
| **Redevelopment / Change of Use** | Sometimes limited | Re-entitle or repurpose obsolete asset | Construction plus entitlement and demand risk |

The more a thesis depends on future execution rather than present income, the less weight an underwriter should place on sponsor upside and the more weight should move to controls, reserves, and downside funding capacity.

### 2. Total Project Cost Discipline

Underwriting should begin with a full sources-and-uses view rather than a headline construction budget.

```
Total Project Cost
  = acquisition or land basis
  + hard costs
  + soft costs
  + contingency
  + interest reserve / carry
  + lease-up or operating deficit reserve
  + tenanting costs, TI/LC, and commissions
  + taxes, insurance, and closing costs
  + any subordinated fee or sponsor-funded overrun support
```

### 3. Budget Review Principles

| Budget Line | What To Validate | Common Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard costs** | Scope completeness, bid support, contract structure, site complexity | Missing scope, aggressive assumptions, change-order dependence |
| **Soft costs** | Permits, consultants, legal, financing, insurance, leasing | Underbudgeted professional costs and timing drag |
| **Contingency** | Appropriate to project complexity and unknowns | Insufficient buffer for adaptive reuse or difficult sites |
| **Interest reserve** | Sized to realistic draw curve, rate stress, and delay cushion | Reserve exhaustion before completion or stabilization |
| **Lease-up reserve** | Covers downtime, concessions, payroll, and operating deficit | Assumes instant stabilization once construction ends |
| **Developer fee / sponsor economics** | Subordination, cash timing, and skin in the game | Fees extracted before risk is burned off |

### 4. Draw, Reserve, and Control Structure

Construction lending is as much about control architecture as pricing.

- **Draw process**: Monthly or milestone-based draws should be tied to verified work in place.
- **Independent oversight**: Use an independent engineer or cost consultant to validate budget, schedule, draw progress, and change orders.
- **Retainage**: Hold back a portion of each draw until substantial completion and lien clearance.
- **Interest reserve**: Size for scheduled duration plus delay cushion; stress both timeline and floating-rate exposure.
- **Contingency access**: Define who controls contingency release and when sponsor equity must fund overruns first.
- **Completion support**: Completion guarantees, carry guarantees, or liquidity covenants should protect the lender if the business plan slips.

### 5. Construction and Execution Risk

| Risk | What It Means | Preferred Mitigant |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost overrun** | Budget no longer funds delivery | GMP or well-governed contract, contingency, sponsor overrun support |
| **Timeline delay** | Reserve burn and take-out timing deteriorate | Schedule cushion, liquidated damages, extension planning |
| **Contractor failure** | Need to replace GC midstream | Bonding, lender step-in rights, replacement protocol |
| **Lien risk** | Unpaid parties impair title | Lien waivers, title updates, retainage discipline |
| **Entitlement / permit risk** | Project cannot proceed as planned | Close only after critical approvals are in hand |
| **Environmental or site surprises** | Hidden remediation or foundation cost | Site investigation, geotech, environmental diligence |
| **Lease-up risk** | Project completes but income lags | Conservative absorption, marketing budget, reserve for operating deficit |
| **Rate / take-out risk** | Permanent financing unavailable on expected terms | Forward take-out options, lower leverage, refinance stress testing |

### 6. Value-Add Underwriting

Value-add underwriting should separate **as-is**, **post-capex**, and **stabilized** cases. Do not collapse them into one blended pro forma.

#### Core questions

- What is the current earning power of the asset without sponsor intervention?
- What specific work creates value: physical renovation, re-tenanting, lease restructuring, expense cleanup, or repositioning?
- Which assumptions are observable today, and which depend on market acceptance later?
- What happens if the project reaches only partial stabilization or takes materially longer?

#### Value creation bridge

```
As-stabilized value
  - as-is basis
  - renovation and carrying costs
  - leasing costs
  - contingency
  = implied value creation
```

The bridge only has underwriting value if each assumption can be tied to evidence from rent comps, absorption, turnover costs, and realistic renovation scope.

### 7. Ground-Up Development Underwriting

Ground-up development has no in-place income, so underwriting hinges on whether stabilized cash flow can absorb the capital stack after construction and lease-up risk.

#### Underwrite in three phases

1. **Construction phase**: Can the project be built with current approvals, budget, contractor, and reserve structure?
2. **Lease-up phase**: Can the market absorb the delivered product at underwritten rents and concessions?
3. **Stabilized phase**: Does stabilized NOI support permanent debt and justify development yield over market cap rates?

#### Development yield

```
Development Yield = Stabilized NOI / Total Project Cost
```

Development yield should be judged relative to exit cap rates and the project's risk profile, not in isolation. A spread to market cap is only meaningful if the stabilized NOI itself is credible.

### 8. Conversion and Exit

| Exit Path | What Must Be True | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Construction-to-perm** | Completion and stabilization tests are clearly defined | Borrower misses conversion triggers |
| **Mini-perm** | Asset can operate while lease-up finishes | Balloon risk before full stabilization |
| **External take-out** | Permanent lender terms remain available | Market moves or take-out conditions fail |
| **Sale on stabilization** | Exit liquidity and valuation are credible | Cap-rate widening or weaker demand at delivery |

Typical conversion tests should include completion, lien and title cleanup, required occupancy or leasing threshold, environmental clearance, and a debt-serviceability test on stabilized cash flow.

### 9. Underwriting Outputs

A complete construction or transitional underwriting should show:

1. **Sources and uses**
2. **Draw and reserve logic**
3. **Construction and lease-up timeline**
4. **As-is, partially stabilized, and stabilized NOI where relevant**
5. **Development yield or value-creation bridge**
6. **Permanent take-out sizing and refinance risk**
7. **Downside case showing additional equity need, reserve shortfall, or failed conversion**

### 10. Common Mistakes

- Treating sponsor business plan as if it were current income
- Underwriting one completion date with no delay cushion
- Using optimistic stabilization rents without concession or downtime assumptions
- Ignoring the path from construction debt to permanent debt
- Counting contingency and sponsor liquidity twice
- Assuming value-add and ground-up deserve the same leverage simply because both are "transitional"
