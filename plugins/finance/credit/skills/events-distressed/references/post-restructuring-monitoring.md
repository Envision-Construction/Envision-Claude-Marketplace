# Post-Restructuring Monitoring Framework

An emerged company is a new credit, not a repaired version of the old one. Post-restructuring monitoring should rebuild the thesis from the confirmed capital structure, fresh-start financials, and the operational realities created by the restructuring process.

> Cross-reference: Use `references/escalation-trigger-thresholds.md` and the `surveillance-monitoring` skill for current escalation definitions and numeric threshold calibration.

## 1. Re-Baseline the Thesis

Start over rather than patching the pre-petition thesis.

- Map the new debt, equity, warrants, management incentives, and governance structure
- Rebuild leverage, coverage, liquidity, and maturity analysis from the post-emergence capital structure
- Separate confirmed plan economics from management aspirations and turnaround hopes
- Define what must go right for the new credit to work

## 2. Reset the Financial Model

- Treat emergence as a new starting point for trend analysis
- Rebuild the model from fresh-start or post-reorganization financial statements
- Remove restructuring noise from recurring earnings, but do not assume all disruption is temporary
- Test downside cases independently rather than relying on disclosure-statement projections

## 3. Focus on the First Sources of Failure

Post-emergence credits most often fail because one of these breaks early:

- **Liquidity**: initial cash cushion proves inadequate or new revolver capacity is more limited than expected
- **Operations**: cost savings, customer retention, or volume recovery do not materialize
- **Governance**: new owners, creditor-appointed boards, or management teams push a different strategy
- **Capital allocation**: re-leveraging, acquisitions, dividends, or new secured debt appear too early
- **Stakeholder confidence**: customers, vendors, employees, and counterparties do not fully normalize after emergence

## 4. Build Thesis-Kill Triggers

Every post-restructuring position should have explicit triggers tied to the new thesis:

- Revenue or EBITDA materially below re-based expectations
- Liquidity running materially below plan
- Unexpected management turnover or board conflict
- Re-leveraging behavior inconsistent with the emergence case
- Customer or supplier attrition that undermines the recovery narrative
- Delay or failure of the key operational actions that justified the restructuring case

Document each trigger with:

1. Measurable threshold
2. Detection method
3. Monitoring frequency
4. Escalation mapping into `surveillance-monitoring`

## 5. Monitoring Priorities

### Financial

- Compare actual performance against the new base case, not the old capital structure
- Watch liquidity first, then margin recovery, then leverage trajectory
- Reassess any take-back paper, warrants, or contingent instruments as the story evolves

### Operational

- Track whether the company is delivering the few operational fixes that justified emergence value
- Look for deferred maintenance, delayed capex, or underinvestment disguised as temporary prudence
- Monitor customer retention, vendor terms, and employee stability

### Governance and Ownership

- Understand who now controls strategy and what their monetization path may be
- Watch for MIP changes, sponsor exits, board turnover, or aggressive financing behavior
- Re-underwrite incentives whenever control shifts

## 6. Handoff to Surveillance

Before handing the credit to ongoing surveillance, provide:

- New capital structure map
- Fresh thesis and downside case
- Thesis-kill trigger set
- Key contacts, milestones, and documentation constraints
- Specific risks created by the restructuring rather than resolved by it

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Treating emergence as the end of the work rather than the start of a new credit case
- Comparing post-emergence ratios directly with pre-petition ratios without resetting the base
- Assuming management projections from the plan are the correct base case
- Failing to define explicit triggers for re-leveraging, liquidity slippage, or operational miss
- Handing off to surveillance without documenting what would falsify the thesis
