# Credit Agreement Trends & Documentation Risk

**Last Updated:** March 2026
**Update Cadence:** Annually

---

### Covenant Erosion Cycle

In strong credit markets (tight spreads, high demand), documentation quality deteriorates — investors accept weaker protections to participate. In stressed markets, the pendulum swings back. Track the documentation cycle:

- **2005–2007: Peak Erosion**: Aggressive LBO financing; minimal covenants; broad baskets; unlimited debt incurrence; weak restricted payments controls
- **2008–2009 GFC Reset**: Market froze; survivors demanded stringent documentation; tight baskets; conservative leverage tests; mandatory prepayment triggers
- **2013–2019: Gradual Re-Erosion**: Credit markets reopened; demand for yield drove investor appetite for weaker terms; cov-lite became standard for large cap HY; loan covenant standards loosened
- **2020 COVID Reset**: Short spike in tight terms and enhanced protections; rapid reversal as central banks flooded markets with liquidity
- **2021–2023: Aggressive Re-Erosion**: Record cov-lite issuance; unlimited ratio debt baskets; broad free-and-clear baskets; aggressive equity distribution formulas; minimal upside protection

**Implications for Credit Analysts**: Vintage matters significantly. A 2006-vintage high-yield bond has vastly different documentation protections than a 2019-vintage equivalent. When analyzing credit, always review the documentation, not just the credit metrics. Weak documentation can make a seemingly solid credit risky in a downturn.

### Key Documentation Weaknesses to Flag

**Unlimited "Ratio Debt" Baskets**
- Permits additional pari passu debt if a leverage test is met *at the time of incurrence*, even if the company subsequently deteriorates
- Example: Company with 4.0x leverage incurs $100M new debt. Later, company's EBITDA falls 30%; now at 5.5x leverage, but new debt already issued
- Risk: Creditors diluted by subsequent leverage deterioration
- Protection: Ratio debt basket should have low leverage thresholds (2.5x–3.0x max) and sunset provisions (basket expires after 2–3 years)

**Large "Free and Clear" Baskets**
- Permit incremental debt without any leverage test; typically capped at the greater of fixed amount ($X million) or Y% of EBITDA
- Example: "Free-and-clear basket: up to $50M or 10% of consolidated EBITDA"
- Risk: Company can issue $100M+ new unsecured debt without any covenant control
- Protection: Tight caps on free-and-clear baskets relative to company size; preferably require leverage test or mandatory prepayment triggers

**Generous EBITDA Addback Definitions**
- Addbacks for cost savings, synergies, "run-rate" adjustments, restructuring charges
- Example: "Permitted to add back anticipated annual cost savings of up to $75M for 24 months post-acquisition, even if not yet realized"
- Risk: Inflated EBITDA overstates true credit quality; allows higher debt incurrence on artificial metrics
- Protection: Addbacks should be capped at realized/run-rate only, with conservative sunset periods (12 months max) and clear documentation of what qualifies

**Broad Restricted Payments Baskets**
- Allow significant equity distributions (dividends, buybacks) even at high leverage
- Example: "Permitted Payments = (a) cumulative EBITDA since inception minus (b) debt repaid; may distribute amount A-B so long as leverage <4.0x"
- Risk: Management distributes cash to equity holders instead of deleveraging during downturns
- Protection: Tie distributions to leverage thresholds; include negative covenants that prevent distributions if leverage >3.5x or interest coverage <2.5x

**Loose Investment Baskets** ("J.Crew" Risk)
- Enable transfer of material assets to unrestricted subsidiaries without meaningful limitations
- Example: "Permitted to transfer assets valued up to 15% of consolidated total assets to unrestricted subsidiaries"
- Risk: Borrower transfers key IP, production capacity, or revenue-generating assets outside the collateral package, eroding lender security
- Historical example: J.Crew moved valuable real estate holdings to unrestricted subsidiary; when distressed, lenders lacked full recourse to those assets
- Protection: Cap asset transfers to unrestricted subsidiaries at low thresholds (5% max) or require that key assets (IP, real estate, core operations) remain restricted

**Limited "Blocker" Provisions** ("Serta" Risk)
- Fail to prevent subordination of existing debt via uptier exchange or refinancing
- Example: Company refinances term loan B due 2028 with new term loan A due 2026; new term loan is senior lien, old term loan becomes junior lien
- Risk: Existing creditors suffer mechanical dilution; their security position weakened without consent
- Historical example: Serta Simmons (2020-21) conducted uptier exchanges that subordinated existing term loan B holders to new term loan A; lenders had no contractual protection
- Protection: Require unanimous or supermajority consent for any transaction that subordinates existing lenders' claims or liens

### Anti-LME Provisions (Post-2020 Evolution)

The market's response to 2020-2023 distressed situations has been hardening of anti-LME protections:

**Anti-Serta / Uptier Protection**
- Require unanimous or supermajority consent for any transaction that subordinates existing lenders' claims or liens
- Includes: refinancing to new senior debt, exchange offers that create new senior tranches, collateral releases that benefit junior creditors
- Mechanism: "No amendment shall permit subordination of existing liens without consent of holders of [100% / 75%] of term loans"

**Anti-J.Crew / Dropdown Protection**
- Cap the value of assets transferable to unrestricted subsidiaries
- Require that material IP, collateral, and key revenue-generating assets remain within restricted group
- Mechanism: "Cannot transfer assets >5% of consolidated total assets to unrestricted subsidiaries. All Intellectual Property and collateral must be pledged to lenders."

**Enhanced Sacred Rights**
- Expand the list of amendments requiring unanimous consent (beyond standard sacred rights like maturity, interest rate, principal reduction)
- Include: lien subordination, guarantee release, pro-rata sharing changes, collateral release, material covenant modification
- Purpose: Prevent narrow creditor coalitions from forcing unfavorable restructurings on dissenting lenders

**"Blocker" Provisions** (Anti-Exchange Mechanics)
- Prevent non-pro-rata exchanges by requiring any open market purchases or exchanges be offered to all lenders on the same terms
- Mechanism: "Any tender offer or exchange offer must be offered to all lenders. If lender rejects, any consenting lender accepting different terms must offer same terms to dissenting lender, or deal fails."
- Purpose: Prevents ad hoc groups from extracting favorable terms while others are left with worse debt

**Minimum Lender Consent Thresholds**
- Raise required consent levels for material amendments from simple majority (>50%) to supermajority (66.67%–75%)
- Protects minority lenders from being overridden by coalitions

### Documentation Review Checklist for Credit Investors

When analyzing leveraged credit, use this checklist to assess documentation quality and identify risk gaps:

1. **EBITDA Definition & Addbacks**: What qualifies as "Consolidated EBITDA"? What addbacks are permitted? Are there caps and sunset periods? (Conservative: no addbacks for anticipated cost savings; 12-month realization requirement)

2. **Incremental Debt Capacity**: How much additional debt can be raised? What are ratio debt baskets (leverage thresholds)? What are free-and-clear baskets (fixed amounts)? (Conservative: ratio debt at 3.0x max; free-and-clear <5% of EBITDA)

3. **Asset Transfers**: Can the company move assets to unrestricted subsidiaries? What are the limitations? Are key IP and collateral protected? (Conservative: <5% of assets; key IP/collateral remain restricted)

4. **Sacred Rights**: What amendments require unanimous vs majority consent? Does the agreement specifically protect against uptier exchanges and lien subordination? (Conservative: unanimous consent required for any subordination)

5. **Open Market Purchase Provision**: Is there a blocker that prevents non-pro-rata exchanges? Or can narrow creditor groups extract favorable terms? (Conservative: blocker provision present; any exchange offered to all on same terms)

6. **Anti-Serta / Anti-J.Crew Protections**: Are there specific provisions preventing uptier exchanges and dropdown transactions? (Conservative: explicit 100% consent required for subordination; asset transfer caps <5%)

7. **Restricted Payments Baskets**: What are the distributions allowed even at high leverage? Are there leverage thresholds that tighten restrictions? (Conservative: distributions blocked if leverage >3.5x or interest coverage <2.5x)

8. **Collateral Package**: How is collateral defined? Is there leakage risk? Can collateral be released or pledged to junior creditors? (Conservative: first lien on all material assets; release requires lender consent)

9. **Most Favored Nation (MFN) Protections**: If company raises incremental debt with better terms, do existing lenders get same terms? (Conservative: MFN applies to all material terms; or ratio debt limited to minimal MFN issues)

10. **Governing Law & Dispute Resolution**: What is the governing law? Can disputes be arbitrated, or must they go to court? Does the agreement waive jury trial rights? (Standard: New York law; federal court jurisdiction; no arbitration for credit agreements)
