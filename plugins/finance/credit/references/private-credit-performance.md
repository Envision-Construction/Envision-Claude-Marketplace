## Portfolio KPIs and Rate Sensitivity

### Key Performance Indicators for Private Debt Portfolios

When analyzing a private credit portfolio (BDC, direct lending fund, or CLO), these metrics are essential:

**Yield Metrics**
- **Weighted Average Current Yield**: Weighted average coupon/spread across portfolio positions. For current thresholds, see `references/market-benchmarks.md`
- **Loss-Adjusted Yield**: Current yield minus expected annual loss rate. True expected return metric. For current benchmarks, see Section 1 (Return Benchmarks by Strategy) above
- **Fee-Adjusted Net Yield**: Loss-adjusted yield minus management/incentive fees. What LP actually earns. For current ranges, see Section 1 (Return Benchmarks by Strategy) above

**Credit Quality Metrics**
- **Weighted Average Leverage**: Aggregate Debt/EBITDA across portfolio. For current ranges, see `references/rating-agency-thresholds.md`
- **Weighted Average Interest Coverage**: Aggregate EBITDA/Interest. Should be >2.0x for senior portfolios. Rising rates compress coverage — monitor carefully.
- **% on Non-Accrual**: Percentage of portfolio not currently paying interest. For current benchmarks, see Section 3 (Credit Quality Metrics) above
- **PIK Income as % of Total Income**: High PIK percentage (>15-20%) may signal deteriorating credit quality (borrowers unable to pay cash interest)
- **Realized Loss Rate**: Cumulative realized losses / cumulative deployed capital. For current benchmarks by tranche, see `references/default-recovery-rates.md`

**Portfolio Health Metrics**
- **Fair Value / Cost Basis (Mark-to-Market Ratio)**: Portfolio fair value ÷ amortized cost. Should be 97-100%+ for healthy portfolio. Below 95% signals meaningful credit deterioration
- **Unrealized Depreciation / NAV**: Total unrealized losses as % of NAV. BDC benchmark: <5% healthy; 5-10% elevated; >10% significant stress
- **Amendments / Waivers as % of Portfolio**: Number of positions requiring covenant amendments in past 12 months. >10% suggests loosening credit quality
- **Watchlist %**: Positions on credit watch / heightened monitoring. Benchmark: <5%
- **Diversification**: Number of positions, industry concentration (no single industry >15%), single-name concentration (no position >3% of NAV)

**Comparison Benchmarks**

| Metric | Top Quartile | Median | Bottom Quartile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net IRR (Direct Lending) | >10% | 8-10% | <7% |
| Realized Loss Rate | <0.5% | 0.5-1.5% | >2.0% |
| Non-Accrual Rate | <1% | 1-3% | >4% |
| MOIC | >1.25x | 1.10-1.25x | <1.10x |
| PIK % of Income | <5% | 5-15% | >20% |

### Rate Sensitivity Analysis for Floating-Rate Portfolios

Private debt is overwhelmingly floating-rate (SOFR-based). Rising rates directly impact borrower coverage:

**SOFR Impact on Borrower Interest Coverage**
```
Scenario: $100M EBITDA company, $400M debt at SOFR + 500 bps

SOFR = 1.0%: Interest = $400M × 6.0% = $24M → Coverage = 4.2x
SOFR = 3.0%: Interest = $400M × 8.0% = $32M → Coverage = 3.1x
SOFR = 5.0%: Interest = $400M × 10.0% = $40M → Coverage = 2.5x
SOFR = 7.0%: Interest = $400M × 12.0% = $48M → Coverage = 2.1x
```

**Key insight**: A 400 bps rise in SOFR cuts interest coverage nearly in half. Lenders must stress-test portfolios for rate scenarios.

**Floor Value**: SOFR floors (typically 0.50-1.00%) provide lender protection when rates are low but are irrelevant when SOFR exceeds floor. In rising rate environments, floors have no protective value for borrowers — they only help lenders in low-rate scenarios.
