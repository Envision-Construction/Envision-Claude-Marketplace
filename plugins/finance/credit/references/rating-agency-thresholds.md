| Rating | Moody's Scale | S&P Scale | Total Debt/EBITDA | Net Debt/EBITDA |
|--------|---------------|-----------|------------------|-----------------|
| Aaa/AAA | Aaa | AAA | <1.5x | <0.5x |
| Aa1-Aa3 | Aa | AA | 1.5x - 2.0x | 0.5x - 1.0x |
| A1-A3 | A | A | 2.0x - 2.5x | 1.0x - 1.5x |
| Baa1-Baa3 | Baa | BBB | 2.5x - 3.5x | 1.5x - 2.5x |

**Notes:**
- Moody's and S&P scales are broadly equivalent across rating bands
- Ranges reflect median practices across industrial corporates; utilities typically accept higher leverage
- Private company benchmarks generally tighten by 0.3x - 0.5x due to volatility discount

## 2. Interest Coverage Thresholds

| Rating | EBITDA/Interest (x) | (EBITDA - CapEx)/Interest (x) | FCF Interest Coverage (x) |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|
| AAA | >12.0x | >10.0x | >8.0x |
| AA | 10.0x - 12.0x | 8.0x - 10.0x | 6.0x - 8.0x |
| A | 6.5x - 10.0x | 5.0x - 8.0x | 4.0x - 6.0x |
| BBB | 3.5x - 6.5x | 2.5x - 5.0x | 2.0x - 4.0x |
| BB | 2.0x - 3.5x | 1.2x - 2.5x | 1.0x - 2.0x |
| B | 1.2x - 2.0x | 0.8x - 1.2x | <1.0x (often negative) |
| CCC | <1.2x | <0.8x | Negative |

**Notes:**
- EBITDA/Interest is primary metric; operating lease coverage typically adds 10-15% to EBITDA
- Below 2.5x EBITDA/Interest signals elevated refinancing risk regardless of other metrics
- Agencies apply covenant stress testing (typically 10-15% EBITDA decline scenario)

## 3. Cash Flow Metrics by Rating

| Rating | FCF/Total Debt | FFO/Total Debt | Retained Cash Flow/Net Debt |
|--------|----------------|----------------|------------------------------|
| AAA | >30% | >60% | >40% |
| AA | 20% - 30% | 45% - 60% | 30% - 40% |
| A | 12% - 20% | 30% - 45% | 20% - 30% |
| BBB | 6% - 12% | 20% - 30% | 10% - 20% |
| BB | 2% - 6% | 10% - 20% | 0% - 10% |
| B | <2% | <10% | Negative to 0% |
| CCC | Negative | <5% | Highly Negative |

**Definitions:**
- **FCF** = EBITDA - Cash Interest - CapEx - Taxes - Working Capital Changes
- **FFO** = Funds From Operations (operating cash flow metric)
- **RCF** = FCF - Dividends - Share Repurchases

**Notes:**
- S&P and Moody's weight FCF generation heavily for stability of rating
- Negative RCF acceptable at BB+ and above if leverage deleveraging on track
- Strong FCF generation (>20% of debt) can support rating despite moderate leverage

## 4. Upgrade/Downgrade Triggers

### Quantitative Triggers

**Downgrade Indicators:**
- Total Debt/EBITDA breaches rating band ceiling by >0.3x for 2 consecutive quarters
- EBITDA/Interest falls below 2.0x (all ratings) or rating-specific floor by >0.5x
- Net Debt/EBITDA negative FCF for 3+ consecutive quarters
- Liquidity cushion (available cash + revolver capacity) falls below 12 months of debt service
- Leverage spike >1.0x above peer median within 12 months

**Upgrade Indicators:**
- Sustained leverage improvement: 2+ consecutive years of 0.3x+ annual reduction
- Coverage metrics exceed rating band high-end by >25% sustained over 4+ quarters
- FCF generation >20% of total debt on sustainable basis
- Peer leverage and coverage position moves to top quartile of rating cohort
- Liquidity improves to 18+ months of debt service with no negative FCF

### Qualitative Triggers

**Downgrade Catalysts:**
- Loss of major customer (>15% revenue concentration)
- Unexpected significant M&A without simultaneous deleveraging
- Refinancing risk: debt maturity wall with limited capital access
- Competitive position erosion; market share loss >10% YoY
- Management/governance change signaling materially different strategy
- Regulatory or legal developments materially reducing cash flow

**Upgrade Catalysts:**
- Market share/competitive position strengthening
- Successful integration of strategic acquisition accretive to EBITDA
- Voluntary debt reduction accelerating above base case trajectory
- Improved operational efficiency/EBITDA margin expansion
- Shift to stronger end-market fundamentals (industry tailwinds)

## 5. Investment Grade vs High Yield Threshold

### BBB-/Baa3 Boundary (IG/HY Divide)

| Metric | BBB- (Minimum IG) | BB+ (Lower HY) | Wedge Factor |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Total Debt/EBITDA | <3.5x | 3.5x - 4.2x | ±0.3x - 0.5x |
| EBITDA/Interest | >3.5x | 2.5x - 3.5x | ±0.3x - 0.5x |
| Net Debt/EBITDA | <2.5x | 2.5x - 3.5x | ±0.3x |
| FCF/Total Debt | >6% | 2% - 6% | ±2% |
| EBITDA Stability | ±5% volatility | ±10% volatility | Risk factor |

**Critical Dynamics:**
- BBB- median company: leverage near 3.0x, coverage near 4.0x, improving trajectory
- BB+ median company: leverage near 4.0x, coverage near 2.5x, stable to improving
- "Fallen angels" (BBB- downgraded to BB+): typically trigger on unexpected leverage spike or EBITDA miss
- "Rising stars" (BB+ upgraded to BBB-): require 2+ years of sustained metrics improvement and peer positioning shift

**Cost of Capital Impact:**
- IG/HY boundary: 150-250 bps yield spread differential
- Refinancing risk materially increases below BBB-
- Covenant package tightens at HY threshold

## 6. Industry Adjustments

### Leverage Tolerance by Sector

| Industry | Typical Leverage Range | Key Adjustments | Rationale |
|----------|------------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Technology (Software)** | 1.5x - 2.5x | Raise by 0.5x for SaaS model; apply 10% EBITDA haircut for cyclicality | Recurring revenue; customer concentration risk |
| **Healthcare (Pharma/Device)** | 2.0x - 3.0x | Raise by 0.3x for patent moat; haircut 5% for R&D risk | Stable cash flows; IP/regulatory risk |
| **Utilities (Regulated)** | 3.5x - 5.0x | Raise by 1.0x-1.5x vs industrial; lower coverage requirements | Predictable cash; regulatory framework |
| **Energy (E&P)** | 2.0x - 3.5x | Apply volatile EBITDA (±15%); scenario stress to $40 WTI | Commodity exposure; stranded asset risk |
| **Retail** | 2.0x - 3.0x | Lower by 0.5x post-2020; apply 20% rent adjustment | Margin pressure; real estate linkage |
| **Telecom** | 3.5x - 4.5x | Raise by 0.5x for monopolistic position; haircut 10% for tech disruption | Stable cash; capex intensity |
| **Automotive** | 1.5x - 2.5x | Apply EV transition haircut (15%); cyclical EBITDA stress | Cyclical; transformation risk |
| **Chemicals** | 2.5x - 3.5x | Haircut 15-20% for commodity cycle; apply stress scenario | Cyclical; commodity-driven |
| **Homebuilders** | 1.5x - 2.5x | Haircut 20% for cyclicality; include land inventory volatility | Highly cyclical; interest rate sensitive |

**General Rules:**
- Defensive sectors (utilities, telecom): accept leverage +0.5x higher for rating
- Cyclical sectors (energy, retail, auto): require leverage -0.5x lower for equivalent rating
- Technology/SaaS: downgrade cyclicality haircut to 5%; upgrade capital efficiency credit
- Private company operating in cyclical sector: additional 0.3x-0.5x tightening

## 7. Private Company Adjustments

### Rating Modifier Framework

| Adjustment Factor | Moody's Term | S&P Term | Impact | Rationale |
|-------------------|--------------|----------|--------|-----------|
| **Governance Discount** | Limited covenant protections | Weak governance | -1 to 2 notch | Fewer lender safeguards; family/founder-led risk |
| **Information Discount** | Audit/reporting gaps | Limited transparency | -1 to 2 notch | Financial statement quality; less frequent disclosure |
| **Liquidity/Size Discount** | Smaller equity base | Scale/complexity | -1 notch | Less capital flexibility; lower M&A exit optionality |
| **Leverage Tightening** | -0.3x to -0.5x | -0.3x to -0.5x | Threshold shift | Higher volatility; concentrated customer/geography |
| **Coverage Tightening** | -0.3x to -0.5x | -0.3x to -0.5x | Threshold shift | Less operating margin buffer; margin volatility |

### Private vs Public Equivalent

**Example: Industrial Company**
- Public equivalent at 3.0x leverage, 5.0x coverage → BBB rating
- Private equivalent at 2.5x leverage, 4.7x coverage → BBB- rating (information + governance discount)
- Private equivalent at 2.0x leverage, 5.5x coverage → BBB rating (de facto match)

**Implications:**
- Professional management team (-0.5 notch) vs founder-led (+1 notch)
- Third-party audited financials (-0.5 notch); owner financials (+1 notch)
- Diversified ownership structure (-0.5 notch); concentrated family ownership (+1 notch)
- Multi-bank relationship (-0.5 notch); single relationship lender (+0.5 notch)

### Migration Path: Private to Public

- IPO typically results in 1-2 notch upgrade potential (improved transparency, liquidity, governance)
- Rating agencies flag 12-month stabilization period post-IPO before re-evaluation
- Debt-to-equity ratio and capital allocation decisions immediate focus
- Public float quality and analyst coverage influence upgrade trajectory
